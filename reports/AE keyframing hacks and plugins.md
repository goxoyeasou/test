# Ship the plug-ins Adobe never built

After Effects users still reach for a hidden shortcut, a pasted expression, a free script or a paid panel for eight keyframing jobs, and the evidence says a new motion app should ship all eight as built-in defaults: segment-level easing with a preset library and overshoot/elastic/bounce types (the job Flow, Easify, Kease, Ease and Wizz and Keyframe Wingman sell), ease copy/paste independent of values (EaseCopy, which users asked Adobe to bundle and were told "Not going to happen"), stagger/offset/sequence with ordering, eased distribution, randomisation and typed values (Rift, Dojo Shifter, Keystone, Staircase, Stratify, Lazy 2, Mt. Mograph Stagger), a value graph with handles on every channel including merged Position, a non-destructive settle/follow/wiggle layer that replaces the Ebberts inertial-bounce expression and its many wrappers, selection-scope-aware bulk editing, whole-frame snapping, and a dope-sheet timing view. Between October 2022 and September 2026 Adobe closed only the mechanics: keyframe navigation scoped to selected layers (23.0), multi-layer copy/cut/paste and Paste Reversed (24.4), the Quick Offset stagger drag (25.4, cross-layer and linear only, with in-layer stagger acknowledged by Adobe as an open request) and Proportional Scrubbing (26.2 beta); nothing touched the Graph Editor, easing presets, ease transfer or overshoot, which is why a mid-2025 Adobe Ideas post calls the Graph Editor "untouched for over a decade". Every popular tool converges on the same small control set (two influence sliders plus a cubic-bezier field; five stagger parameters; amplitude, frequency and decay plus a delay), so the native versions are cheap to build, and nearly all of them are pure functions of time that need no baking: Bézier eases, time offsets, loops, velocity-launched overshoots, closed-form springs and seeded noise each evaluate at any frame in isolation. Only true simulations (Newton, Duik Kleaner's overlap and soft-body modes, a spring chasing a moving keyframed target, AutoSway) and recorded or AI-generated data must be baked to keys, and Lottie export must bake everything that is not a cubic Bézier. Ranked by independent evidence of demand, easing presets, stagger/offset and ease paste lead; multi-layer paste and scoped navigation are now After Effects baseline and should be treated as parity rather than differentiation. Prices quoted below are last-seen values from search excerpts because the vendor pages were unreachable during research, and Reddit and X sentiment could not be sampled, so the evidence counts are floors.

## Adobe fixed timeline mechanics from 2022 to 2026 and left easing untouched

Adobe's keyframing roadmap over the window is consistent: it shipped things users do with the mouse on the timeline and nothing that touches how a curve is shaped. The run started with 23.0 in October 2022, which "adds commands for navigating between keyframes on only selected layers or properties", mapped to **Shift+J and Shift+K**, in direct response to a 2022 Ideas post complaining that plain J/K were useless on busy timelines ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/feature-focus-new-shift-j-and-shift-k-keyboard-shortcuts-in-after-effects-314162); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/add-option-for-j-k-hotkeys-to-work-with-only-selected-layer-s/m-p/13229473)). The Properties panel left beta for text and shape layers in 23.4 (May 2023), exposing transforms "without twirling" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/whats-new/2023-4.html)). Version 24.4 (May 2024) let users "copy keyframe selections that span multiple layers and paste them anywhere in the timeline without creating a new layer", added **Paste Reversed Keyframes on Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+V** "including across multiple layers", and made I and O jump to the earliest in-point and latest out-point across a multi-layer selection ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/using/whats-new/2024-4.html)); Adobe engineering closed a sixteen-year-old request with that release, since the same ask appears in a 2008 wishlist thread ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/copy-paste-keyframes-of-multiple-layers-at-the-same-time/idc-p/14617155); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/after-effects-wishlist-67159/index12.html)).

The headline change for timing was **Quick Offset in 25.4 (August 2025)**: hold Ctrl+Alt (Windows) or Cmd+Opt (macOS) and drag layers or keyframes, and "The first layer you selected stays in place, while the last layer moves the full distance you drag. All layers in between are evenly spaced in time", with an on-screen overlay showing "Total Offset" and "Per Layer" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/whats-new/2025-4.html)). Its limits surfaced within weeks. Users in the Feature Focus thread asked to snap middle layers to the playhead (only the first and last snap with Shift) and for a dialog to type the stagger after invoking the gesture ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-beta-discussions/feature-focus-quick-offset-stagger-your-layers-just-by-dragging-them/td-p/15377178)), and in September 2025 an Adobe employee confirmed that Quick Offset "only works across multiple layers and it considered each layer a unit... all keyframes on the same layer move together", adding that "staggering with multiple keyframes in a single layer is a great feature request and we'll be looking into it!" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-bugs/stagger-isn-t-behave-like-expected/idc-p/15484699)). The 2026 releases continued the pattern: 26.0 (January) brought "new property methods" for expressions and scripting that locate the keyframe before or after a time plus lossless compressed disk caching ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/01/22/whats-new-in-adobe-after-effects-26-0/)); 26.2 introduced **Proportional Scrubbing** in beta, which adjusts "multiple selected properties across multiple layers, distributing the value change proportionally based on the selection order" but, per Adobe, not in the Properties panel ("At the moment, no") ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-532/new-in-beta-proportional-scrubbing-for-values-in-the-timeline-1550451); [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/in_hi/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-keyframes/proportional-scrubbing-in-timeline.html)); 26.3 (June) fixed the Properties panel so that changing a value "only affects the current time, no longer sets all selected keyframes to the same value" and made "Remove All Keyframes on many layers" significantly faster ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/troubleshooting/fixed-and-known-issues/fixed-issues.html)); 26.5 (September) added percentage-based guides and colour labels for effects, not keyframes ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-527/after-effects-26-5-is-here-1641108)). The AI Assistant public beta shown at IBC 2026 spends its effort on "project organization and expressions", generating or repairing expression rigs, and describes no keyframe capability ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-532/new-in-after-effects-beta-after-effects-ai-assistant-1635658)).

What Adobe did not touch is the louder half of the record. The June 2025 Ideas post "After Effects: A Legacy Tool in a Modern World" lists "Graph Editor – still outdated, clunky, and untouched for over a decade. No real-time preview, no modular behavior, no intelligent UI" and "No modern easing tools – no built-in bounce, elastic, inertia, or overshoot. You still need to rely on Expression tricks or 3rd-party scripts for basic animation finesse" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/after-effects-a-legacy-tool-in-a-modern-world/idi-p/15373747)). A December 2025 post asks for a shortcut to select all keys visible in the Graph Editor, to which an Adobe employee replied only that it "could be a great addition" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/select-all-keyframes-in-the-graph-editor/idi-p/15613831)); a June 2026 request wants an animation dope sheet "for blocking, retiming, spacing, and coordinating many layers and properties at once... seeing which layers hit on the same frame", because today that "often requires manually expanding and collapsing properties, selecting many separate keyframes, using markers, shifting layer bars, or jumping between views" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/feature-requests-530/an-animation-dope-sheet-in-after-effects-1629664)); and an August 2026 bug report says the timeline "gets progressively slower as more keyframes are selected with marquee selection", starting around **100 selected keys** ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-528/timeline-ui-gets-progressively-slower-as-more-keyframes-are-selected-with-marquee-selection-1636432)). The complaints are stable across eras: a 2010 Creative COW poster estimated spending "half their After Effects time repeating the same easing task", manually building "what would basically be a Quad, Quint, or Expo curve" and wishing for "a keyframe right-click, or better a keyboard shortcut, offering a range of easy-ease options" ([Creative COW](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/advanced-easing-on-keyframesae/)), and 2008–2012 wishlists already asked for keyframe colour labels and grouping "like Nuke's backdrop node" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/after-effects-wishlist-67159/index6.html)). The strategic reading is simple: anything in the mechanics class is parity, and anything in the easing, graph-editor, overshoot and dope-sheet class is a visible differentiator that Adobe has chosen not to build in four years of releases.

## Easing: one segment popover replaces F9, the velocity dialog and five plug-in families

The native easing toolkit is a fixed recipe and a modal dialog. Easy Ease (F9, with Shift+F9 for in-only and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+F9 for out-only) sets "a speed of 0 with an influence of 33.33% on either side" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/speed.html); [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/keyboard-shortcuts-reference.html)), and there is no preference to change it: a user who searched the prefs file "found nothing relating to ease, keyframe, velocity, or any other parameter identified in the Keyframe Velocity options", so anyone who wants, say, 60% must edit every key or run a script ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/changing-the-ease-default-values/m-p/10906650)). The Keyframe Velocity dialog (Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+K, or Alt/Opt+double-click a key) takes incoming and outgoing speed and influence with a Continuous checkbox; influence accepts **0.1 to 100** in the scripting model ([Adobe Scripting Guide](https://after-effects-scripting-guide.readthedocs.io/other/keyframeease.html)). The folklore numbers experts type into it are informative because they show the default is wrong for most work: an Adobe Community expert suggests "an influence value of about 70% as a starting point for a nice acceleration curve" and "setting influence to zero on keyframes at the bottom of a bounce path" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/upside-down-curve-in-graph-editor/m-p/13940904)); Motion Array calls Easy Ease "a weak starting point" ([Motion Array](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/create-better-eases-in-after-effects/)). Hold keys toggle with Ctrl+Alt+H and can be set on a whole selection ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/can-t-get-composition-to-stop-and-hold-in-after-effects/m-p/9777140)). The `ease()` expression family exists for scripted remaps, but even that does not match the keyframe version exactly, since "the ease expression uses an exact influence of 1/3 while the keyframe one uses 33.333333%" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease-vs-eased-keyframes/td-p/7493370)).

The Graph Editor is where the real easing happens and where the structural complaints live. It has two mutually exclusive views, and newcomers routinely edit the wrong one ("The speed graph is the light gray line, while colored lines are the value graph") ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effects-graph-editor-problem/m-p/10552533)); there is no shortcut to switch between them as of a 2025 request ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/keyboard-shortcut-to-toggle-between-value-graph-and-speed-graph/idc-p/15234788)). Merged Position has no value-graph handles at all until the user runs Separate Dimensions, which in turn "removes the Bezier Handles from the spatial motion path in the Composition panel", so experts advise "the speed graph for Position is almost always a better idea" and Joey Korenman says he works in the value graph for everything else because the speed graph "tells him less about the motion" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/need-help-with-bezier-handles/m-p/13804367); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/only-affect-x-value-in-graph-editor-not-y-or-vice-versa/td-p/13635386); [School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/tutorials/intro-to-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects/)). Anchor Point cannot be separated at all, so the workaround is to drive it from a null's separated Position ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/where-are-my-bezier-curve-handles-in-the-graph-editor/m-p/11939629/highlight/true)). View state leaks: keyframes selected in the timeline "lost their selection after they clicked the Graph Editor button" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effects-selected-keyframes-selection-not-going-to-graph-editor/m-p/10764725/highlight/true)), the default "Show Animated Properties" piles unrelated curves into the view ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/only-show-selected-parameter-in-timeline-graph/m-p/9022523)), and the multi-key transform box has no numeric input, so users "are eyeballing the graph" when they want to type a scale percentage ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/idea-after-effects/graph-editor-improvements-|-scale-keyframes-precisely/idi-p/15283237)). Roving keyframes, the one genuinely clever native feature, let interior spatial keys "move in time as needed to produce smoother speed changes" while first and last stay locked ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after-effects-hidden-gems-weekly-roving-keyframes/)).

The plug-in market splits the job four ways, and each product's controls are a specification. **Flow** stores curves "in the CSS cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2) format" so a curve copied from cubic-bezier.com can be pasted and applied to selected keys, ships "25 pre-defined, commonly-used motion curves based on Robert Penner's Easing Functions", and lets users "build your own library and share it with the world, or import custom presets from other animators" ([Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=22477)); School of Motion's review calls "the preset library ... the killer feature of Flow" ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/training/flow-for-after-effects-review)), Spotlight FX ranked it second of twenty plug-ins in March 2026 as "a replacement for the default graph editor", and one community thread cites a ~$30 price ([Spotlight FX](https://spotlightfx.com/blog/best-after-effects-plugins); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease/m-p/15119393)). Its complaints are the costs of living in a panel: "the top-right curve handle disappears outside the plugin panel", the panel is not resizable, an "Apply As Expressions" toggle makes users think Flow "stopped creating easy ease keyframes" (staff: "this isn't a problem, it's a setting you enabled"), M1 users reported After Effects stalling after every click, the last release is 1.5.1 from July 2023, and a 2026 reviewer notes "there have been no major updates in years" ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1340/flow/p3); [aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1340/flow/p4); [Spotlight FX](https://spotlightfx.com/blog/best-after-effects-plugins)). Flow also "only works on the Bezier curve between two key frames at a time", so overshoots and bounces that need multiple eases are out of scope ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/flow-after-effects)). **EaseCopy** (name-your-own-price) exists because AE cannot copy an ease without its value: it lets you "Copy/paste your eases without overwriting your values and copy/paste your values without overwriting your eases", requires "the same number of keys, or a multiple of that number", "scales your eases appropriately and even flips them for velocities traveling in opposite directions", and has a Pass-through option for strips of three or more keys; a KBar button maps click, Alt-click and Shift-click to copy, paste eases and paste values ([Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=21775); [aescripts](https://aescripts.com/learn/easecopy-plus-kbar)). A February 2023 Ideas post titled "Ship EaseCopy plugin standard with AE" (about 2.6K views) says the poster "couldn't imagine a user who wouldn't keep it docked", and a community legend answered "Not going to happen. There's a ton of legal red tape" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/ship-easecopy-plugin-standard-with-ae/idi-p/13563168)). EaseCopy appears in every 2025–2026 free-script roundup found ([Quickbar](https://quickbar.pro/blog/top-50-free-after-effects-scripts/); [Made by Loop](https://madebyloop.co.uk/blog/free-after-effects-scripts/); [Goodboy](https://www.goodboy.ninja/best-after-effects-plugins)).

The slider appliers all converge on one widget. Motion Design School's free **Easy Keyframes** has "two primary sliders for Ease In and Ease Out influence, plus a central slider to control them symmetrically", applied in real time, with Shift snapping in steps of 10, Ctrl/Cmd for fine adjustment, Alt for symmetric movement and a numeric field ([Motion Design School](https://tools.motiondesign.school/tools/easy-keyframes)); Mt. Mograph's Ease Sliders do the same with a centre handle that "can be clicked, held, and dragged to change both values together, mirrored to either side", and Motion Studio adds Influence, Speed and Library panels and a December 2023 "Easing Screen" with import/export ([Mt. Mograph](https://motion.mtmograph.com/m-screens/home/ease-sliders); [Mt. Mograph](https://motion-studio.mtmograph.com/easing/easing-panels); [Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/pages/changelog)); **Dojo Ease** ($9.99, 3.9 stars from 8 ratings) adjusts speed and influence of many keys at once and pastes eases between properties ([Creative Dojo](https://creativedojo.gumroad.com/l/dojo-ease)). The curve editors with libraries are **Easify 2** ($34.99 standard, $59.99 Pro, with Bézier handles, unlimited presets, mirrored curves, rove across time, copy/paste easing and, in Pro, "customizable Back, Elastic, and Bounce curves") and **Kease**, which "goes beyond standard two-handle curves" for anticipation, overshoot, bounce and elastic with "mirrored handles" and reusable presets ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/easify-pro); [aescripts](https://aescripts.com/kease/)). **Ease and Wizz** applies Penner curves as expressions so keys stay editable and "the easing will be updated immediately" when moved, but multi-easing "only applies the combined easing to the first two and last two keyframes", its developer concedes "nothing beats the graph editor for custom motion", and users wanted amplitude and frequency sliders instead of editing code ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/ease-and-wizz); [aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/14/ease-and-wizz)). Mister Horse's redesigned Keyframe Wingman in Animation Composer 4 (July 2025) promises "one-click easing control" that "no longer requires opening the graph editor" ([Mister Horse](https://misterhorse.com/what-s-new/animation-composer-4-is-here)), and Motion Tools Pro ($40) bundles Ease Keyframes and an Easings Manager ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/motion-tools-pro/)). The pricing spread, from free to $59.99 perpetual to Mt. Mograph's **$13 per seat per month** (or $11 annual) ([Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/motion/pricing)), is what users pay to avoid F9-then-drag.

The native feature follows directly. Model ease as a property of the key pair, not as two half-tangents on each key, and open a popover when the user clicks the bar between two keys or presses a key with keys selected: in and out influence sliders with a mirrored centre drag and the Shift/Ctrl/Alt step conventions above, a `cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2)` text field that accepts pasted CSS and copies back out, a preset grid with both CSS and Penner names and fuzzy search, a "set as default" action, and live preview on the canvas while hovering a preset (the Flow feature users requested and never got) ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1340/flow/p4)). This is the shape Cavalry's right-click "magic easing" takes and the thing AE users name when comparing the two ([Tuts+](https://photography.tutsplus.com/is-cavalry-the-end-of-after-effects--ytc-101c)), and Rive already shows the numeric form "below the preview graph" ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing)). Default new keys to an asymmetric curve such as Material's standard `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` rather than AE's symmetric 1/3 ([material-web](https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)). Make the library entry either four numbers or a multi-segment path in the unit square so that overshoot, anticipation and bounce presets (Kease's and Easify Pro's selling point) live in the same popover, and make "apply" write handles onto every selected key pair. Ship Paste Ease with EaseCopy's exact rules (same count or multiple, scale to the target's value delta, flip for reversed direction, pass-through option) as plain paste with a modifier, and give the Easings Manager semantics a home: plain paste applies easing and leaves values alone, one modifier pastes values only, another pastes both ([Motion Design School](https://tools.motiondesign.school/tools/easings-manager)). In the graph, draw a value graph with real Bézier handles on every channel including merged X/Y Position and Anchor Point, show speed as a read-only overlay rather than a second mode, normalise curves to a shared vertical range so opacity and position can be edited together, keep selection across the switch from bars to curves, provide Fit Selection and Fit All as buttons with shortcuts, give the transform box numeric scale fields, keep a "rove" toggle for spatial keys, and add a select-all scoped to the graph. None of this needs baking: every cubic Bézier and every multi-segment unit-square path is an exact function of time, which is also why the same objects export directly to Lottie and CSS.

## Timing: every stagger tool exposes the same five parameters, so ship them as one live popover

Native After Effects has one keyframe assistant that sequences whole layers, two modifier drags, nudge keys and snapping, and nothing that offsets keyframes inside a layer. Sequence Layers has a Duration field that has no effect unless Overlap is checked, treats a negative Duration as a gap, offers three transitions (Off, Dissolve Front Layer, Cross Dissolve), and sequences in the order the layers were clicked, "Your sequence is going to start at the beginning of the first layer you select", so users must Ctrl-click in order or Shift-click bottom-up to get a direction ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after-effects-hidden-gems-sequence-layers/); [Lynda](https://www.lynda.com/After-Effects-tutorials/Understanding-importance-layer-order/79655/80668-4.html?src=c.r.su); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/sequence-layers-command-troubles/m-p/11824481)). Its Overlap is literal bar overlap rather than an offset, so a 10-frame overlap on 30-frame layers yields a 20-frame stagger, and sequenced layers run past the comp end because the command "uses the full layer duration" unless the layers are trimmed with Alt+] first ([Lynda](https://www.lynda.com/After-Effects-tutorials/Move-animated-images-around-frame/625926/678825-4.html); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/keyframe-assistant-sequence-layers-n%C3%A3o-est%C3%A1-fazendo-a-sequ%C3%AAncia-de-todas-as-camadas-selecionadas/m-p/14592761)); Frame.io still published a whole article on this recipe in December 2023 ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2023/12/13/insider-tips-how-to-create-a-staggered-layer-sequence-in-after-effects)). Alt/Opt-drag of the edge-most key scales a group proportionally, but grabbing a non-edge key moves the group instead, and the result "almost always leads to subframe positioning of keyframes", with a standing request for a quantise modifier ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-to-make-keyframes-proportionally-to-the-composition-length-in-after-effects/m-p/14384044/highlight/true); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com:443/t5/after-effects-ideas/option-to-quantize-keyframes-when-time-stretching-with-alt-key/idc-p/14067207)); the same modifier is why Option-drag cannot duplicate keys, because it "is currently occupied by the time-stretch keyframes behavior" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/option-drag-to-duplicate-keyframes-and-layers/m-p/13231142)). Alt+arrow nudges keys one frame and Alt+Shift+arrow ten ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/get-started/keyboard-shortcuts/keyboard-shortcuts-reference.html)); Shift while dragging snaps to any visible key or marker ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-to-make-the-current-time-indicator-jump-between-keyframes/td-p/14577510)). Randomising timing has no native command at all; a user asking to "jitter keyframe timing" noted wiggle handles values but "there was no equivalent for time" and was sent to Rift ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/jitter-keyframe-timing/m-p/10532799)). For retiming, Time Stretch applies one factor to a whole layer and all its keys, Time-Reverse Keyframes flips each property within its own span and leaves markers alone, and Time Remap lets speed vary between keys ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/time-stretching-and-time-remapping/time-stretching-time-remapping.html)); the expert trick for easing dozens of staggered keys at once is Jake Bartlett's "leave all the keyframes as linear, precompose, and then time remap" ([Reddit via redlib](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/MotionDesign/comments/1hktal5/how_do_i_make_this_or_similar)).

Expressions fill the in-layer gap with one primitive. The canonical delay is `thisComp.layer("Leader").position.valueAtTime(time - d)`, scaled by `index` so a stack staggers itself; the Motion Array version hard-codes the leader's index (`m = index - 4`) and breaks when a layer is inserted above, the robust form reads the leader's `.index`, and a slider on a null makes the delay editable ([Motion Array](https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/how-to-offset-layers-in-after-effects/); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/expression-to-offset-layers-in-position-as-well-as-time/m-p/13908763)). Daisy chains through `thisComp.layer(index-1)` compound the lag but hit a performance cliff: "once you get past about four or so of these references, you should give serious thought to using absolute indexing" ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/dmoe6_time/)). `loopOut("cycle" | "pingpong" | "offset" | "continue")` handles repetition, with "offset" accumulating the start-to-end difference each cycle and "continue" extrapolating the last velocity ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/loop-expression-after-effects)). The reason users write these is that AE has no non-destructive time-offset attribute on a layer or property; every one of them is `f(t − delay)`.

The scripts converge on a single data model. **Rift** (name-your-own-price, suggested $29.99) shifts in/out points, source start, markers and keyframes "by relative or absolute values", acts before, after or within range of the playhead, sequences "over a specified time using linear, ease in, ease out, ease both" with an expo field for strength, staggers "by a given offset", randomises "with a range", and accepts unit suffixes; a reviewer says it "can save hours" on stacks "numbering in the tens or twenties" ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/rift); [PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/four-time-saving-ae-scripts/)). Its limits are the two every tool shares: "Rift at its core shifts keyframes as a whole on a per layer basis", so users split shapes into layers to stagger within one, and randomising 200+ layers comes out "not-so random" and clumpy ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/286/rift/p2); [aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/286/rift/p3)). **Dojo Shifter v2** (pay what you want, **4.9 stars from 73 ratings**) staggers layers and keyframes across multiple properties with linear, quad, cubic, quint and expo easing on the stagger in frames or seconds ([Creative Dojo](https://creativedojo.gumroad.com/l/dojo-shifter)). **pt_ShiftLayers** (suggested $9.99) shifts by a fixed time or staggers by N frames starting at the marker or added to each layer's own time, again in selection order ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/pt_shiftlayers/)). **Staircase** staggers, sequences and randomises blocks of layers defined by size or label colour but cannot reorder playback and produces "clumpy" random offsets ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1725/staircase); [Creative COW](https://creativecow.net/?p=181654)). **Stratify** ($19.99) adds ordering by spatial pattern (right, left, up, down, inward, outward, radial) and a sort mode ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/stratify/)). **Keystone** bundles align, stretch and stagger with link/unlink so you can "stagger layers without affecting their keyframes", and its "user favorite feature" in version 3 is Constant Speed ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/keystone); [aescripts](https://aescripts.com/keystone/)); its forum records the complaint that best explains why these tools should be live rather than fire-and-forget: the stagger is not cumulative, so "If you've already applied a 5 frames offset... you need to enter a new value (e.g. 10 if you want to double it)" ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/3655/keystone-2)). **Mt. Mograph Stagger** (added March 2023, in-layer group stagger February 2025) is "a full-page workflow" with Type (layers or keyframes), Mode (in/out point), Direction, Alignment, Start index, Interval versus Overlap, a Graph page that distributes across a duration or the work area, and Randomize, while its Delay tool is an expression rig with a global Delay percentage and a Drag option that randomises with range and seed ([Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/products/motion/tools/stagger); [Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/news/february-2025-release); [Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/products/motion/tools/delay)). **Lazy 2** offsets layers and keyframes "along user-defined bezier curves" and can "pinch keyframes together along your curve" ([School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/free-after-effects-tools)). The duplicate-and-offset rigs show the same demand from another angle: **Layer Repeater** shifts each copy's animation by a Frame Offset but broke when AE 23.0 separated Position dimensions by default and reportedly stops at about 40 copies ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/212/layer-repeater/p3)); **Overlap** lets you "pick the layer that drives the motion and the layers you want it to control" so "only the driver's keyframes need editing", with Offset Control, Factor by Index, Invert and Phase Shift ([Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=35455)). Users wire all of this to **KBar 3** (MSRP $39.99) buttons with modifiers, which is how one Nudge button moves a selection 12 or 24 frames ([Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=83765); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/feature-requests-530/move-layer-and-selected-keyframes-on-a-second-layer-without-moving-the-second-layer-1214789)).

Read together, every one of these tools exposes the same five parameters: a selection (layers, keyframes, or keyframe runs inside a layer, optionally grouped), an ordering (selection, stack, reverse, label, spatial pattern, random with seed), a spacing (per-item interval, total span, distribute across a range, or overlap), an easing of the spacing (linear, in, out, both, with strength), and an anchor (relative to each item's own time or absolute to the playhead). GSAP's stagger object is the same list in code (`each`, `amount`, `from` as start/center/end/edges/random, `grid`, and an `ease` that "distributes the start times of the animations") ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/resources/getting-started/Staggers/)), and Cavalry's Duplicator plus Stagger behaviour is why School of Motion says animating "50 circles in sequence usually means keyframes, expressions, or a complex setup" in AE and three clicks in Cavalry ([School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/cavalry-houdini-of-2d-after-effects)). The native feature is therefore a Stagger popover reachable from the Quick Offset gesture: drag with the modifier to see Total and Per-item readouts as Adobe does, release to get the popover with the five parameters pre-filled, and keep the values live so that changing Per-item from 5 to 10 frames re-distributes from the original times instead of compounding. Treat keyframe runs inside one layer as items (the acknowledged open request), include a seeded random jitter (the "jitter keyframe timing" ask), and let Shift snap any item, not only the first and last, to the playhead. Pair it with two non-destructive properties: a **Time Offset** on every layer and property group, which replaces `valueAtTime(time − d)` and the slider-on-a-null rig with one editable number, and a **Follow** link (source property, delay in frames, per-index multiplier, optional spring) that replaces `thisComp.layer(index−1)` chains without the four-link performance cliff, because a memoised evaluator looks up the source curve at `t − d` rather than re-running a chain. For retiming, keep the edge-drag but snap to whole frames by default with sub-frame placement as an opt-in, show a numeric scale percentage in the same overlay, add Shift-ripple so that fixing one pause moves everything after it as Motion Canvas does ([Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io/docs/time-events)), offer Maya-style retime markers for non-uniform warps ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-A6061A36-81FF-4F57-A081-C967F749D546.htm)), and make Keystone's Constant Speed a toggle on spatial keys. Add a dope-sheet row per layer that summarises all keyed properties, with keyframe guides and a "same frame" highlight, since both are open requests ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/feature-requests-530/an-animation-dope-sheet-in-after-effects-1629664); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/feature-request-keyframe-guide/m-p/11892650)). Nothing in this section needs baking: a stagger is `f(t − delay(i))`, a loop is a modulo on `t`, a retime rewrites key times, and all three remain pure functions of time; the only caveat is aesthetic, since a controlled study found staggering "has a negligible, or even negative, impact on multiple object tracking performance", so offer it as a style choice rather than push it as clarity ([Chevalier et al.](https://hal.inrae.fr/CRISTAL-MINT/hal-01054408v1)). The one place to expect original work is Dragimation-style timing by dragging the object along its path, which in a 27-participant study was "significantly better than scrubbing and is the overall preferred technique" and writes ordinary keys ([Dragimation](https://almeida.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/karrer2012b.pdf)).

## Follow-through: Ebberts' ten lines are a closed-form settle, and only true simulations need baking

Mt. Mograph states the gap plainly: "After Effects does not have native Overshoot or Bounce tools", so the manual method is a keyframe past the target (scale to 150, settle at 100) and the procedural method is Dan Ebberts' expression ([Mt. Mograph](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/the-bounce-and-overshoot-animation-trick-every-motion-designer-should-know)). The canonical inertial bounce, as republished by Animoplex, sets `amp = 5.0; freq = 2.0; decay = 4.0`, finds the last keyframe before `time`, samples the arrival velocity a tenth of a frame before it (`velocityAtTime(key(n).time - thisComp.frameDuration/10)`), and returns `value + v*(amp/100)*Math.sin(freq*t*2*Math.PI)/Math.exp(decay*t)` for the first second after the key, with the comment "Use this on any property with two keyframes" ([Animoplex](https://gist.github.com/animoplex/aafd6a157282351c8dfeea385d969ef2)). Its one famous bug is numerical, not conceptual: Ebberts explained that on long timelines the decay term grows until "JavaScript returns Infinity", and his fix was to limit the calculation to a few seconds after the key ([Creative COW](https://creativecow.net/?p=1859347)). The gravity variant (a dropped ball) walks bounce segments with elasticity, gravity and a maximum count, each segment a projectile parabola ([Elast-o-matic](https://github.com/ivg-design/AE/blob/main/packages/ae-scripts/src/keyframes/Elast-o-matic.jsx)); School of Motion's explainer notes "it only takes two keyframes to create a bounce" ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/after-effects-bounce-expression)). The wrappers all expose the same three sliders. Mt. Mograph's **Excite** applies Overshoot, Bounce and Friction presets and creates per-property effect controls for magnitude, frequency and decay ([Mt. Mograph](https://motion-studio.mtmograph.com/ae-extension/tools/excite)); **After Ease** ($25) previews "the bounce or elastic type of animation before the expression is created", with controls for number of bounces, amplitude and vertical and horizontal decay, and can apply as expression or bake to keys ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/after-ease); [Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=21407)); EZ Inertia, Ukramedia's Bounce & Drop preset and 2dnMotion's Bounce Motion are free wrappers of the same formula ([Motion Array](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/how-to-create-a-realistic-bouncing-effect-in-after-effects/); [Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=45802)). Mamoworld's **iExpressions** goes further with UIKit, Android and React Spring interpolators that honour only keyframe values ("the easing you set in Ae is ignored"), a Throw that "after the last keyframe the simulation starts automatically" with ground height, elasticity and gravity, and an Elastic Connection that "Makes one property follow another with a delayed, elastic motion" ([iExpressions docs mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Amal-David/docingest/main/server/storage/docs/docs.mamoworld.com/documentation_2026-09-18T13:25:45.115Z.md)). Duik's free **Kleaner** is the most complete spec: presets Alive, Inanimate, True stop, Spring, Bounce and Limits; parameters for anticipation amplitude and duration, overlap percentage, follow-through "Flexibility" and "Duration", a Bounce checkbox, soft limits and randomness; a Gaussian curve that "makes acceleration faster than deceleration, unlike Bézier curves"; follow-through contacts "placed on exact composition frames"; and the design note that "It uses analytical methods, not AI, machine learning, or physical simulation" ([Duik](https://github.com/RxLaboratory/Duik/blob/master/src-docs/docs/guide/automation/kleaner.md)). Educators teach the same three numbers, delay frames, overshoot amount and settle time ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/animation-tricks-follow-through-after-effects)), and Apple Motion ships the result as an Overshoot behaviour that makes "a parameter ramp toward an end value, pass it, and settle back" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/motion/motn0c60c821/mac)).

The seekability question has a crisp answer because After Effects gives expressions no place to store state: "Expressions in After Effects are all evaluated (computed) when rendering each frame of the composition", and anything that depends on its own earlier output must "re-compute everything for each frame", a cost of (f² + f)/2 for f frames ([Duik](https://github.com/RxLaboratory/Duik/blob/master/src-docs/docs/advanced/performance.md)). The inertial bounce, the gravity bounce, `valueAtTime(t − d)` delay chains, `wiggle`, `temporalWiggle`, `smooth()` and `loopOut` are therefore all pure functions of `time`, the keyframe list and static sliders; `velocityAtTime` and `valueAtTime` are lookups into the keyframe curve, not into prior outputs ([Adobe expression reference](https://github.com/docsforadobe/after-effects-expression-reference/blob/master/docs/objects/property.md)). What makes users bake them is cost, not state: a chain of depth N evaluates N upstream expressions per frame, `smooth()` multiplies that by its sample count, and motion blur re-evaluates every expression per blur sample ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/sourcerectattime-expression-causing-major-performance-issues-with-motionblur/m-p/12585183)). The things that genuinely need history are the ones the tools themselves flag: Kleaner's overlap and soft-body "simulations... have to compute previous movements" and can be disabled in its Performance section ([Duik](https://github.com/RxLaboratory/Duik/blob/master/src-docs/docs/advanced/performance.md)); **Newton 4** ($249.99 MSRP, keyframe output only) says it outright, "Newton only creates keyframes!" ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/newton/?aff=50); [Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/news/new-motion-boutique-newton4-is-now-available/)); AutoSway ships Bake and Unbake because the sway rig is too expensive live ([Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=31131)); and Cavalry's experimental Spring behaviour, which adds "dynamic secondary motion to a Shape's position keyframes" by chasing a moving target, is a simulation over the whole input history ([Cavalry](https://cavalry.studio/docs/nodes/behaviours/spring/)). Closed-form springs sit on the pure side: Apple's SwiftUI Spring is evaluated directly "at a given time", and Motion's generator has explicit closed forms for under-, critically and over-damped regimes with an analytic velocity, so a spring segment with stored initial conditions is as seekable as a Bézier ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spring); [Motion](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/packages/motion-dom/src/animation/generators/spring.ts)). Seeded noise is pure only if it hashes (t, seed): AE's wiggle "randomizes itself, depending on the 'index' of the layer", which is why copies and reorders change the motion, and Duik's Wiggle adds a seed that stays stable "when layers are reordered or copied" ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/dmoe9_wiggle/); [Duik](https://github.com/RxLaboratory/Duik/blob/master/src-docs/docs/guide/automation/wiggle.md)); the same rule is Remotion's, whose renderer may compute "frame 30... before frame 10" and therefore bans `Math.random()` in favour of a seeded `random()` ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering)).

The native feature is a stack of non-destructive modifiers on a property, each a pure function of time, drawn in the graph as a post-modifier curve the way AE's "Show Post-Expression Graph" works today. A **Settle** modifier attaches to a key or a property with three types: Overshoot (amount as a percentage of the arrival velocity, frequency, settle time in frames, launched from the analytic arrival velocity exactly as the expression does), Bounce (elasticity, gravity, maximum bounces, contacts snapped to whole frames as Kleaner does) and Spring (bounce and duration in Apple's parametrisation, with duration defined by a settle threshold so the next key begins at a known value). Compute `exp(−decay·t)` and clamp the active window so the Infinity bug cannot recur. A **Follow** modifier takes a source property, a delay in frames, a per-index multiplier for stacks and an optional spring, replacing the Overlap script and the Delay rig. A **Wiggle** modifier exposes frequency, per-axis amplitude, octaves, a seed derived from layer and property identity, and a loop length so it closes seamlessly, the four things iExpressions and Duik each had to add on top of `wiggle()` ([iExpressions docs mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Amal-David/docingest/main/server/storage/docs/docs.mamoworld.com/documentation_2026-09-18T13:25:45.115Z.md)). Presets should carry the user-facing names the tools converged on (Overshoot, Bounce, Friction; Alive, Inanimate, Spring) with the three numbers animators actually think in, and the panel should preview the curve before applying, the After Ease feature users praise. Two things must be baked. Anything that chases a moving target or collides (Newton-class physics, Kleaner-style soft body, a spring whose target is itself keyframed) is admitted only as a layer whose keys are baked from the composition start at the composition frame rate, with the parameters retained so the bake can be re-derived; and every modifier, pure or not, is baked for Lottie export, because Lottie's support table marks expressions unsupported on Android, iOS and Windows and lottie-web runs only a subset in which "linear, velocityAtTime and wiggle are not supported" ([airbnb/lottie](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie/blob/master/supported-features.md); [airbnb/lottie](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie/blob/master/web.md)); even baked wiggle "would never work exactly the same since I don't have AE's random seed", which a seeded native noise avoids ([Webflow forum](https://forum.webflow.com/t/lottie-ae-wiggle-effect-bodymovin-oh-my/100067)). LottieFiles' own Creator now sells exactly this split, a Spring Curve ease plus a Physics Simulator that generates "keyframes for fall, bounce and collision" ([LottieFiles](https://lottiefiles.com/de/lottie-creator)). Copy the bake semantics AE users already know and the third parties improved: Convert Expression to Keyframes "creating a keyframe at every frame, and then turns off the expression" while "The original expression is preserved" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/expression-basics.html)), which is why Smart Baker skips keys "that add nothing", Easy Bake ($14.99) "prunes away any redundant keys", AEJuice adds a one-click Unbake, and Duik offers a "smart" mode "using just a few Bézier keyframes" ([Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/2011/05/smart-baker-for-after-effects/); [aescripts](https://aescripts.com/easy-bake/); [AEJuice](https://aejuice.com/product/bake-expressions); [Duik](https://github.com/RxLaboratory/Duik/blob/master/src-docs/docs/advanced/performance.md)). A native bake should curve-fit with a tolerance, snap to frames, keep the modifier parameters so "unbake" is just re-enabling, and be the only thing that runs at export.

## Bulk editing and navigation: selection scope, offset entry and frame snapping are the open gaps

The native bulk-editing model is "select, then apply one operation", and its sharp edges are well documented. Clicking a property name selects all its keys, Ctrl/Cmd+Alt+A selects "all visible keyframes and properties", and right-click offers Select Equal, Select Previous and Select Following Keyframes ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/keyboard-shortcuts-reference.html); [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/ae_ar/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-keyframes/setting-selecting-deleting-keyframes.html)), but there is no "select all keyframes in the comp", and "only those keyframes your eyeballs can view will be affected by the CTRL+ALT+A shortcut", which silently excludes collapsed properties from bulk moves ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects/select-all-keyframes-in-a-composition/m-p/9686516)). Editing a value with many keys selected splits invisibly between relative and absolute: dragging the hot text "shifts every selected keyframe by that same amount" while typing a number "makes every selected keyframe take the new value exactly", and all keys "must belong to the same layer property" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/editing-moving-copying-keyframes.html)); Ctrl scrubs by decimals, Shift by tens, nothing by integers ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/can-i-get-the-quot-hot-text-quot-to-work-the-way-it-does-in-premiere/m-p/9616381)). Paste places "the earliest keyframe... at the current time" and matches properties by name or by data type, one-to-one when the target property differs ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/editing-moving-copying-keyframes.html)); Copy With Property Links pastes expressions that keep copies following a master ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/after-effects/desktop/work-with-expressions/edit-expressions/edit-expressions.html)). Time-Reverse Keyframes ignores sparse selections, reversing "the whole keyframe sequence, ignoring my carefully selected subset", and Adobe closed the bug as a feature request ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-bugs/time-reverse-keyframes-should-only-affect-selected-keyframes/idc-p/15103795)). After a frame-rate change a user had to move "around 50 keyframes manually" because keys sat on half frames and the only script that fixes it "does not preserve keyframe easing" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/snapping-all-keysframes-to-a-full-frame-54655)). The Smoother reduces keys by a Tolerance ("New keyframe values vary no more than the specified value from the original curve") but is meant for motion-sketched data and "may result in unexpected changes" on hand-set keys ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/after-effects/using/assorted-animation-tools.html)).

The script layer fills six holes, and its mechanics are reusable. Zack Lovatt's free **zl_Scriptlets** supply selection by rule: Select Keys in Work Area, Select Keys at Current Time, Select Alternating Keyframes, Key Sets that save a selection so it "survives timing/value changes", Keep Every N Keys for pruning baked data, and Key Stumbler for irregular in-betweens, all designed to run from KBar with Shift/Ctrl arguments, and with a README warning that "as AE has evolved, some may stop working" ([GitHub](https://github.com/zlovatt/zl_Scriptlets); [Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=69284)). **KeyTweak** (name-your-own-price) lets you "modify a few keyframes by hand" and propagates the proportional change to every other key in the work area, with an in/out fade, and is the standard fix for dense tracker keys; its reviewer's caveat was that "it is not quite as robust as a spread/dope sheet would be" ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/learn/how-to-modify-lots-of-keyframes-at-once-with-keytweak-for-after-effects/); [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after_effects_script_of_the_week_keytweak/)). **Paste Multiple Keyframes** predates 24.4 and still adds looping ("to get a clean A-B-A-B pattern you should create 3 keyframes: A B A") ([aescripts forum](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/766/paste-multiple-keyframes-2)); **Key Cloner** (pay what you want) clones or reverses keys across layers and properties, preserving "the order and the relative timing" ([Conigs](https://conigs.gumroad.com/l/key-cloner); [Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=48615)); **Smart Key Reverse 2** reverses "Selected", at the current time or within the work area, fixing the native command's selection blindness ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/smart-key-reverse/)); Mt. Mograph's Clone "duplicates all selected keyframe(s) to CTI" with Standard, Reverse and Mirror modes ([Mt. Mograph](https://motion.mtmograph.com/m-screens/toolbox/tools/clone)). The scale of the long tail is itself a signal: aescripts' Keyframes tag lists **132 products** ([aescripts](https://aescripts.com/tag/product/list/tag/keyframes/)). Even scripting has blind spots, since hold keyframes could not be selected via `setSelectedAtKey` in a March 2025 beta ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/bug-reports-528/inability-to-select-hold-keyframes-via-scripting-1215934)).

Navigation and preview hacks round out the picture. The semicolon toggles between single-frame zoom and the whole comp, with Shift+; the reverse, and the two reports disagree on which is which ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/keyboard-shortcuts-timeline-zoom-to-fit-move-layer-to-playhead/m-p/10227670/highlight/true)); Fit Selection and Fit All live in the Graph Editor menu with no documented shortcut ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/animation-basics.html)). Caps Lock is the universal "stop rendering while I edit" switch, a trick that "has worked since Version Three", yet AE "forgets that it is pressed when switching Comps" and a 24.5 beta showed the Caps Lock banner with the key off ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/audio-scrubbing/m-p/9759212); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-bugs/make-caps-lock-persisitent/m-p/15008692)); the Preview panel lets users bind up to seven shortcuts to resolution, frame-skip and frame-rate presets ([tutkit](https://www.tutkit.com/en/text-tutorials/16621-optimal-ram-preview-for-after-effects)). Rive shows the failure mode a new editor must avoid on the undo side: undoing a key deletion "also undos your most recent scroll/zoom action", which users find disorienting ([Rive forum](https://www.rive.app/community/forums/bugs/fsLjspgTHYcR/undoing-keyframe-deletion-also-reverts-recent-timeline-panzoom-actions/ftolrYCh6C4A)).

The native list is short and each item has a named precedent. Selection must be independent of what is expanded on screen: one command each for all keys of a property, a layer and the composition, plus filters for equal value, before or after a key, in the work area, at the playhead, alternating, and by interpolation type, and saved selections as Key Sets. The inspector's value field needs an explicit Absolute versus Offset toggle so multi-key edits stop depending on whether the user typed or dragged, with Ctrl/Shift step scaling and Proportional Scrubbing available everywhere values appear, including the inspector Adobe excluded. Paste should land the first key at the playhead and fan out to every selected layer or property with the type-compatibility rule shown in the UI, with Clone to Playhead and Paste Reversed as one-key commands and a paste-as-link for instancing. Time-Reverse must honour sparse selections. A Snap All Keys to Frames command must preserve easing by rescaling handle times with the key. Reduce and Smooth should be one dialog with a tolerance slider, a keep-every-N option, a live preview and a scope selector (project, comp, layers, properties), and Bake must keep its parameters for unbake. Keyframe colour labels and groups answer a wish that has been open since 2008, and Blender's typed keys (Keyframe, Breakdown, Extreme, Jitter, Moving Hold) are the mature version of it ([Blender](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/animation/keyframes/introduction.html)). For navigation, scope J/K to the selection by default with "all layers" as the option, put Fit Selection, Fit All, frame view and whole-comp view on visible buttons, cycle value and speed overlays with one key, keep locked layers inspectable, and keep selection, pan and zoom out of the undo stack and stable across the bar and curve views. Replace Caps Lock with a visible, per-document "pause rendering" toggle, ship preview profiles bound to keys, allow edits during playback, and make sure selection of a thousand keys costs nothing, since AE's marquee slows past 100. All of these are transforms on key data and leave every value a pure function of time; none needs baking.

Two things in the table deserve emphasis because they shape the UX more than any single feature. First, the recurring plug-in complaints (Keystone's non-cumulative stagger, Flow's lost handle and mode toggle, Rift's per-layer limit, Ease and Wizz's code-only tuning, AutoSway's per-instance values) are all consequences of being a one-shot script bolted onto a host; a built-in version should be live and scrubbable, so that the stagger, the ease and the settle remain editable parameters on the timeline rather than results to re-run. Second, the only operations that must bake are the ones where output depends on earlier output, and the list is short; everything users currently bake for performance (delay chains, smooth, wiggle, inertial bounce) becomes cheap under a memoised evaluator and should stay live, with baking reserved for Lottie export and for the handful of true simulations, each bake carrying its parameters so it can be re-derived at another frame rate.

## Conclusion

The After Effects plug-in market is not a collection of niche conveniences; it is a twenty-year requirements document for a timeline, and it says the same thing in every era. Users do not want more power in the graph editor, they want the graph editor to be optional: an ease they can pick by name or paste as four numbers, a stagger they can drag and then type, a bounce that is a slider rather than ten lines of JavaScript, and bulk operations that act on what they mean rather than on what happens to be twirled open. Adobe's four years of "mechanics first" releases make the opportunity sharper, not smaller, because the baseline is now well defined (multi-layer paste, Paste Reversed, scoped navigation, Quick Offset) and the gap above it is exactly the easing, overshoot, stagger-in-layer and dope-sheet set that third parties still sell for $10 to $60 per tool or $13 per month.

For the team's deterministic core, the research changes the framing of the bake question. The Ebberts expression, the delay chain, the loop and the wiggle were always pure functions of time; After Effects users bake them because expressions are slow and Lottie players cannot run them, not because the math requires it. A memoised evaluator that keeps those behaviours live, exposes them as three-number modifiers, draws them as post-modifier curves and bakes only at export (or for the few true simulations) delivers the plug-in ecosystem's value with none of its breakage, and the play-equals-seek-equals-export invariant becomes the feature that makes "unbake" trivial rather than a constraint to defend. The remaining original work is in design, not physics: a live stagger that re-distributes from original times instead of compounding, a settle that reads arrival velocity from a segment-owned ease, a dope-sheet row that shows timing across properties without expanding them, and a curve-fitting bake good enough that a Lottie export looks like the live preview.
