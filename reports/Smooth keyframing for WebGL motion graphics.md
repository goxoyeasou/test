# Clamped Béziers and closed-form springs win

Build the animation core around one curve model and make everything else collapse into it: a 2-D cubic Bézier in (time, value) whose handle times are clamped inside the segment, with overshoot-free "auto-clamped" tangents as the default, evaluated by solving x(t) = time with a fixed-iteration solver. That is where After Effects, Blender, Maya, Cavalry, Rive and Lottie have independently landed, and it is exactly the shape a rational-time, pure-function evaluator wants. Springs, elastic eases, staggers, loops, noise, recorded motion and AI output should each be either a closed-form function of time (Apple, Motion, Flutter and Remotion all evaluate springs this way, so any frame can be computed independently) or data that bakes to ordinary keys; numeric spring integrators, state-machine blends and history-dependent "follow" behaviours violate the play = seek = export invariant and should stay out of the timeline unless baked. The UX evidence is just as consistent: AE's two largest complaint clusters (value-graph versus speed-graph confusion, and the "Separate Dimensions" trap that removes motion-path handles) and its three best-selling ease plug-ins all trace to modelling ease as half-tangents on keys rather than as a property of the segment, while web tools that removed keyframes (Jitter, Fable, Framer) are praised for approachability, hit a ceiling, and LottieFiles and Spline have since added graph editors back. Playback smoothness is mostly a presentation problem: derive time from the `requestAnimationFrame` timestamp minus an origin (never `frame++`), render in a worker with its own rAF on an `OffscreenCanvas`, keep scrubbing "latest wins", and accept that 24 or 30 fps content on a 60 Hz panel judders because of refresh cadence, not evaluation. Academic HCI contributes two cheap, validated additions, drag-the-object performance timing (Dragimation: 27 participants, significantly better than scrubbing and the preferred technique) and slow-in/slow-out as the empirically best pacing default, plus one warning: staggering does not help viewers track motion. Avoid uniform Catmull-Rom or Kochanek-Bartels tangents on unevenly spaced keys, gamma-sRGB colour interpolation, normalised rotation angles, sub-frame keys after retiming, GPU-side curve evaluation, and any AI feature that emits frames rather than parameters.

## One time-clamped cubic Bézier serves every scalar property

Every surveyed tool interpolates with a piecewise cubic between keys; they differ only in how they parametrise it. After Effects stores per-key speed plus influence, a speed-graph parametrisation that maps one-to-one onto a value-graph handle, and distinguishes temporal interpolation (Linear, Auto Bezier, Continuous Bezier, Bezier, Hold) from the spatial Bézier motion path ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/keyframe-interpolation.html)). Blender stores 2-D handles in (time, value) and, at evaluation, clamps each handle separately so "handles cannot exceed the adjacent key-frames time position", then solves the cubic in x with a closed-form `solve_cubic()` ([Blender fcurve.cc](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/fcurve.cc)). Maya stores Hermite tangents as an angle with an optional weight, where non-weighted tangents "are locked and you can only change their angle, not their length" ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-884E25DE-2632-42A5-89B3-74B9E94544F4.htm)). Lottie and CSS store normalised handles per segment: Lottie's easing `x` is bounded to [0, 1] while `y` has "no min/max", so AE value-graph overshoot survives export ([lottie-spec](https://github.com/lottie/lottie-spec/blob/main/schema/properties/easing-handle.json)), and CSS requires "Both x values must be in the range [0, 1] or the definition is invalid" with y unrestricted ([CSS Easing 2](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/blob/main/css-easing-2/Overview.bs)). Rive's "Cubic Value" mode stores handles as values and nothing clamps them, so a curve can "travel past 0 and back, creating real movement even when the start and end values are identical" ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing); [rive-runtime](https://github.com/rive-app/rive-runtime/blob/main/src/animation/cubic_value_interpolator.cpp)). These are the same mathematical object in different coordinates. The x-clamp is the load-bearing invariant: with x₁, x₂ ∈ [0, 1] the derivative of x(t) stays non-negative, x(t) is monotone and invertible, and every solver can assume a unique root; a handle allowed outside the segment produces a time-travelling S-bend. **Adopt the absolute (time, value) form with handle x clamped to the segment, and treat CSS-style normalised handles as a view of it**, because the absolute form is what a graph editor draws and the normalised form is what presets, copy-as-CSS and Lottie export need. AE's Easy Ease, "a speed of 0 with an influence of 33.33% on either side" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/speed.html)), is the normalised handle pair (1/3, 0), (2/3, 1), which evaluates to the smoothstep polynomial 3t² − 2t³, a useful parity check for an importer.

The x → t solve is a solved micro-problem with one trap. The algorithm Chrome and Firefox use, as published in `bezier-easing` 2.1.0, samples x(t) at **11 points into a Float32 table, runs up to 4 Newton iterations when the local slope is at least 0.001, and otherwise bisects to 1e-7 within 10 iterations**; Rive's runtime ships the identical constants ([bezier-easing v2.1.0](https://github.com/gre/bezier-easing/blob/v2.1.0/src/index.js); [rive-runtime solver](https://github.com/rive-app/rive-runtime/blob/main/src/animation/cubic_interpolator_solver.cpp)). The library's current master replaced this with a closed-form Cardano/trigonometric root, described as "exact and faster", but **v3.2.0 (October 2026) had to fix "wrong roots near double roots and overflow for tiny x"** ([bezier-easing releases](https://github.com/gre/bezier-easing/releases)). Double roots occur at exactly the "nice" handle positions designers pick, so if you choose the closed form, test it against the LUT-plus-Newton reference across a dense grid of handle positions and keep an iterative fallback. Both families are deterministic because iteration counts are fixed and nothing depends on the previous frame; what breaks determinism is using different solvers (or a sampled approximation with a variable sample count, as GSAP's `CustomEase` lookup table does with `precision`-dependent flattening ([GSAP CustomEase](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP/blob/master/src/CustomEase.js))) for preview and export. **Pick one solver and run the same code path for play, seek and export.** Cost is not a concern: a few hundred curves per frame are microseconds in JS, so WebAssembly is justified for vector path work (Rive, dotlottie's ~500 KB engine, Figma's 3× load-time gain) and not for interpolation ([dotlottie-web](https://github.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web/blob/HEAD/packages/web/README.md); [Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/webassembly-cut-figmas-load-time-by-3x/)).

The choice that most affects perceived smoothness is the automatic tangent rule, and every tool's safe default is a monotone one. Chord-averaging tangents (Catmull-Rom, cardinal, Kochanek-Bartels with C = 0, Blender's "Automatic", Cavalry's "Spline", Maya's "Spline") are C1 but overshoot wherever a key is a local extremum; Blender's manual says Automatic handles "can overshoot and oscillate" and recommends "use Auto Clamped by default", which "clamps them so the curve doesn't overshoot or reverse direction between keys" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/graph_editor/fcurves/properties.html)). Maya's default Auto tangent keeps interior keys from overshooting their neighbours and flattens the first and last keys, Plateau "ensures that the minimum and maximum values along a curve fall on keyframes", and Clamped linearises segments between nearly equal keys, the documented fix for foot slippage ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-43A4FE2C-4863-4EA6-B6AE-6D2B6757F6C7.htm)). Cavalry's Auto-Bézier "flattens them at peaks and troughs" while Spline "can overshoot keyed values" ([Cavalry](https://cavalry.studio/docs/user-interface/menus/window-menu/scene-window/graph-editor/)). AE documents the failure mode directly: "Auto Bezier can cause unwanted back-and-forth 'boomerang' motion between two keyframes with equal values", with Hold or Linear as the workaround ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/keyframe-interpolation.html)). The formal version is Steffen's monotone tangent, shipped in d3: slope = `(sign(s0) + sign(s1)) * min(|s0|, |s1|, 0.5·|p|)` where p is the time-weighted average of the neighbouring secants, zero when they change sign ([d3-shape](https://github.com/d3/d3-shape/blob/main/src/curve/monotone.js)). Blender's source does the same thing in Bézier space: for Auto Clamped keys it keeps the handle horizontal at extrema and caps handle y at the neighbour's value ([Blender curve.cc](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/curve.cc)). **Default new keys to a monotone auto tangent, compute it with the time-weighted three-point formula ẋᵢ = (Δᵢ·vᵢ₋₁ + Δᵢ₋₁·vᵢ)/(Δᵢ₋₁ + Δᵢ) rather than the uniform ½(pᵢ₊₁ − pᵢ₋₁), and store an explicit continuous/broken flag per key** so a user can break tangents for a corner and get them back with one click. The uniform formula implicitly assumes equal spacing and produces too-steep tangents on a short interval next to a long one; the splines documentation shows that Kochanek and Bartels' own 1984 non-uniform adjustment "only rescales the uniform tangents by inbetween counts and leaves their direction unchanged" and plots "the shortcomings of the tangent vectors suggested by Kochanek and Bartels", so porting 1984 TCB code unchanged into a non-uniform keyframe system is a mistake ([splines](https://splines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/euclidean/kochanek-bartels-non-uniform.html)). C2 "Continuous Acceleration" (Blender solves a tridiagonal system under the assumption that handles are one third of the interval) is smoother out of the box but "any changes in the key values may affect interpolation over a significant stretch of the curve" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/graph_editor/fcurves/properties.html); [Blender curve.cc](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/curve.cc)); offer it as an opt-in per curve, never as the default, because non-local edits are exactly what animators hate in a graph editor.

Hold keys and extrapolation are universal and should be modelled as time rules, not curve shapes. Every tool has a hold (AE Hold "available only as a temporal method", Blender Constant, Maya Stepped, Cavalry Step, Rive Hold, Lottie `h: 1`, CSS `steps()`), lottie-web bypasses the easing solve for hold keys, and Lottie permits at most two keyframes at the same `t` as an explicit jump ([lottie-spec](https://lottie.github.io/lottie-spec/latest/specs/properties/); [lottie-web](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web/blob/master/player/js/utils/PropertyFactory.js)). A hold is a deliberate C0 break, so it must also terminate any auto-tangent or C2 smoothing run, which is what Blender's solver does by smoothing "continuous sub-sequences of free auto handles" only ([Blender curve.cc](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/blenkernel/intern/curve.cc)). Extrapolation (constant, linear, cycle, cycle-with-offset, ping-pong, "continue" at the end velocity) appears as Maya Pre/Post Infinity, Blender extrapolation plus the Cycles modifier, and AE's `loopOut("cycle" | "pingpong" | "offset" | "continue")` ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-619CAE9C-107D-4504-A769-227DDC847153.htm); [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/using/expression-language-reference.html)). Implement these as pure remaps of t (and, for offset, of value): t′ = t₀ + ((t − t₀) mod T) for cycle, reflected modulo for ping-pong, plus k·(v_end − v_start) for offset. A cycle is seamless only when value and slope match at both ends, which Blender guarantees by solving auto handles cyclically ("full cycle merges first and last points into continuous loop"); if you do not solve cyclically, show the seam. Lottie has no extrapolation primitives, so loops must be baked to keys on export.

Three property types need their own rules on top of the scalar curve. For position, AE and Lottie keep the path as a spatial Bézier through the keyed positions (Lottie's `to`/`ti` tangents) and drive motion along it by mapping the temporal ease to an arc-length fraction: lottie-web samples each segment at **150 points by default**, accumulates partial lengths, computes `distanceInLine = segmentLength · perc`, and linearly interpolates between samples ([lottie-web bez.js](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web/blob/master/player/js/utils/bez.js); [lottie-web common.js](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web/blob/master/player/js/utils/common.js)). This decoupling is what lets a user retime without reshaping and reshape without retiming; adopt it, with a fixed sample count so position(t) is a pure function of (t, path), and never accumulate distance frame to frame. AE's roving keyframes, where intermediate spatial keys "are not linked to a specific time; their speed and timing are determined by adjacent keyframes" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/speed.html)), are a valuable extension (one speed curve across several path segments), but Adobe does not publish the solver, so budget for original work there. For 2-D rotation, keep an unbounded scalar in degrees: Lottie's `r` has no bound in the schema, AE's `loopOut("offset")` keeps a wheel spinning, and normalising to [0, 360) at keyframe time would destroy multi-turn intent and introduce the shortest-path ambiguity Blender documents for quaternions ("you must set up many intermediate keyframes, 180° from each other at most") ([lottie-spec](https://github.com/lottie/lottie-spec/blob/main/schema/helpers/transform.json); [Blender](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/advanced/appendices/rotations.html)). If 3-D orientation ever arrives, use slerp between keys and avoid squad, whose behaviour "becomes more and more erratic" with uneven key times ([splines](https://splines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rotation/squad.html)). For colour, follow CSS Color 4: interpolation "defaults to Oklab" because gamma-encoded sRGB "produces poorer results (overly dark or greyish mixes)"; premultiply alpha before interpolating; for polar spaces use the "shorter" hue arc by default and treat an achromatic key's hue as missing so it inherits the other key's hue ([CSS Color 4](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/blob/main/css-color-4/Overview.bs)). An overshooting ease (y outside [0, 1]) on a colour pushes chroma or lightness out of gamut, so gamut-map after interpolation.

| Property | Curve model | Default tangent | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opacity, scale, skew, numbers, 2-D rotation (unbounded degrees) | (time, value) cubic Bézier, handle x clamped to segment | Monotone auto (time-weighted), continuous/broken flag | Matches AE/Blender/Maya/Cavalry/Rive; no overshoot or boomerang by default |
| Position | Spatial Bézier path + one scalar timing curve mapped through a fixed-sample arc-length table | Spatial: auto Bézier through keys; temporal: monotone | AE/Lottie model; retiming and reshaping stay independent |
| Colour | Same timing curve; blend in Oklab (Oklch with hue arc for deliberate sweeps), premultiplied | n/a | CSS Color 4 default; avoids grey mid-points |
| Booleans, enums, visibility | Hold only | n/a | Universal; hold bypasses the easing solve |
| 3-D orientation (future) | Quaternion slerp per segment | n/a | Squad is erratic with uneven spacing |

## Ease belongs to the segment, not to half-tangents on keys

The strongest UX signal in the research is that After Effects' power is hidden behind modes, and users experience the modes as clunkiness. The Graph Editor's mutually exclusive Value and Speed views are "pretty confusing to animators who are new to motion design" ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/graph-editor-after-effects)); Position has no value-graph handles until the user runs Separate Dimensions, which in turn removes the motion-path handles from the Composition panel ("you loose the ability to edit a curve in the Comp panel"), so experts advise "For Position, the speed graph is almost always the best choice" and shape the path first, then separate dimensions to retime ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/only-affect-x-value-in-graph-editor-not-y-or-vice-versa/td-p/13635386); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/why-do-my-graphs-look-like-this/m-p/15481046)). Keyframes are deselected when the Graph Editor opens, framing the graph reframes the timeline, and a 2023 beta let users drag a speed handle without seeing the graph update ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effects-selected-keyframes-selection-not-going-to-graph-editor/m-p/10764725/highlight/true); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/ae-beta-2023-0-0-speed-graph-editor-speed-curve-no-longer-responds-to-easing-changes-314154)). The paid ecosystem is a free requirements document: Flow exists because AE has no CSS-style `cubic-bezier` popover with presets ("works with the same timing functions as CSS animation", 25 Penner presets, team-shareable libraries) ([Motion Array](https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/flow-after-effects-extension-review/)); EaseCopy exists because AE cannot copy an ease independently of a value ("Copy/paste your eases without overwriting your values"), and it has to "scale your eases appropriately and even flip them" to make them portable, with a community idea asking Adobe to ship it natively ([Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=37577); [Adobe idea](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/ship-easecopy-plugin-standard-with-ae/idi-p/13563168)); Dojo Ease exists because there is no numeric batch entry across keys ([Dojo Ease](https://creativedojo.gumroad.com/l/dojo-ease)). Each of those is cheap to build in if ease is modelled as a property of the key pair rather than as two half-tangents on each key, which is also why the segment model composes with presets and copy/paste and the AE model does not.

That segment model is now the common denominator of the newer tools. Rive shows an Interpolation panel beside the timeline whenever keys are selected, with Linear, Cubic, Hold and Elastic types and "A text field below the preview graph represents the interpolation in a numerical format" ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing)); Jitter's custom easing (February 2025) is handles on a graph plus numeric fields, saveable and reusable ([Jitter](https://jitter.video/changelog/2025-02-18-custom-easings)); Figma lets users "enter a cubic-bezier value directly into the field" and allows y outside 0–1 for wind-up and overshoot ([Figma](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051748654-Prototype-easing-and-spring-animations)); Theatre.js 0.5 added presets with "fuzzy-matched search" ([Theatre.js](https://www.theatrejs.com/blog/theatre-05-is-out)); Cavalry assigns "Magic Easing" by right-clicking the keybar between two keys, explicitly "following the forthcoming Web Animation standard" ([Cavalry 1.0.1](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/1.0/1-0-1-release-notes/); [Cavalry 1.0.2](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/1.0/1-0-2-release-notes/)). Two details matter. First, named presets are not enough: on top of Magic Easing, both a free script (Easey, "The missing speed graph for Cavalry") and a paid one (Scenery Curves: 24 Penner presets, typed control points, "copy as CSS cubic-bezier") re-create a draggable cubic-Bézier handle editor ([Easey](https://github.com/sammularczyk/easey); [Scenery Curves](https://scenery-vk9r.onrender.com/scripts/curves-42xvUYqCpvY/manual)), and Rive added a "Cubic Value" mode that "gives you draggable Bezier handles directly on the Graph Editor, similar to After Effects" ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing)). Second, removing keyframes entirely caps the product: Jitter's operations model "aren't tied to absolute property values" and copies across layers, but reviewers say it "lacks deep timeline control, scripting, 3D, and physics-based effects, so power users can reach its ceiling" ([Jitter](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/16415500-understand-operations-vs-keyframes); [IconPolls](https://iconpolls.com/blogs/jitter-review-2026-app-animations-video-files-download-templates-pricing-user-experience-and-faqs)); Fable, which replaced keys with Behaviors, shut down in November 2024 ([Fable](https://fable.app/blog/fable-is-winding-down)); and the tools that started simple are converging back, with LottieFiles' Advanced Graph Editor that "mirrors the keyframe curve editing experience of After Effects" and Spline's September 2025 timeline with a graph editor ([LottieFiles](https://docs.lottiefiles.com/en/creator/07_animation/graph-editor); [Spline](https://blog.spline.design/introducing-3d-timeline-animation)). **Ship popover-first and graph-editor-available, with both views editing the same segment-ease object**, so every property including vectors has one 2-D ease per segment and the Separate-Dimensions trap never exists. Give the popover presets with both CSS and Penner names (Figma users complain that "the basic 'ease' option is missing" and that they must "enter the custom bezier values every time" ([Figma forum](https://forum.figma.com/suggest-a-feature-11/please-consider-adding-ease-to-interaction-timing-functions-33864))), and make the default for new keys an asymmetric curve such as Material's standard `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` ([material-web tokens](https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)) rather than AE's symmetric Easy Ease, which practitioners call "a weak starting point" ([Motion Array](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/create-better-eases-in-after-effects/)). One known hand-off problem to solve on day one: Figma, LottieFiles and Rive all expose springs as parameters and none exports a spring to a cubic-bezier (a Figma thread on recreating the "Gentle" spring closed without an answer) ([Figma forum](https://forum.figma.com/ask-the-community-7/recreating-gentle-easing-using-bezier-curves-26763)); a "bake to keys" command on every non-Bézier ease fixes it.

The 3-D incumbents own the vocabulary for editing many keys at once, and most of it is cheap. Blender's Normalize view "Scales the display of each curve so that they all (appear to) occupy the same value range, going from -1 to 1", the standard answer to editing opacity (0–1) and position (0–1920) in one graph ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/editors/graph_editor/introduction.html)); Maya has the same Display Normalized mode ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2019/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-ABB47176-9119-47F2-8466-6FA19C427F9F.htm)). Blender's keyframe types (Keyframe, Breakdown, Extreme, Jitter, Moving Hold) change only the colour yet give animators a mental model of structural versus filler keys ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/animation/keyframes/introduction.html)). Blender's Breakdowner is tweening without a graph editor: press Shift+E with the playhead between two keys and drag, "The more I go to the right, the more I favor the next key", with a live percentage; Blend to Neighbor and Push/Relax are the same slider pattern ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/animation/in_betweens.html); [LinkedIn Learning](https://origin-www.lynda.com/Blender-tutorials/How-effectively-apply-concept-timing/421798/455462-4.html)). Maya's Retime markers ("drag the markers to directly adjust the timing of key movements") and Lattice Deform Keys generalise AE's Alt-drag into non-uniform retiming and value-space warping of a key group ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-A6061A36-81FF-4F57-A081-C967F749D546.htm); [Maya](https://download.autodesk.com/global/docs/maya2014/en_us/files/Lattice_Deform_Keys_Tool.htm)). The one retiming gesture everyone loves, AE's Alt-drag of an edge key, has a documented defect: "time stretching almost always leads to subframe positioning of keyframes", with a standing request for a quantise modifier ([Adobe idea](https://community.adobe.com:443/t5/after-effects-ideas/option-to-quantize-keyframes-when-time-stretching-with-alt-key/idc-p/14067207)); Blender's "Absolute Time Snap" (keys "snap to multiples of Snap To") and Maya 2025.1's Time Snap both exist for this reason ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/editors/dope_sheet/editing.html); [CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/maya-2025-1-released.html)). **Snap retimed keys to whole frames by default and make sub-frame placement an explicit opt-in**; with a rational-time core, a key at frame 37 of 30 fps is exactly 37/30, which is a real advantage over float-time tools when users retime repeatedly. Add ripple semantics from Motion Canvas, where dragging a time event moves "the events after it by default" and Shift isolates it, so that fixing one pause fixes everything after it ([Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io/docs/time-events)).

Two smaller patterns prevent the "state loss" complaints that dominate the sentiment data. Auto-key in animate mode without a stopwatch (Linearity: "editing an element's properties adds keyframes to the Timeline automatically"; Fable auto-generated consecutive keys) is now expected, but it needs Rive's three-state key affordance (grey stroke = not keyed, blue stroke = animated, blue fill = keyed at this frame) because Rive also has to warn that "Moving, resizing, rotating, or recoloring an object while a timeline is open can trigger" an accidental key ([Linearity](https://www.linearity.io/academy/move/mac/user-guide/interface/timeline/); [Rive keys](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/keys.md); [Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/animate-mode-overview)). Keep selection, pan and zoom out of the undo stack and stable across views: AE loses selection when the graph opens, and in Rive undoing a key deletion "also undos your most recent scroll/zoom action", which users call disorienting ([Rive forum](https://www.rive.app/community/forums/bugs/fsLjspgTHYcR/undoing-keyframe-deletion-also-reverts-recent-timeline-panzoom-actions/ftolrYCh6C4A)). In the viewport, keep AE's motion-path handles on the canvas (the thing Separate Dimensions destroys), and add Maya's motion-trail "timing beads" that "control the ease-in and ease-out of animations at each keyframe" directly on the path, plus ghosting of neighbouring frames, so animators rarely need the graph at all ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2016/ENU/Maya/files/GUID-2F2ADC71-0DC5-4F2F-96C8-0B2F061F0703.htm)). AE's Shift+J/K (navigate only the selected layer's keys, added in 23.0) is the kind of detail that shows up in praise lists and costs an afternoon ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/feature-focus-new-shift-j-and-shift-k-keyboard-shortcuts-in-after-effects-314162)).

## Derive time from the timestamp, render in a worker, test frames out of order

No mainstream web runtime uses exact rational time (lottie-web counts float frames, Theatre.js float seconds, Motion Canvas integer frames), so the team's core is ahead of the field; the architecture work is in mapping the display clock onto it without drift and in presenting frames on a regular cadence. The `requestAnimationFrame` timestamp "will generally match the display refresh rate", callbacks "are paused in most browsers when running in background tabs", and MDN's explicit advice is to compute progress from the timestamp argument, otherwise "the animation will run faster on high refresh-rate screens" ([MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/window/requestanimationframe/index.md)). The Web Animations model computes timeline time "as a fixed offset from the |now| timestamp", and its output "is based solely on the input time and is independent of previous inputs" ([Web Animations 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/main/web-animations-1/Overview.bs)). The two robust patterns in shipping code are timeline time = timestamp − origin (GSAP's `_startTime`, Rive's use of `document.timeline.currentTime`) and frame-grid-aligned ticking with a tolerance: Anime.js skips a tick only if `elapsed + min(frameDuration·0.25, 4 ms) < frameDuration` and then re-anchors to `time − (elapsed % frameDuration)` because "Tolerance prevents dropping frames that arrive a bit early due to RAF jitter" ([Anime.js clock](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/juliangarnier/anime/master/src/core/clock.js)). **Compute the composition frame as floor((rafTimestamp − playStartTimestamp) × fps / 1000 + ε) on every callback and evaluate at the exact rational time frame/fps; never increment a frame counter per callback.** Every runtime that survives background tabs also re-origins or clamps large deltas: GSAP's `lagSmoothing` treats any gap over **500 ms as a 33 ms frame**, PixiJS caps the step at 100 ms, Phaser substitutes the last sane delta and enters a 120-frame cooldown, and Three.js's Timer resets on `visibilitychange` ([GSAP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/greensock/GSAP/master/src/gsap-core.js); [PixiJS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pixijs/pixijs/dev/src/ticker/Ticker.ts); [Phaser](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/phaserjs/phaser/master/src/core/TimeStep.js); [Three.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/src/core/Timer.js)). For an editor the right equivalent is pause-on-hidden and re-anchor the origin on resume, so the play position stays frame-exact instead of jumping.

Judder when the composition rate does not divide the refresh rate is decided by which refresh each frame lands on, not by evaluation. Android's frame-pacing documentation describes the mechanism exactly: a 30 fps loop on a 60 Hz panel "doesn't realize that a repeated frame remains on the screen for an extra 16 milliseconds", producing frame times like **49 ms, 16 ms, 33 ms**, and "Short frames followed by long frames are perceived by the player as stuttering" ([Android](https://developer.android.com/games/sdk/frame-pacing)). A 60 fps stream on a 120 Hz panel is clean only when each frame is held exactly two refreshes, and 24 fps on 60 Hz alternates two and three refreshes ([Blur Busters](https://forums.blurbusters.com/viewtopic.php?p=80448)). Safari limits web content to about 60 fps on ProMotion displays unless a feature flag is changed, while Chrome and Firefox run rAF at 120 fps ([WebKit bug](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=236780)), so the Electron build will see 120 Hz callbacks on such hardware and the browser build may not. The options are a regular cadence (change the displayed frame on the rAF whose timestamp crosses the frame boundary, and never skip-and-double unpredictably), sub-frame interpolation as lottie-web's `setSubframe(true)` does by default ("will update on every requestAnimationFrame with intermediate values"), or locking to an integer multiple when fps divides the refresh rate ([lottie-web README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/airbnb/lottie-web/master/README.md)). Sub-frame interpolation is the smoothest preview and is trivially available from a rational-time core, but it shows images that will not exist in the export; make frame-exact the authoring default and expose "smooth preview" as a toggle, mirroring `setSubframe` and dotlottie's `useFrameInterpolation`.

Scrubbing is three layers. First, coalesce input to one evaluation per frame and always render the latest requested time: Rive stores a single `scrubTo` slot and, on advance, does "instance.time = 0; instance.advance(scrubTo)", a reset-then-advance seek rather than a queue ([rive.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rive-app/rive-wasm/master/js/src/rive.ts); [Animation.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rive-app/rive-wasm/master/js/src/animation/Animation.ts)). A debounce adds latency and a queue adds lag that grows with drag speed; a latest-wins slot makes stale-frame cancellation implicit. Browsers already merge pointer updates, so `getCoalescedEvents()` is only useful for pre-warming a cache in the direction of travel ([MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/pointerevent/getcoalescedevents/index.md)). Second, keep an uncached frame cheap enough to land within one refresh: Motion Canvas separates preview and render scale, and the open-source Cap editor cured timeline lag by virtualising the timeline, throttling frame rendering to about 30 fps with rAF batching, prefetching in parallel with a frame-skip cap of 3, and raising its decoded-frame cache from 100 to 500 frames ([Cap PR](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/pull/1417)). Render at reduced resolution and skip expensive effects while the pointer is down, then re-render the final frame on release and fill neighbouring frames while idle. Third, cache rendered frames for playback on the After Effects model: "After Effects renders individual frames and temporarily stores them as cache in RAM", overwriting the oldest when full, and AE 25.2 added disk-to-RAM streaming because before it the playable length was capped by RAM ([School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/cached-preview); [Puget Systems](https://www.pugetsystems.com/blog/2025/02/24/upcoming-boost-to-after-effects-preview-playback-with-ram-and-disk-cache/)). The arithmetic is unforgiving: a 1920×1080 RGBA8 frame is 1920 × 1080 × 4 ≈ 8.3 MB, so 100 cached frames is about 0.83 GB of GPU memory, and browsers do not report a budget (a Khronos list test saw Chrome allocate 15 GB before context loss on a 3 GB GPU, with iOS failing near 1 GB) ([Khronos](https://www.khronos.org/webgl/public-mailing-list/public_webgl/1505/msg00018.php)). Probe by allocating and checking for `OUT_OF_MEMORY` and `webglcontextlost`, keep a configurable hard cap of a few hundred 1080p frames, and treat disk tiering as a later Electron-only feature. Motion Canvas is the cautionary example for the whole section: because its scenes are generators, a backward seek must "reset()" and replay from the scene start ([PlaybackManager](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/motion-canvas/motion-canvas/main/packages/core/src/app/PlaybackManager.ts)); a pure function of time makes random access O(1) per frame, so guard that property jealously.

Threading follows from the sources. `OffscreenCanvas` can be transferred to a worker and "It's also possible to use `requestAnimationFrame()` in workers" ([MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/offscreencanvas/index.md)); the WHATWG design notes explain why the worker must own its rAF: relaying the main thread's tick "may add undue latency to the signal, especially when the browsing context's event loop is busy", and an unthrottled GPU-accelerated worker loop can "cause the accumulation of a multi-frame rendering backlog, resulting in high latency and eventually OOM crashes" ([WHATWG wiki](https://wiki.whatwg.org/wiki/OffscreenCanvas.requestAnimationFrame)). So: the UI thread owns the DOM timeline and pointer input and writes {requested time numerator, denominator, playing, generation} into a `SharedArrayBuffer` with `Atomics.store`; the render worker runs its own rAF on the transferred canvas, reads the latest state at the top of each frame (latest-wins for free), evaluates at exact rational time, renders, and never queues more than one frame. Shared memory requires a secure, cross-origin-isolated context (COOP/COEP headers), which also raises `performance.now()` resolution from 100 µs to 5 µs ([MDN SAB](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/javascript/reference/global_objects/sharedarraybuffer/index.md); [MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/performance/now/index.md)); Electron can set those headers on its own scheme, and the browser build should fall back to `postMessage` with the same byte layout. Chromium's pipeline adds at least one vsync between the callback and pixels, drops work that misses the activation deadline, and may block the swap "until the next vblank" when the GPU queue is full ([Chromium](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/docs/life_of_a_frame.md)), so use the worker's rAF as the only pacing source. In Electron, set `webPreferences.backgroundThrottling: false` (or `setBackgroundThrottling(false)`) on the editor window, otherwise a preview behind another window has its animations and timers throttled ([Electron](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/main/docs/api/structures/web-preferences.md); [Electron](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/electron/electron/main/docs/api/web-contents.md)); drive export with an as-fast-as-possible loop that yields explicitly, as Motion Canvas's renderer does, never with rAF ([Renderer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/motion-canvas/motion-canvas/main/packages/core/src/app/Renderer.ts)). Electron's offscreen-rendering mode is for headless capture, not interactive preview: it accepts only 1–240 fps without `useSharedTexture`, and its default path copies GPU to CPU ([Electron](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/main/docs/tutorial/offscreen-rendering.md)); `--disable-frame-rate-limit` is not documented on Electron's switch list and is absent from Chromium's `cc/base/switches.cc`, so treat it as unsupported ([Electron](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/main/docs/api/command-line-switches.md)).

Rendering details that keep interpolated motion clean are small and specific. Motion blur in motion-graphics tools is defined by shutter angle, phase and sample count, with AE's defaults reported as **180°, 16 samples per frame and an adaptive limit of 128**, and a phase of −90° for a 180° shutter to centre the blur ([Creative Cow](https://creativecow.net/?p=97118); [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/cmg_hidden_gems_chapter_8_motion_blur_and_more/)); EEVEE's accumulation blur likewise re-evaluates the scene per time step ([Blender](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/eevee/render_settings/motion_blur.html)). With a pure function of time this is simply N exact-time evaluations accumulated into a float target, and play, seek and export produce identical blur, something velocity-buffer post-process blur derived from frame deltas cannot guarantee; use a lower N while scrubbing. Keep the drawing buffer premultiplied (the WebGL default; the compositor "will assume the drawing buffer contains colors with premultiplied alpha"), avoid `preserveDrawingBuffer` for the preview, and read back exports from your own framebuffer with a fence rather than a synchronous `readPixels` ([WebGL spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/WebGL/main/specs/latest/1.0/index.html); [MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/webgl_api/webgl_best_practices/index.md)). Pre-snap the canvas to whole device pixels with `ResizeObserver` and `device-pixel-content-box` because a fractional `devicePixelRatio` "will cause moire artifacts", and never pixel-snap the content itself; call `generateMipmaps()` or set `LINEAR` filtering, since the default min filter expects mipmaps ([MDN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/webgl_api/webgl_best_practices/index.md)). Upload evaluated property values as a uniform buffer per frame; there is no documented web example of evaluating keyframe curves on the GPU, and the native-engine pattern is to bake curves to a 256-sample table and sample it in the shader ([Unity forum](https://discussions.unity.com/t/need-way-to-evaluate-animationcurve-in-the-job/702132)), which only pays when thousands of per-element values feed shaders directly.

Determinism needs an explicit contract and a test harness, and Remotion has written the clearest one: it renders "in several tabs at once", so "Frame 30 might be rendered before frame 10, or frame 50 might be rendered twice"; a component must "always display the same image when called multiple times", must "not animate when the video is paused", and must use a seeded `random(seed)` instead of `Math.random()`; falling back to `--concurrency=1` "will fix flickering / choppyness in many cases, but it is way slower and the correct timing of the animations is still not guaranteed" ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering)). GSAP's tween render is the same idea in a tweening library: value is a pure function of `totalTime`, and `prevTime` is used only to decide event direction, never the value ([GSAP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/greensock/GSAP/master/src/gsap-core.js)). Gaffer's loop analysis adds the floating-point caveat that semi-fixed stepping is "not *exactly the same*" across runs and exact reproducibility "requires a fully fixed dt" ([Gaffer on Games](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gafferongames/gafferongames/master/content/post/fix_your_timestep.md)); for the team this means converting the rational time to a double once, in one place, and running the same evaluator build in preview and export. Rive's state machines, which are advanced incrementally with `advanceAndApply(elapsedTime)` and are therefore history-dependent, mark the boundary of what belongs on a timeline ([rive.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rive-app/rive-wasm/master/js/src/rive.ts)). **Test like Remotion: render a composition sequentially, in random frame order, in N parallel workers and after scrubbing back and forth, assert byte-identical (or ε-identical for GPU) frames, keep a golden-frame set, and include "pause does not change the image" as a case.** This harness is also how you will validate every feature in the next section.

## Closed-form springs and seeded noise keep procedural motion seekable

Every shipping spring is a damped harmonic oscillator; the only decision that matters for the invariant is closed form versus numeric integration. Apple's SwiftUI `Spring` is evaluated directly as a function of time via `value(target:initialVelocity:time:)`, which "Calculates the value of the spring at a given time given a target amount of change" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spring)); Motion's generator has explicit closed forms for all three regimes (underdamped `target − env·(A·sin + delta·cos)`, critically damped `target − exp(−ω·t)·(delta + (v + ω·delta)·t)`, overdamped as a sum of a slow and a fast exponential) with an analytic velocity ([Motion spring.ts](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/packages/motion-dom/src/animation/generators/spring.ts)); Flutter's engine "models the closed form of the second order differential equation" and matches iOS's `CASpringAnimation` ([Flutter](https://flutter.googlesource.com/mirrors/engine/+/HEAD/third_party/spring_animation/spring_animation.h)); Remotion's `spring({frame, fps, config})` is evaluated per frame for video rendering ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/spring)). React Spring, by contrast, integrates with fixed 1 ms semi-implicit Euler steps inside `advance()` ([react-spring](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-spring/blob/main/packages/core/src/SpringValue.ts)), and Anime.js "simulates the spring in small time steps" and requires 200 ms at rest before stopping ([Anime.js](https://www.mintlify.com/juliangarnier/anime/easings/spring)); those are deterministic only by replaying from zero and are a poor fit for seek and export. The wobble library chose closed form over RK4 precisely because it is "Easier to generate keyframes and build continuous/interruptible gestures and animations" ([wobble](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/skevy/wobble/master/README.md)). **Adopt a closed-form oscillator evaluator as the second of your two evaluator families**, and copy Motion's overdamped formulation rather than the textbook sinh/cosh one: the older form overflowed, was capped at 300, and the cap made slow overdamped springs "visibly snap to the target mid-animation" (at stiffness 4, damping 35, mass 0.5 the value jumped from ≈625 to ≈989 between 8594 ms and 8700 ms); the fix uses λ_slow = −ω₀/(ζ + √(ζ² − 1)) to avoid cancellation ([Motion plan 031](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/plans/031-overdamped-spring-exponential-form.md)).

Expose springs to designers with Apple's parametrisation and define "duration" the settle-based way. Apple's `Spring(duration:bounce:)` takes a bounce from −1 to 1 and a perceptual duration "chosen to be predictable and not move around, even as the other parameters of a spring change"; its documented example maps **duration 0.5, bounce 0.3 to mass 1, stiffness 157.9, damping 17.6**, which is reproduced exactly by ω₀ = 2π/duration, ζ = 1 − bounce, stiffness = ω₀², damping = 2ζω₀ ([Apple WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spring)). Motion's `findSpring` uses the same ζ = 1 − bounce convention but solves for the frequency at which the decay envelope falls below 0.001 at the requested duration ([Motion plan 033](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/plans/033-findspring-closed-form.md)); Remotion measures the natural settle time and rescales so the spring "is first stretched to the duration that you pass", with a default rest threshold of 0.005 ([Remotion](https://v3.remotion.dev/docs/measure-spring)). The settle-based definition is the one a timeline needs, because it guarantees the value is within ε of the target when the next key begins; Apple's perceptual duration is a nicer slider but "technically it keeps moving forever" ([Apple WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)). Whatever threshold you pick (Motion's `restDelta` 0.5 or 0.005 when granular, wobble's 0.001, Figma's "0.1%"), compute the end time once from the parameters so play, seek and export agree on when the following key starts ([Motion spring.ts](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/packages/motion-dom/src/animation/generators/spring.ts); [Figma forum](https://forum.figma.com/archive-21/how-does-spring-animation-duration-work-33227)), and reject damping 0, which "will run forever". Rive shows how to present a spring-like ease as a per-segment interpolation type: its Elastic mode has Amplitude and Period, is evaluated as `valueFrom + (valueTo − valueFrom) · transform(factor)` with a decaying sine, and is a pure function of normalised segment time ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing); [rive-runtime](https://github.com/rive-app/rive-runtime/blob/main/src/animation/elastic_interpolator.cpp)). Do the same with (bounce, duration) per segment and derive ω₀ from the segment length. Interruption, Apple's "velocity it had when it was retargeted as the initial velocity towards its new destination", stays pure if each retarget is stored as a new segment whose initial conditions (x, v) are evaluated from the previous closed form at the retarget time; note Motion zeroes inherited velocity for duration-defined springs because the duration solve assumes v₀ = 0, so carrying velocity means either re-solving or accepting a slightly different settle time ([Motion spring.ts](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/packages/motion-dom/src/animation/generators/spring.ts)). Follow-through that tracks a keyframed value as a moving target, which is what Cavalry's experimental Spring behaviour does for "dynamic secondary motion to a Shape's position keyframes" ([Cavalry](https://cavalry.studio/docs/nodes/behaviours/spring/)), is a simulation whose output depends on the whole input history; offer it only as a non-destructive layer that is baked from the composition start at the composition frame rate, with the bake re-derivable from the stored parameters.

After Effects expressions are the reference for procedural motion that stays seekable, and the pattern is that every operator takes time as an argument. `wiggle(freq, amp, octaves, amp_mult, t)` accepts a base time "if you want the output to be a wiggle of the property value sampled at a different time", `valueAtTime(t)` samples the keyframed value anywhere, `smooth(width = 0.2, samples = 5, t)` averages a finite window around t, and `loopOut` remaps t into the keyed range ([AE expression reference](https://github.com/docsforadobe/after-effects-expression-reference/blob/master/docs/objects/property.md)). A procedural operator is seekable exactly when it is f(t, params, keys) with no hidden per-frame state, which rules out accumulated random walks; noise must be a hash of (t, seed) with the seed derived from layer and property identity, as Remotion's seeded `random()` requires. Cavalry generalises this into stackable Behaviours (Noise, Oscillator, Stagger and more) and its Keyframe Layers, which blend several keyframe stacks with a Strength and Normal ("a layer's keys add to the layers beneath it") or Overwrite modes ([Cavalry](https://docs.cavalry.scenegroup.co/user-interface/menus/window-menu/scene-window/keyframe-layers)); that is the right container for procedural and AI-generated motion beside hand keys. Stagger is a time offset, not a new evaluator: GSAP's stagger distributes start times by `each`, `amount`, `from` (start, center, end, edges, random, index), `grid` and an `ease` that "distributes the start times of the animations" ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/resources/getting-started/Staggers/)), and Cavalry's Stagger generates "sequential values between a minimum and maximum" per duplicate ([Cavalry](https://docs.cavalry.scenegroup.co/nodes/behaviours/stagger)); evaluating layer i at time t is f(t − delay(i)). Rive's state machines and blend states are a different kind of thing, "input-driven" and advanced from live inputs ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/state-machine/states)); the seekable equivalent is a blend between two keyframed curves driven by a keyframed weight, which is what Cavalry's Keyframe Layer Strength already is.

Presets split cleanly into what a single Bézier can hold and what cannot. All Penner polynomial, sine, expo and circ families and the "back" overshoot family are single cubics with y-handles beyond the unit square (easeInOutCubic = 0.65, 0, 0.35, 1; easeOutBack = 0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1), while elastic and bounce are marked `css: no` ([easings.net source](https://github.com/ai/easings.net/blob/master/src/easings.yml)). Material 3's tokens are all single cubics (standard 0.2, 0, 0, 1; emphasized-decelerate 0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1; durations from 50 ms to 1000 ms) ([material-web](https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss); [Flutter](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/blob/master/packages/flutter/lib/src/material/motion.dart)). For the rest, GSAP's approach is the most editable: `CustomBounce` emits a sequence of cubic segments with each bounce shrinking geometrically, `CustomWiggle` builds a path whose half-cycles alternate sign under an amplitude envelope (with an "anticipate" type that negates the first peak), and both hand the path to `CustomEase` ([GSAP CustomBounce](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP/blob/master/src/CustomBounce.js); [GSAP CustomWiggle](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP/blob/master/src/CustomWiggle.js)). **Store a library entry as either four numbers or a multi-segment path in the unit square, support multi-segment eases between one key pair in the same graph editor, and make "apply" write handles onto selected key pairs** as Flow's "apply the curve to each pair of keys" does ([Motion Array](https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/flow-after-effects-extension-review/)); a multi-segment path is still an exact pure function of time.

Recording and curve fitting turn performances into keys, and the shipping thresholds are small and unit-specific. AE's Motion Sketch defaults smoothing to 1 and a user who set it to zero got a key on every frame ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2023/05/17/quickly-create-complex-motion-paths-in-after-effects/)); the Smoother's Tolerance "will define the number of pixels new Position keyframes are allowed to vary from the original path", with practitioners using about 10 px ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/creating_motion_graphics_with_after_effects_hidden_gems_chapter_5_-_animati/); [LinkedIn Learning](https://www.lynda.com/After-Effects-tutorials/Working-Smoother/78545/79322-4.html?src=c.r.su)); Maya's Simplify defaults are **0.05 s time tolerance and 0.01 value tolerance** ([Maya](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-455F76C5-F761-4007-AFFE-E0F80BEFB627.htm)); Blender's Decimate converts linear keys to Bézier with one-third handles first, then removes keys that "affect the curve shape the least" by ratio (0.333) or error margin ([Blender](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/editors/animation/keyframes_general.cc); [Blender API](https://docs.blender.org/api/4.2/bpy.ops.graph.html)). The fitter nearly everyone ports is Schneider's from Graphics Gems: chord-length parameterisation, a 2×2 least-squares solve for the tangent lengths, up to 4 Newton reparameterisation rounds when the error is within 4× tolerance, and a split at the maximum-error point otherwise ([FitCurves.c](https://github.com/erich666/GraphicsGems/blob/master/gems/FitCurves.c); [fit-curve](https://www.npmjs.com/package/fit-curve)). Use it unchanged for spatial paths with a pixel tolerance; for value-versus-time curves parameterise by time and constrain handle x inside the segment so the result is a function of time. The pipeline that yields hand-editable keys is: low-pass the samples, force splits at velocity extrema (these become the key poses), Schneider-fit each span with tangents continuous across splits, snap key times to frames, and keep the raw recording as a hidden source so the user can re-fit at another tolerance without re-performing. Keep "smooth values, times fixed" distinct from "simplify" in the UI: Blender's Gaussian smooth samples the curve per frame, convolves with a half-kernel (**sigma 0.33, width 6**), writes back only the value at each existing key and blends by a factor; its Butterworth smooth is a forward-backward IIR low-pass with a **3 Hz cutoff and order 4** that "preserves the peaks of the animation" but "can introduce a ripple effect when the key values change rapidly"; its classic Smooth Keys is a 5-tap weighted average that deliberately does not touch endpoints "otherwise, curves slowly explode" ([Blender](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/editors/animation/keyframes_general.cc); [Blender API](https://docs.blender.org/api/4.2/bpy.ops.graph.html); [Blender manual PR](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender-manual/pulls/104511/files)). All of these are transforms on key data, so the result remains a pure function of time.

AI assistance, as of October 2026, has shipped in one shape only: a model emits editable parameters, never frames. Jitter's "AI Brainstorm" (November 2025) produces "a fully editable animation", and its "Animate with AI" (May 2026) generates a custom effect refinable by "tweaking animation parameters manually" ([Jitter](https://jitter.video/changelog/2025-11-25-ai-brainstorm/); [Jitter](https://thomas.jitter.video/changelog/2026-05-13-animate-with-ai/)); Figma Motion (Config, June 2026, open beta) lets users "prompt effects, apply presets, or refine them manually on a timeline" ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/6/figma-launches-a-new-integrated-motion-design-tool-alongside-code-layers-and-new-ai-features/)); Rive's AI Agent generates scripts and animation from the editor sidebar ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/editor/ai-agent)); Adobe's After Effects AI Assistant (IBC, September 2026) creates "expression rigs from natural-language prompts" and repairs broken expressions, with CineD noting Adobe "published its list of limitations at greater length than its list of capabilities" ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/adobe-video-ibc-2026-announcements/); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/?p=441813)). The research line is the same: Apple's Keyframer has GPT-4 write CSS animation code from an SVG and found that its 13 users preferred decomposed, one-element-at-a-time prompting plus direct code and property editing ([Keyframer](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06071v2)); Adobe's LogoMotion exposes generated code through "editing widgets, which are linked to the underlying code" ([LogoMotion](https://research.adobe.com/publication/logomotion-visually-grounded-code-synthesis-for-creating-and-editing-animation)); Stanford's MoVer verifies LLM-generated motion against a first-order-logic specification and feeds failures back, lifting correctness from **58.8% to 93.6% within 50 iterations** on 5,600 prompts ([MoVer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13372)). No shipped product performs learned in-betweening of property curves; "generative inbetweening" means pixel video synthesis between two images ([Generative Inbetweening](https://huggingface.co/papers/2408.15239.md)), which is neither seekable in your sense nor editable. **Point any AI feature at the keyframe and behaviour schema, verify its output with MoVer-style constraint checks run as tests, and surface it through the same direct-edit widgets as hand keys**; the smoothing itself then comes from the deterministic tools above, not from the model.

## Performance timing and tangent-space solves beat spacetime constraints

The best-validated direct-manipulation technique in the literature is one the app can build in a week on top of the spatial path it already has. Terra and Metoyer observed that for novices "setting keyframe values is straightforward while specifying the keyframe timing is difficult and often time consuming", and separated the two: keep the values, discard the times, and let the user perform the timing with a mouse while the system re-distributes the keys to match ([Terra & Metoyer 2004](https://diglib.eg.org/items/33142f18-9951-441a-a966-9f4b53c68bd8/full)). Dragimation (Graphics Interface 2012) refined this into dragging the object itself along its motion path while "the timing of the dragging motion is recorded and applied to the keyframes"; in a study with **27 participants including professional animators and novices**, it was "comparable regarding objective error measurements to the sketch-based approach and significantly better than scrubbing and is the overall preferred technique" ([Dragimation](https://almeida.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/karrer2012b.pdf)). The same idea, applied to data as DimpVis, was "subjectively preferred and quantitatively competitive with the traditional time slider" ([DimpVis](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2003_20tvcg12-kondo-2346250.pdf)). The implementation is entirely CPU-side at UI rate: arc-length-parameterise the interpolated path for the selected layer (you already have the table from the position model), project the cursor onto it with hysteresis to survive self-intersections, record (wall-clock time, path parameter) samples during the drag, assign each key the moment the drag passed its parameter, smooth the time warp, and snap to frames. The output is ordinary keys, so the invariant holds by construction. Stroke-speed-as-timing is the companion gesture: Motion Doodles parses a continuous stroke into timed motions ([Motion Doodles](https://cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2004-TOG-doodles/index.html)), and Space-Time Sketching showed that "squash and stretch is controlled directly by drawing faster and slower" ([Space-Time Sketching](https://hal.science/hal-01153763/file/SpaceTimeSketching.pdf)); a "draw a path to create keys" tool should keep the stylus timestamps as key times and may map pen speed to a secondary property. K-Sketch's demonstration-based recording, with an event-only timeline whose tick marks are dragged to retime, had participants working "about three times faster" with "half the learning time" and lower cognitive load than PowerPoint ([K-Sketch](https://www.springermedicine.com/doi/10.1145/1357054.1357122); [K-Sketch report](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2008/EECS-2008-171.html)).

Two signal-processing papers from 2006 give one-slider "liveliness" and "ease everything" operators. The Cartoon Animation Filter "adds a smoothed, inverted, and (sometimes) time shifted version of the second derivative (the acceleration) of the signal back into the original signal", producing "anticipation, follow-through, exaggeration and squash-and-stretch", and "The user only needs to set the desired strength of the filter" ([Wang et al. 2006](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/wang-2006-tca.pdf)); on a sampled property curve it is a 1-D convolution with a Laplacian-of-Gaussian kernel followed by subtraction, which the app can apply as a non-destructive effect on the evaluated channel or re-fit into keys. The Slow In and Slow Out filter time-warps motion to "concentrate motion around key frames", with one mode that "keeps the original duration by speeding up the in-between frames" ([White et al. 2006](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2006-siggraph-slowin.pdf)); that is a monotone warp t′ = w(t) compressing time near each key, the same primitive as "ease all selected keys" with a strength slider. Autodesk's sketch-animation line (Draco's kinetic textures, Kitty's in-situ relational graph drawn as sketched edges, Motion Amplifiers' stackable principle operators that deform a grid under the strokes) shows that anticipation, follow-through and secondary motion work best as composable operators on a base track rather than baked curves ([Draco](https://www.lri.fr/~anab/teaching/evaluation/papers/draco.pdf); [Kitty](https://www.research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/kitty-sketching-dynamic-and.pdf_recaeoUxJfgubRMCL.pdf); [Motion Amplifiers](https://research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/motion-amplifiers-sketching-dynamic.pdf_recLfzzXycCoes7Un.pdf)), but those interactions are patented (US 10,403,020 and 9,734,618) and should be checked before cloning verbatim; the LoG filter, time warps, path-drag retiming and displacement curves come from unencumbered 1995–2012 papers ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10403020); [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9734618)).

Perception studies settle two defaults and leave one gap. Dragicevic et al. tested constant speed, slow-in/slow-out, fast-in/fast-out and adaptive pacing and found slow-in/slow-out "gave the best object-tracking performance", explaining it by endpoint predictability rather than the naturalness animators cite ([Dragicevic et al. 2011](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/timedistort.pdf)); Heer and Robertson's two experiments showed "animated transitions can significantly improve graphical perception" and that simple staging helps ([Heer & Robertson 2007](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions)). Chevalier, Dragicevic and Franconeri found that "introducing staggering has a negligible, or even negative, impact on multiple object tracking performance", because it removes common-motion grouping and makes start times unpredictable ([Chevalier et al. 2014](https://hal.inrae.fr/CRISTAL-MINT/hal-01054408v1)). So slow-in/slow-out is the empirically justified default for positional motion (and for the editor's own UI transitions), while stagger is an aesthetic choice to offer, not a comprehension aid to push. No controlled study compares spring and Bézier easing for perceived naturalness; Framer's claim that springs "feel more natural than linear or bezier-based easing" cites no data ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/dictionary/spring-animation)). On frame rate, a Dolby judder study reported second-hand found perceived judder rising with brightness and object speed at 30 fps, with differences that "diminish significantly at 60 fps and are lower still for 120 fps" ([Display Daily](https://displaydaily.com/?p=60403)), which argues for rendering previews at the display's native rate and expecting bright backgrounds to expose cadence problems first.

For curve editing itself, the practical descendants of Witkin and Kass's spacetime constraints (an offline "constrained optimization problem" over physics ([Witkin & Kass 1988](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/spacetime-constraints))) are small least-squares problems that could run in a worker at UI rate. Ciccone, Öztireli and Sumner's tangent-space optimisation poses "interpolation control under positional constraints" as an optimisation "in the tangent space of the animation curves", explicitly "does not add new keyframes that might conflict with an artist's preferred keyframe style", and "works in the space of artist editable animation curves" ([Tangent-Space Optimization 2019](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Tangent-Space-Optimization-of-Controls-for-Character-Animation.pdf)); in 2-D the variables are the in/out handles of the affected curves, the constraint is "the layer is at the dragged point at frame f", and the objective is minimal tangent change plus smoothness, which gives a "drag the motion trail at any frame" feature that preserves the dope sheet. Motion warping adds a sparse "smooth deformation that preserves the fine structure of the original motion" as an offset curve ([Motion Warping 1995](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/motion-warping)), the right primitive for an adjustment layer over recorded or dense keys. DirectPaint's motivation, that conventional tools make "temporal control ... manual and performed separately from the painting process", forcing "a frequent mental shift between the two dimensions" ([DirectPaint 2013](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fchevali/resources/projects/directpaint/directpaint-CHI2013.pdf)), is the research version of the Separate-Dimensions complaint, and Maya's trail timing beads are the shipped answer. A 2026 preprint, bbsolver, accepts or rejects "key timing and path geometry together, under one sampled error budget" and keeps vertex counts uniform because After Effects "can't replay vertices appearing or disappearing as ordinary path keys", adding only 1.02×–1.15× wall-clock for verification ([bbsolver](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09741)); that is a template for a "simplify animation" command that also keeps GPU tessellation caches stable. The 2026 CHI paper Notational Animating analysed 135 real animation sketches into 23 notation types and pairs generated motion with "dynamically generated sliders", "an auto-constructed timeline for timing edits" and onion skins, scoring 5.8/7 on ease of expressing intent with seven professionals ([Notational Animating](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06880v1)); together with Keyframer and LogoMotion, the consistent lesson is that any generated motion must be exposed through direct-edit widgets bound to the underlying representation. Little of this classic work ships as code (Gemini's BSD-3 transition recommender and the MIT-licensed Apparatus editor are the readable exceptions ([Gemini](https://github.com/uwdata/gemini); [Apparatus](https://github.com/cdglabs/apparatus))), but the algorithms above are fully specified in the papers and are each a few hundred lines on top of a pure-function evaluator.

## Conclusion

The invariant the team already holds is not a constraint to work around but the organising principle of the whole design: two evaluator families, a time-clamped cubic root-solve and a closed-form damped oscillator, plus a small set of time-remap operators (hold, cycle, offset, ping-pong, stagger delay, slow-in/slow-out warp) cover every interpolation, ease, spring, loop and procedural behaviour in the survey, and anything outside them (follow-through simulations, Rive-style state machines, recorded performances, AI output) is admitted only through a "bake to keys" command whose source parameters are retained so the bake can be re-derived. That same discipline is what makes the hard UX features cheap: segment-owned eases make presets, copy-paste and copy-as-CSS trivial; a fixed-sample arc-length table makes Dragimation-style path dragging a projection problem; frame-exact rational keys make whole-frame snapping lossless where float-time tools accumulate sub-frame drift. The plug-in ecosystems around After Effects and Cavalry are, read this way, a product-requirements document someone else paid to write.

Three places will require original engineering rather than copying, and each should be guarded by the out-of-order frame-diff harness rather than assumed: no published web runtime evaluates at rational time, so clock-to-frame mapping and the preview/export cadence toggle are yours to prove; Adobe does not document its roving-keyframe solver or any GPU memory budget exists in browsers, so roving and RAM preview need measurement-driven designs; and no controlled study compares springs to Béziers for perceived quality, so the spring-versus-ease default should be decided by the team's own preference testing, not by vendor claims. What the evidence does settle is that overshoot-free auto tangents, asymmetric default eases, slow-in/slow-out pacing, popover-first editing with a graph editor behind it, and parameter-emitting AI are not matters of taste; every tool and study that tried the alternatives either reverted or produced worse outcomes.
