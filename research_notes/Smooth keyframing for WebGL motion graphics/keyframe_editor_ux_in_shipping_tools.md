# Keyframe-editor UX in shipping motion-graphics and animation tools

Scope: timeline, graph-editor and viewport interaction patterns for keyframing across After Effects, Cavalry, Rive, web-native tools (Jitter, Fable, Spline, Framer, Figma, Linearity Move, LottieFiles Creator, Motion Canvas, Theatre.js) and the 3D incumbents (Blender, Maya), with a cross-cutting section and a user-sentiment section at the end. Current as of 2026-10-09.

Method note: most vendor doc domains (helpx.adobe.com, rive.app, cavalry.studio, docs.blender.org, help.figma.com, aescripts.com, lottiefiles.com, theatrejs.com, jitter.video, docs.spline.design, linearity.io, community.adobe.com) were blocked for full-page fetches by the research sandbox's egress proxy. Findings below for those domains come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages (quoted phrases are as returned by the extract), not from a full read of the page. GitHub pages were fetched in full. Where a claim rests on a secondary summary rather than the primary page, that is flagged.

## KQ1. After Effects: graph editor, Easy Ease, Keyframe Velocity, assistants, Flow / EaseCopy, motion paths, Alt-drag scaling, complaints

### Takeaway
AE's keyframing is a two-layer system — a dope-sheet-style Timeline plus a Graph Editor with mutually exclusive Value and Speed views — and almost every friction point users report traces to that split (Position has no value-graph handles until you "Separate Dimensions", which in turn removes viewport motion-path handles). The ecosystem of paid add-ons (Flow, EaseCopy, Dojo Ease, Ease and Wizz) exists to fill three native gaps: a CSS-style cubic-bezier popover with presets, copy/paste of eases independent of values, and numeric multi-key ease entry.

### Cited Findings

**Graph editor: value vs speed graph (what the user does / what happens)**
- The Graph Editor is opened with the graph icon "to the right of motion blur" at the top of the Timeline or with Shift+F3; School of Motion describes the Speed Graph as "A visual representation of the speed of your movements (out of a possible 100)" and the value graph as the actual property value, noting "The difference between the speed graph and the value graph can be pretty confusing to animators who are new to motion design." — [School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/graph-editor-after-effects)
- In the value graph the vertical axis is the property value; in the speed graph "the value on the vertical axis indicates speed, not position." The graph type is chosen from the "Choose graph type and options" button at the bottom of the panel. — [MT Mograph guide](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/how-to-use-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects)
- Speed-graph edits change timing without moving keyframes: "the speed graph modifies only the velocity of your animation without altering keyframe values or total duration," and "unlike the value graph, the speed graph simplifies easing adjustments by not requiring separation of dimensions." — [AEJuice speed graph guide](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-use-the-speed-graph-in-after-effects)
- Adobe's own doc: to increase a keyframe's influence "drag the direction handle away from the center of the keyframe"; direction handles can be used to create a bounce-like acceleration where the speed graph "rises quickly and peaks." — [Adobe Help: Speed between keyframes](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/speed-between-keyframes/speed.html)
- Adobe staff and community experts on which graph to use: "The Speed Graph and Value Graph are both incredibly powerful and equally useful"; an expert: "Use the speed graph to adjust acceleration between values. Edit the Value graph to adjust the values," and "For Position, the speed graph is almost always the best choice." A bouncing-ball example shows why: with separated dimensions "you get two rising acceleration graphs that are split by a vertical cliff when the ball hits the ground." — [Adobe Community: why do my graphs look like this](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/why-do-my-graphs-look-like-this/m-p/15481046); [Adobe Community: upside-down curve](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/upside-down-curve-in-graph-editor/m-p/13940904)
- School of Motion's workflow for editing position speed in the value graph is to right-click the keyframe and choose Separate Dimensions, which splits X and Y into their own curves — and the instructor notes this "messed up our easy ease." — [School of Motion: Intro to the Graph Editor](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/tutorials/intro-to-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects/)

**Easy Ease, Keyframe Velocity dialog, Auto Bezier**
- Influence can be set "by stretching or contracting the handles, or using the automatic 33% easy ease shortcuts, or numerically in the keyframe velocity window." Speed (handle up/down) and influence (handle length) are independent; "changing one influence handle does not affect another influence handle." — [Adobe Community: keyframe velocity, speed and influence](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/keyframe-velocity-speed-and-influence-question/m-p/9624203/highlight/true)
- The Keyframe Velocity dialog is opened by Option/Alt + double-clicking a keyframe outside the Graph Editor; before dedicated scripts existed "a user would easy ease the keyframes and then open up keyframe velocity and type in the values wanted." — [Motion Array tutorial](https://motionarray.com/tutorials/after-effects-tutorials/how-to/how-to-easily-create-better-animations-in-adobe-after-effects)
- "Auto Bezier will give half the ease of Easy Ease." — [Creative COW thread: easy ease adjusting softness](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/easy-ease-adjusting-softness/)
- Switching linear keyframes to Auto Bezier "automatically averages the incoming and outgoing speeds at the keyframe, so the layer doesn't seem to slow down as it passes through." — [MT Mograph guide](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/how-to-use-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects) (surfaced in the search extract for this cluster of tutorial pages)
- Dojo Ease (third-party) lets users "manually enter in speed and influence values across multiple keyframes at once." — [Dojo Ease product page](https://creativedojo.gumroad.com/l/dojo-ease)
- A Motion Array tutorial argues the default Easy Ease "is a weak starting point and that custom graph shaping produces better motion." — [Motion Array: create better eases](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/create-better-eases-in-after-effects/)

**Keyframe assistants and roving keyframes**
- Time-Reverse Keyframes lives at Animation > Keyframe Assistant > Time-Reverse Keyframes; keyframes across multiple layers/properties can be reversed "but each set of keyframes for a property is reversed only within its original time range," and "Markers in the Timeline panel are not reversed." Time-Reverse Layer (Ctrl+Alt+R / Cmd+Opt+R) flips a layer's keyframe order. — [Adobe Help: Time-stretching and time-remapping](https://helpx.adobe.com/se/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/time-stretching-and-time-remapping/time-stretching-time-remapping.html)
- Exponential Scale is a keyframe assistant whose results are a frequent subject of remapping tutorials; the same source warns that after a layer is reversed "keyframes will appear to be off by one frame in the timeline" and recommends reversing in a precomp. — [ProVideo Coalition: Hidden Gems ch. 28](https://www.provideocoalition.com/cmg_hidden_gems_chapter_28_frame_rate_manipulation/)
- Roving keyframes: select keyframes, right-click, choose Rove Across Time; "Only the first and last of the selected keyframes are bound to a specific time. All of the keyframes in between automatically slide (rove) in time as needed to create smooth speed changes," without changing the Position path. — [ProVideo Coalition: Roving keyframes](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after-effects-hidden-gems-weekly-roving-keyframes/)

**Alt/Option-drag keyframe scaling (retiming a group)**
- Steps: select the keyframes, hold Alt (Win)/Option (Mac), grab the edge-most keyframe and drag; "the middle keyframes will automatically adjust the required ratio to fit the new duration"; dragging inward speeds up, outward slows down. — [AEJuice: speed up / slow down animations](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-speed-up-and-slow-down-animations-in-after-effects)
- Adobe's community manager notes Alt/Option-drag cannot be used to duplicate keyframes because it "is currently occupied by the time-stretch keyframes behavior." — [Adobe Community: Option-drag to duplicate keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/option-drag-to-duplicate-keyframes-and-layers/m-p/13231142)
- Complaint: "time stretching almost always leads to subframe positioning of keyframes"; a feature request asks for a Shift modifier to quantize to whole frames. — [Adobe Community idea: quantize keyframes when time-stretching](https://community.adobe.com:443/t5/after-effects-ideas/option-to-quantize-keyframes-when-time-stretching-with-alt-key/idc-p/14067207)

**Motion-path editing in the viewport and spatial interpolation**
- "When you create spatial changes in a layer, After Effects uses Auto Bezier as the default spatial interpolation"; this default can be switched to Linear in preferences, and "Bezier spatial interpolation is ideal for drawing a motion path that follows a complex shape, such as a map route or the outline of a logo." — [Adobe Help: Keyframe interpolation](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-keyframes/keyframe-interpolation.html)
- Converting an existing straight path: select keyframes, right-click, Keyframe Interpolation, change Spatial Interpolation from Linear to Bezier (shortcut Alt/Option+Ctrl/Cmd+K). If handles still don't appear, hover the Pen tool over a position keyframe in the Comp panel so it becomes the Convert Vertex tool and "drag out a bezier control handle"; hold Alt/Option if it doesn't switch. A user noted this "makes the bezier handles appear on both sides instead of on the single side as you want." — [Adobe Community: can't find the bezier handles](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/can-t-find-the-bezier-handles/m-p/14002758); [Sessions.edu: Motion Paths and Interpolation](https://documents.sessions.edu/eforms/courseware/coursedocuments/after_effects/lesson2.html)
- "Any Position animation change is nearly impossible to control if you are animating the value graph because that changes the motion path. Motion paths are best edited in the Comp panel using the pen tool." — [Adobe Community: problem with handles in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/please-help-problem-with-handles-in-graph-editor/m-p/11509517)

**Separate Dimensions: the central structural complaint**
- "there is no tangent handles for position until i do separate dimension" and the value graph "was 90% useless." — [Adobe Community: separate dimensions issues](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/separate-dimensions-issues-cc-2017-standard-things-33166)
- "If you separate dimensions you can have access to bezzier controls (speed is much more useful than value when editing position). The problem with separating dimensions is that you loose the ability to edit a curve in the Comp panel." Separating removes the Bezier handle from the Comp panel "because you need X and Y to generate a curve by pulling on a handle. It is nearly impossible to accurately edit a motion path using the Graph Editor/Value graph, but it is easy in the Comp or Layer panel." Recommended order: shape the path first, then separate dimensions to retime. — [Adobe Community: only affect X value in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/only-affect-x-value-in-graph-editor-not-y-or-vice-versa/td-p/13635386)
- 2023: "I find it really hard editing the 'scale' curves in the graph editor due to not being able to separate dimensions"; an expert states position "is the only property that allows separate dimensions." — [Adobe Community: problems with scale in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/problems-with-scale-in-graph-editor/m-p/13808251/highlight/true)
- A thread literally titled "graph editor is garbage" exists on Adobe's forum; one response suggests the Maya-like behaviour the poster wanted "could be reproduced by enabling weighted tangents." — [Adobe Community: graph editor is garbage](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/graph-editor-is-garbage-36841)

**Other recurring graph-editor complaints**
- Keyframes get deselected when pressing the Graph Editor button, forcing reselection. — [Adobe Community: selection not going to graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effects-selected-keyframes-selection-not-going-to-graph-editor/m-p/10764725/highlight/true)
- Framing keyframes in the Graph Editor also reframes the Timeline, so users "have to readjust framing when switching back"; handles stay flat if the user is in the wrong graph type; time-remapped layers block keyframe selection unless precomposed. — [Adobe Community: graph editor stuck with multiple keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/graph-editor-stuck-with-multiple-keyframes/td-p/11895162); [Adobe Community: can't move/edit keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/i-can-t-move-edit-my-keyframes-my-graphs-move-out-of-position-when-i-edit-them-as-well/m-p/15143234)
- In the AE 2023 beta, dragging a speed handle did not visibly update the speed graph until switching to the value graph: "It's a little frustrating to not be able to see the change in the speed graph." — [Adobe Community: speed curve no longer responds](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/ae-beta-2023-0-0-speed-graph-editor-speed-curve-no-longer-responds-to-easing-changes-314154)
- Bezier handles are only available on certain properties; for others the fix is to switch graph type. — [Adobe Community: missing ease-in handle](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/missing-ease-in-function-graph-edit-handle/m-p/8884905/highlight/true)

**Native gap: copying eases**
- "After Effects does not allow copying easing curves across layers directly," so artists rely on reference layers and manual alignment. — [BringYourOwnLaptop course page](https://bringyourownlaptop.com/courses/become-a-motion-graphics-designer-using-after-effects/19-custom-easing-using-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects-after-effects)
- A user question "copying/pasting keyframes but not their easing velocity" and a 2023 Adobe idea titled "Ship EaseCopy plugin standard with AE" both confirm this is a felt gap. — [Adobe Community question](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/copying-pasting-keyframes-but-not-their-easing-velocity-67473); [Adobe Community idea](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-ideas/ship-easecopy-plugin-standard-with-ae/idi-p/13563168)

**Flow (aescripts + aeplugins)**
- Flow "works with the same timing functions as CSS animation, and its values match cubic-bezier (x1, y1, x2, y2) settings"; ships "25 common presets based on Robert Penner's easing functions"; a curve can be applied "as keyframes or as an expression"; "In/Out toggles control whether the curve eases into your keys, out of them, or both"; libraries can be imported/exported to share with a team; installed via ZXP and appears under Window > Extensions > Flow. — [Motion Array: Flow extension review](https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/flow-after-effects-extension-review/)
- Flow was announced in 2016 as something that "will change how you work with AE's graph editor." — [Lester Banks, Sept 2016](https://lesterbanks.com/2016/09/flow-will-change-work-aes-graph-editor/)
- Gotcha: a user's Flow was set to "Apply As Expressions" rather than "Apply As Keyframes"; toggling fixed it. — [Adobe Community: ease](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease/m-p/15119393)
- Negative sentiment: a forum post says "Flow became absolutely unusable due to extreme sluggishness." — [aescripts forum: Flow, page 4](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1340/flow/p4)
- Flow is bundled with other ease tools in the "Take it Easy Bundle"; a 2020-era changelog lists AE 2017–2021 support. — [aescripts: Take it Easy Bundle](https://aescripts.com/take-it-easy-bundle)

**EaseCopy (aescripts)**
- "Copy/paste your eases without overwriting your values and copy/paste your values without overwriting your eases." — [Toolfarm](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=21775)
- It "analyzes your animation and scales your eases appropriately and even flips them for velocities traveling in opposite directions"; "A same number of keys or a multiple of the keys in your clipboard must be selected before pasting"; a "Pass-through" option handles strips of three or more keyframes; the panel can be folded into a single KBAR button; pricing is "Name Your Own Price." — [Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=37577)
- School of Motion lists EaseCopy among "15 paid After Effects tools we love." — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/15-paid-after-effects-tools-we-love)
- Listed compatibility covers AE CS6 through 2024; latest listed version 1.7.3 (Aug 2022). — [aescripts learn tag EaseCopy](https://aescripts.com/learn/tag/EaseCopy/?p=2)

**Navigation and scrubbing**
- J / K: "Go to previous (J) or next (K) visible item in time ruler (keyframe, layer marker, work area beginning or end)." — [Keyboard shortcuts – After Effects](https://computerstudio.uni-ak.ac.at/tutorials/keyboard-shortcuts-after-effects/)
- Since AE 23.0, Shift+J / Shift+K "navigate between keyframes or layer markers on a selected layer, ignoring keyframes and layer markers on unselected layers above and below." — [Adobe Community: Feature focus Shift+J/K](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/feature-focus-new-shift-j-and-shift-k-keyboard-shortcuts-in-after-effects-314162)
- Audio scrubbing: hold Cmd (Mac)/Ctrl (Win) and drag the current-time indicator; keyboard frame-stepping "doesn't preview with audio," and the mouse-driven scrub "is triggered by some sort of internal delay threshold, so it isn't frame-exact." — [Adobe Community: frame-by-frame audio scrubbing](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/frame-by-frame-audio-scrubbing-in-after-effects/td-p/10331943)

### Inferences
- AE's Speed Graph is the de facto "easing popover" of AE: it exists because the Value Graph cannot show per-axis handles for multi-dimensional properties, and users consistently advise it for Position. A new tool that gives every property (including vectors) a single 2D easing curve editable per segment would avoid the entire Separate-Dimensions trap.
- The three best-selling ease add-ons (Flow, EaseCopy, Dojo Ease) each map to a missing primitive: (a) cubic-bezier preset library applied to a key pair, (b) ease as a first-class copyable object independent of value, (c) numeric batch entry of ease parameters. These are cheap to make native.
- Alt-drag scaling is loved but produces sub-frame keys; Blender's "Absolute Time Snap" (KQ5) is the obvious fix.

### Gaps
- Adobe's official Keyframe Velocity dialog page and the exact default influence numbers could not be read directly (helpx.adobe.com blocked); the 33% default is cited from community posts only.
- No full-text access to the "graph editor is garbage" thread; only the title and one reply are confirmed.
- I found no Reddit threads directly; all AE sentiment comes from Adobe Community, Creative COW, tutorial sites and plugin reviews.

## KQ2. Cavalry: keyframes, Magic Easing, Behaviours vs keyframes, reducing the need for the graph editor

### Takeaway
Cavalry pairs a conventional Time Editor/Graph Editor (Linear / Bézier / Step keys) with "Magic Easing" — named mathematical easing functions following the Web Animations model, applied by right-clicking the keybar between two keyframes — and leans heavily on Behaviours (Noise, Oscillator, Stagger, Auto-Animate) so many motions never need keyframes at all. Even so, two community scripts (Easey, Scenery Curves) exist specifically to add a speed graph and a cubic-bezier preset editor, which suggests the native easing UI still leaves a gap for AE-trained users.

### Cited Findings

**Native keyframe model and Graph Editor**
- "The Graph Editor can be used to edit Keyframes and their resulting animation curves. An animation curve is the line drawn between Keyframes to represent the speed in which the Keyframe values change — also known as 'interpolation'. This can be adjusted by manipulating the handles of a Keyframe to create more natural movement using easing." Three interpolation states: Linear ("straight line with uniform change"), Bézier ("curved line with smooth change... ease in and out"), Step (hold). Procedural curves (e.g. a Noise Behaviour connected to position) can be previewed in the Graph Editor. — [Cavalry docs: Graph Editor](https://cavalry.studio/docs/user-interface/menus/window-menu/scene-window/graph-editor/)
- Time Editor interactions: after the first key is set in the Attribute Editor, further keys are set by moving the playhead and scrubbing the value, clicking the keyframe icon in the attribute control, or right-click > Add Keyframe on a key or keybar. "Double click a keyframe and scrub or edit the attribute's value via the pop up control." Multi-select with cmd/ctrl-click or marquee; keys from multiple attributes/Elements move together; right-click > Set Color colours keys; copy/paste is Cmd/Ctrl+C then move playhead and Cmd/Ctrl+V. A "Keybar" is the line drawn between a pair of keyframes. — [Cavalry docs: Time Editor](https://cavalry.studio/docs/user-interface/menus/window-menu/scene-window/time-editor/)
- Shortcuts: Cmd/Ctrl+Option/Alt+arrow jumps to next/previous keyframe; `=` and `-` nudge a keyframe forward/back one frame; `+` and `_` nudge five frames; `[` / `]` set visibility-bar start/end to the current frame; "The Shortcut Manager can be used to customise most shortcuts/hotkeys." — [Cavalry docs: Shortcuts](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tips/shortcuts/)

**Magic Easing (named easing functions as a key-pair property)**
- 1.0.1 (Aug 2020, Beta): "Magic Easing - apply mathematical interpolation between keyframes. Note - we are following the forthcoming Web Animation standard which may be unfamiliar for After Effects users." — [Cavalry 1.0.1 release notes](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/1.0/1-0-1-release-notes/)
- 1.0.2 (Oct 2020): "Magic Easing is now previewed in the Graph Editor. Magic Easing can now also be assigned in the Time Editor by right clicking on the keybar between two keyframes." — [Cavalry 1.0.2 release notes](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/1.0/1-0-2-release-notes/)
- 1.4 (Oct 2022): Time Editor rewritten; "Custom Magic Easing Expressions - Set a custom expression for Magic Easing via the context menu in the Time Editor or Graph Editor." — [Cavalry 1.4 release notes](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/1.4/1-4-0-release-notes/)
- The third-party Curves script says Cavalry's Magic Easing presets include Spring and Bounce, which it cannot convert to cubic-Bézier. — [Scenery Curves manual](https://scenery-vk9r.onrender.com/scripts/curves-42xvUYqCpvY/manual)

**Behaviours instead of keyframes**
- Behaviours are animation "drivers" for values or attributes, "with over 40 available - including Noise, Random, Morph, Bend, Modulate, and more," added by right-clicking an attribute and choosing Add Behaviour. — [School of Motion: Getting Started with Cavalry](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/getting-started-with-cavalry-5-things-every-beginner-should-know)
- "In After Effects, animating 50 circles in sequence usually means keyframes, expressions, or a complex setup. In Cavalry, you place those shapes in a Duplicator, add a Stagger behavior, and the animation is ready." "After Effects is layer-based, while Cavalry is built around relationships and systems." — [School of Motion: Cavalry, the Houdini of 2D](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/cavalry-houdini-of-2d-after-effects)
- Oscillator Behaviour: deforms/affects shapes using trigonometric waves (Sine, square, triangle, sawtooth, custom drawn on a graph); looping via Time Mode, Frequency, Time Scale. — [Cavalry docs: Oscillator](https://cavalry.studio/docs/nodes/behaviours/oscillator/)
- Cavalry 2.0 added an "Auto-Animate behavior for animating shapes with fewer keyframes." — [Renderu: Cavalry 2.0](https://renderu.com/en/news/post/25117); 2.0 release notes list cameras, pre-comp overrides, components, background rendering, tapered strokes — none mention easing. — [Cavalry 2.0 release notes](https://cavalry.studio/docs/tech-info/release-notes/2.0/2-0-0-release-notes/)
- Keyframe Layers "allow you to blend between keyframes or animation curves," e.g. moving from a run cycle to a walk cycle "without having to copy and paste blocks of animation." — [Lester Banks: Keyframe Layers in Cavalry](https://lesterbanks.com/2020/10/working-with-keyframe-layers-in-cavalry/)
- ProVideo Coalition: Cavalry "seems to have avoided the pitfalls of Motion by preserving the efficiency of keyframing workflow and maintaining flexibility in procedural animation, allowing artists to more easily 'tweak' the final results." — [ProVideo Coalition: Is Cavalry the AE killer?](https://www.provideocoalition.com/is-cavalry-the-after-effects-killer/)

**Community scripts that patch the easing UI**
- Easey (Canva Creative Team, MIT): "Open source speed and value graph for Cavalry"; "allows cubic-bezier notation for easing between two keyframes"; save/export presets; "copy duration and easing values to pass along to developers"; hold Ctrl/Cmd while dragging handles to mirror them in the Speed graph. — [sammularczyk/easey on GitHub](https://github.com/sammularczyk/easey)
- The Canvalry repo describes Easey as "The missing speed graph for Cavalry," and says "If you're looking for a more polished value graph editor that supports Magic Easing, check out Curves at Scenery.io." Easey "will not modify any stagger, oscillator or similar procedural elements." — [phillip-motion/Canvalry-scripts](https://github.com/phillip-motion/Canvalry-scripts)
- Scenery Curves (paid, €20): "Apply cubic-Bézier curves to keyframes, save and share your favorites"; "Select at least 2 adjacent keyframes to show the editor and presets tabs"; dragging handles applies immediately to the selected keyframes; Shift keeps handles within 0–1; exact control-point values can be typed; 24 Penner presets; converts Magic Easing presets to cubic-Bézier; menu to copy as CSS cubic-bezier; 1.0.0 released March 2024; a changelog entry fixes "a breaking change introduced in Cavalry 2.5.0." — [Scenery Curves manual](https://scenery-vk9r.onrender.com/scripts/curves-42xvUYqCpvY/manual); [Scenery Curves changelog](https://scenery-vk9r.onrender.com/scripts/curves-42xvUYqCpvY/changelog)

**Positioning / sentiment**
- A 2026 review: "Rather than tweaking individual keyframes on a timeline, Cavalry enables us to define animation logic through nodes and data-driven workflows," with the cost being that it "requires rethinking animation from a procedural mindset." — [SuperRenders: Cavalry review 2026](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/cavalry-motion-design-review-2026)
- A headline reports "Cavalry Pro Is Now Completely Free" (details not verified; see Gaps). — [3DArt.it](https://www.3dart.it/en/cavalry-pro-is-now-completely-free-the-after-effects-alternative-rides-in/)

### Inferences
- Cavalry's "right-click the keybar between two keys" is a cheap, discoverable alternative to a graph editor: the ease is a property of the segment, and the menu offers named functions. That it follows the Web Animations model (and explicitly warns AE users) is a deliberate alignment with how web developers already think.
- The fact that both a free and a paid script re-create a cubic-bezier handle editor with Penner presets — on top of Magic Easing — indicates that named presets alone are not enough; animators also want direct handle manipulation and copy-as-CSS.
- Behaviours replace whole classes of keyframes (oscillation, stagger, noise) with parameters, reducing graph-editor visits; "Keyframe Layers" turn blocks of keys into blendable clips.

### Gaps
- Could not read Cavalry's own Magic Easing docs page (cavalry.studio blocked); the list of built-in Magic Easing presets is only partially known via the Curves script (Spring, Bounce, "Custom").
- No primary confirmation of the "Cavalry Pro is now free" claim or its date.
- No direct user complaints about Cavalry's keyframing surfaced in search (no Reddit/forum threads found).

## KQ3. Rive: timeline, Interpolation panel, curve presets, state-machine blending

### Takeaway
Rive's easing lives in an Interpolation panel docked beside the timeline that appears whenever keys are selected, with four types (Linear, Cubic, Hold, Elastic) plus a numeric field, and an optional Graph Editor with a "Cubic Value" mode that gives AE-style draggable handles. Blending between animations is handled outside the timeline by State Machine blend states (1D and Direct), which the docs warn are additive, not linear.

### Cited Findings

**Interpolation panel**
- "You can set the easing on your keys by either using the Interpolation panel to the right of the Timeline, or by using the Graph Editor, which you can toggle on via the shortcut near the Timeline options." The panel appears when keys are selected; types are switched with icons above the graph. — [Rive docs: Interpolation (Easing)](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/interpolation-easing)
- Linear is the default (constant rate of change). Cubic: two draggable handles; "You can drag the handles as far as you want on the Y-axis. If you drag the handles outside of the graph, the graph view will update to ensure that the handles are always in view"; the default cubic "creates a gentle curve from the first key to the next... the value changing slowly at the start and end, and changing the most in the middle." Hold: "holds the current value until the next key is reached, where the next value is set instantly." Elastic: "overshoots the value and oscillates before settling on it"; Amplitude sets overshoot size, Period sets how fast it settles. - The runtime's cubic interpolator is open source in rive-cpp. — [rive-cpp cubic_interpolator.hpp](https://skia.googlesource.com/external/github.com/rive-app/rive-cpp/+/9ba44a3e868dec6a96cefcb2652cec1a840f2041/include/animation/cubic_interpolator.hpp)

- Modes: "Design Mode is for creating, editing, importing, and rigging objects. - Implicit auto-key: "If a timeline is selected, changes to animatable properties may be keyed automatically"; to avoid it, be in Design Mode or deselect the timeline. — [Rive docs: Animate Mode overview](https://rive.app/docs/editor/animate-mode/animate-mode-overview)
- Key button affordance: "the key button has a grey stroke when a key is not set. If the property has been animated, then the property has a blue stroke. If the property has been animated at the current playhead position, then the key button has a blue fill." - Feature request (status unknown): "Select All Keyframes with Cmd+A... contextual for timeline." — [Rive forum](https://rive.app/community/forums/feature-requests/fsyCzA4A9Yar/select-all-keyframes-with-cmda/ftPG3VErDgz1)

**State-machine blending**
- Three state kinds: single animation, 1D blend, direct blend; "A Blend State is any state that blends together two or more timeline animations," suggested for loading bars, health systems, scrolling, dynamic face rigs. A 1D blend "works by ramping up one animation and ramping down the other as the value changes"; the docs warn the mixing "is not linear, but is additive and could give you unexpected results," recommend keying only a few properties per timeline, and (newer docs) say the input is a view-model property. — [Rive docs: States](https://rive.app/docs/editor/state-machine/states)
- Runtime: the 1D blend binary-searches the ordered animations for the current value and blends the "from"/"to" neighbours; in a Direct blend each animation has its own number input clamped 0–100 and scaled to a 0–1 weight. — [rive-cpp blend_state_1d_instance.cpp](https://skia.googlesource.com/external/github.com/rive-app/rive-cpp/+/64515c6e810931b9656979ea75e72eec71dae46b/src/animation/blend_state_1d_instance.cpp); [rive-cpp blend_state_direct_instance.cpp](https://skia.googlesource.com/external/github.com/rive-app/rive-cpp/+/42deae4ab1874dd3b43e132583599c49311bf8eb/src/animation/blend_state_direct_instance.cpp)
- Rive's positioning: its state machine "uses timelines as states to create interactive graphics that can animate, respond, and transition at any moment." — [Rive blog: Rive vs After Effects](https://www.rive.app/blog/rive-vs-after-effects)

**Sentiment**
- A review aggregating Reddit: "some motion designers on Reddit say Rive can feel awkward when they first switch from After Effects"; "building animation logic with inputs, states, and transitions can feel less obvious than just pressing play"; another thread mentions "bugs and confusing workflows like ViewModels, and a cluttered interface"; G2 reviewers praise "State Machines, timeline blending, and fast previews." — [VijayTalksAI: Rive review](https://vijaytalksai.com/rive-review/)

### Inferences
- Rive's "panel beside the timeline that appears on key selection" is a middle path between AE's full graph editor and a popover: it's always-visible, per-selection, and includes a numeric representation — good for copy/paste by hand.
- Elastic as a first-class interpolation type (amplitude/period) means spring-like motion needs no overshoot keys; this is the same idea as Figma's spring and LottieFiles' Spring Curve.
- The "Cubic Value" mode suggests Rive found that AE users still wanted direct handle manipulation on the curve, not just in the side panel.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether Rive ships a dropdown of named cubic presets (ease-in/out etc.); the docs extract only describes a default cubic and free handles.
- The "Navigate Timeline" shortcut list and any copy/paste-ease or multi-key ease editing behaviour were not retrievable.
- No HN or Reddit thread specifically about Rive's keyframing UX was found.

## KQ4. Newer web and design tools: Jitter, Fable, Spline, Framer, Figma, Linearity Move, LottieFiles Creator, Motion Canvas, Theatre.js

### Takeaway
Web-native tools diverge from AE along three axes: (1) replacing keyframes with relative "operations"/"instructions" (Jitter) or behaviours/presets (Fable, Linearity, Framer); (2) making easing a per-transition popover with cubic-bezier handles + named presets + springs rather than a graph (Figma, Jitter, LottieFiles, Theatre.js); (3) code-first timing with an editor only for scrubbing and dragging time events (Motion Canvas). Several are now converging back toward AE: LottieFiles shipped an "Advanced Graph Editor" that explicitly "mirrors the keyframe curve editing experience of After Effects," and Spline added a keyframe timeline with a graph editor in Sept 2025. Fable shut down in Nov 2024.

### Cited Findings

#### Jitter (browser; operations, not keyframes)
- Jitter pairs "familiar design-app controls with timeline-based animation" and "an elegant timeline-based approach (no complex keyframes)." — [Jitter help: What is Jitter](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/12089209-what-is-jitter)
- Model: "an operation that has one purpose to set an end value"; "Basic instructions — move, scale, rotate, change color, change opacity, etc. — are combined and repackaged to form bigger instructions (e.g. a Slide in is made of Move and Change opacity)"; this is contrasted with "AE or Rive where you have to keyframe the properties of the layers" and likened to Webflow, Protopie, Keynote. The design state "is the resting state for the scene"; timeline rows "represent animations that target layers, not the layers themselves"; trimming visibility is done with In/Out or opacity animations, not trimming. The same operations are used by Jitter's "AI Superagents" so AI output can be handed to a designer. — [Jitter help: Understand operations vs keyframes](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/16415500-understand-operations-vs-keyframes)
- Easing presets with intensity: "built-in easing presets (Slow down, Accelerate, Elastic, Bounce, Overshoot, and more) and customize them with intensity controls"; "a smarter timeline for aligning, staggering, and organizing animations." — [Jitter product page](https://jitter.video/product/)

### Inferences
- The "easing popover with bezier handles + named presets + numeric cubic-bezier field" is now the common denominator across Jitter, Figma, LottieFiles, Theatre.js, Scenery Curves (Cavalry) and Flow (AE). The field being literally `cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2)` matters because it is copy-pasteable to CSS and to developers.
- Three web tools expose springs as parameters (Figma stiffness/damping/mass; LottieFiles tension/friction/mass + clamp; Rive amplitude/period) rather than as keyframes — and none can export a spring to a cubic-bezier, which is a known hand-off problem.
- Tools that removed keyframes (Jitter, Linearity Auto Animate, Fable Behaviors, Framer effects) are praised for approachability and criticised for a low ceiling ("lacks deep timeline control"); tools that started simple are adding graph editors back (LottieFiles, Spline). The market signal is "popover first, graph editor available."
- Motion Canvas's draggable time events are a code-first analogue of retiming: timing offsets live in editor metadata, not in code.

## KQ5. Blender and Maya: graph-editor features worth borrowing

### Takeaway
The 3D incumbents have the most mature "edit many keys at once" tooling: normalised curve display, box/column selection, time-only scaling around the playhead, snapping modes with absolute vs incremental semantics, typed keyframes (Keyframe/Breakdown/Extreme/Jitter/Moving Hold), slider operators that generate in-betweens (Breakdowner, Push/Relax, Blend to Neighbor), lattice/retime deformation of key groups, and viewport feedback via motion trails and ghosting.

### Cited Findings

**Blender Graph Editor**
- Normalize: "Scales the display of each curve so that they all (appear to) occupy the same value range, going from -1 to 1. This can make editing easier when you're working with curves whose value ranges are far apart"; when enabled "the view is zoomed accordingly and the area outside the normalized value range is darkened"; with a preview range "keyframes within the range are normalized, while the others are scaled proportionally"; Auto Normalization will "Automatically recalculate curve normalization on every curve edit." — [Blender 4.2 manual: Graph Editor introduction](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/editors/graph_editor/introduction.html)
- Known normalisation edge cases: easing/dynamic interpolation changed a curve's apparent Y scale with no value change (2.80 report); a fix made normalize respect the preview range. — [Blender issue #67274](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/67274); [Blender PR #106888](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/106888)
- Key menu operators (3.x manual): Easing Type (Ctrl-E); Blend to Neighbor "transitions the current keyframe with the neighboring keyframes," requiring a key on each side, with a Blend factor below 0.5 favouring the left key; Breakdown "moves the selected keyframes to an in between position relative to the adjacent keyframes"; Decimate "simplifies an F-Curve by removing keyframes that influence the curve shape the least" (by Ratio or Allowed Change). — [Blender 3.2 manual: F-Curve editing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/3.2/editors/graph_editor/fcurves/editing.html)

**Blender Dope Sheet and keyframe types**
- Keyframe types: Keyframe (white/yellow diamond), Breakdown (small cyan, "transitions between key poses"), Extreme (large pink), Jitter (tiny green, "filler or baked keyframe for keying on ones"), Moving Hold (dark grey/orange, "adds a small amount of motion around a holding pose"). — [Blender 4.1 manual: Keyframes introduction](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/animation/keyframes/introduction.html)
- The type is a visual sorting aid only: "all it does is change the color. The main intent is to help visual sorting when you are looking at your Dope Sheet." — [Blender 2.6 wiki: Dope Sheet](https://wiki.blender.jp/Doc:2.6/Manual/Animation/Editors/DopeSheet)
- Selection: Box Select (B); Alt-B axis-range "selects the keyframes inside the corresponding time range, even if they're above or below the box"; Circle (C), Lasso (Ctrl-RMB); Columns on Selected Keys (K) selects keys on the same frame as any selected key. Snapping: Snap toggle; Snap To = Frame / Second / Nearest Marker; "Absolute Time Snap" — when disabled "keyframes will move in increments of Snap To," when enabled "keyframes will snap to multiples of Snap To" (removing sub-frame offsets). Slide (Shift-T) stretches one set of keys while compressing the adjacent set so total duration is unchanged. R sets keyframe type (4.2+). — [Blender 4.2 manual: Dope Sheet editing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/editors/dope_sheet/editing.html)
- Scaling in the Dope Sheet "only moves them along the time axis (with the Playhead serving as the pivot point); it doesn't change their values." — [Blender 5.0 manual (ja): Dope Sheet editing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/ja/5.0/editors/dope_sheet/editing.html)

**Blender in-between (tweening) tools**
- Push Pose from Breakdown (Ctrl-E) "interpolates the current pose away from the linear interpolation between the surrounding keys"; Relax Pose to Breakdown (Alt-E) is the opposite — "at full influence, the resulting pose will be identical to a linear interpolation between the surrounding keys"; Pose Breakdowner (Shift-E) "Creates a suitable breakdown pose on the current frame," and while active can be restricted with G/R/S (move/rotate/scale), B (bendy bones), C (custom props), X/Y/Z; Blend to Neighbor (Shift-Alt-E) "transitions the current pose toward the neighbouring keyframes" and needs a key before and after. The dev manual says Push/Relax/Breakdowner work in Pose and Object mode; 3.x listed Breakdowner as LMB-drag and Pose-mode only. — [Blender dev manual: In-Betweens](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/animation/in_betweens.html)
- Interaction: with the playhead between two keys, press Shift+E and move the mouse; "you'll see a little percent and the word 'Breakdown'. The more I go to the right, the more I favor the next key. The more I move my mouse to the left, the more that I favor the previous key." — [Lynda/LinkedIn Learning: Blender timing](https://origin-www.lynda.com/Blender-tutorials/How-effectively-apply-concept-timing/421798/455462-4.html)

- Display Normalized (View menu / toolbar) scales displayed curves to -1..1; View > Renormalize rescales selected curves; "useful when you want to view, compare, or edit animation curves that are related (such as rotation and translation curves), but have drastically different ranges"; "No values are shown in Normalized or Stacked View." — [Maya 2019: Graph Editor View menu](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2019/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-ABB47176-9119-47F2-8466-6FA19C427F9F.htm)
- Retime Tool: double-click in the graph view to place retime markers, then "drag the markers to directly adjust the timing of key movements... making them occur faster or slower, and dragging them to occur earlier or later in time." — [Maya 2024: Graph Editor toolbar](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2024/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-A6061A36-81FF-4F57-A081-C967F749D546.htm)
- Lattice Deform Keys Tool: draw a lattice around a set of keys, then move lattice control points or scale them around a picked point to reshape many keys at once; "the tool can also deform keys that lie along a single graph view axis." — [Maya 2014: Lattice Deform Keys Tool](https://download.autodesk.com/global/docs/maya2014/en_us/files/Lattice_Deform_Keys_Tool.htm)

**Maya viewport feedback**
- Ghosting "simulates a technique in animation where an animator flips through a sequence of drawings to get a feel for the timing"; Visualize > Ghost Selected / Ghost Editor shows the object "for specified frames before and after the current frame," controllable per object, including whole skeletons; combining with Time Editor/Animation Layer ghosts is "unpredictable." — [Maya 2023: Ghosting Editor](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-2656574F-FBC6-457B-B0C1-5C1249DA89EF.htm)
- Motion trails: "you can display and edit the trajectory of an object"; trails work for keyframe, deformer, mocap and expression animation, but editing is only possible for keyframed objects; "Unlike a Motion Trail, which is a representation of keys around your animated model, an Animation Ghost shows you the object rather than a trail." — [Maya 2025: Previewing animation](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Maya-Animation/files/GUID-1EC3357B-62DD-424F-9595-277C373D133C.htm)

### Inferences
- Normalised view is the standard answer to "how do I edit opacity (0–1) and position (0–1920) curves in one graph" — directly relevant to a multi-property graph editor in a mograph tool.
- Blender's keyframe-type tagging (Breakdown/Extreme/etc.) is cheap to implement (it is "just a colour") and gives animators a mental model for which keys are structural vs filler.
- Slider operators (Breakdown %, Blend to Neighbor, Push/Relax) are a "tweening without the graph editor" pattern: they generate an in-between pose from the neighbours with a single drag and a live percentage readout — an interaction with no equivalent in AE.
- Maya's Retime markers and Lattice Deform Keys generalise AE's Alt-drag: non-uniform retiming and value-space warping of a key group, both as direct manipulation in the graph.
- Maya 2025.1's "Time Snap" and Blender's "Absolute Time Snap" both exist because scaling key groups produces sub-frame keys — the same complaint seen in AE.

### Gaps
- Blender 4.x/5.x release notes for Graph Editor changes (e.g. newer handle/snapping behaviour) were not retrievable; most Graph Editor operator details come from 3.x manual pages.
- Maya's Auto Key behaviour and Time Slider bookmarks were not found in results.

## KQ6. Cross-cutting patterns

### Takeaway
Across tools, friction is lowest when: keys are created implicitly from direct manipulation (with a visible "this will key" affordance), easing is a per-segment property editable in a popover/side panel with named presets and a numeric cubic-bezier, eases can be copied independently of values, groups of keys can be retimed by dragging an edge with whole-frame snapping, and the viewport shows the path and the neighbouring frames so the animator rarely needs the graph.

- Per-property easing within a single Figma Smart Animate transition could not be confirmed (sources imply one easing per interaction).

## KQ7. What users say makes keyframing feel "smooth" vs "clunky"

### Takeaway
Praise clusters around: ease presets you can apply in one click and share, direct handle manipulation that updates live, easing that can be copied without touching values, timeline gestures that mirror the canvas, and procedural helpers (behaviours, staggers) that eliminate keys. ### Inferences
- "Smooth" in user language mostly means *fewer modes and fewer hidden prerequisites*: one easing editor that works on every property, handles that always exist, selection/framing preserved across views, and immediate visual feedback on drag.
- "Clunky" is overwhelmingly about state loss and discoverability rather than missing power: AE has every capability but hides them behind modes (graph type, separate dimensions, spatial interpolation preference, Alt modifiers).
- A tool that ships EaseCopy-style ease portability, Flow-style presets, Blender-style whole-frame snapping and Rive-style always-visible key-state affordances natively would address the majority of the sourced complaints.
