# Academic and research-prototype work on making keyframe animation easier, smoother and more direct

Scope note for the report writer: this note covers HCI/graphics research (CHI, UIST, SIGGRAPH/TOG, Graphics Interface, VIS/TVCG, SCA, arXiv) on direct-manipulation keyframing, sketch-based timing, space-time curve editing, motion filtering, novel timeline UIs, motion-perception studies, and open-source code, with the reader being a team building a WebGL2 (browser + Electron) 2D motion-graphics tool. Year of each paper is given inline. Research method caveat: the sandbox's egress proxy blocked nearly every academic host (ACM DL, arXiv, HAL, university sites, Autodesk/Disney Research), so most findings below come from search-engine summaries of the primary pages plus the abstracts those summaries quoted; where a detail is second-hand (a citing paper, a lecture slide, a press article) that is flagged. Exact statistics from inside paper bodies (effect sizes, p-values, participant task times) were mostly not retrievable and are listed under Gaps. The per-session web-search budget was exhausted before a final round of verification searches (spacetime-constraints compute cost, Heer & Robertson experiment numbers, Live Sketch details, K-Sketch source availability, spring-vs-bezier perception); those are explicitly listed as gaps.

## Key Question 1: Direct manipulation of motion over time (drag-to-time, sketch-based timing, Bret Victor, Draco/Kitty/Motion Amplifiers, space-time sketching, K-Sketch, 2020–2026 work)

### Takeaway
The best-validated "direct" timing technique is performance timing: keep the keyframe values, throw away their times, and let the user drag the object along its precomputed motion path while the system records the drag's timing and re-spaces the keyframes (Terra & Metoyer 2004/2007; Dragimation 2012 — 27 participants, significantly better than timeline scrubbing on error, comparable to sketch-timing, and the preferred technique). A second family encodes timing in stroke speed or in reusable "animation-principle" operators (Motion Doodles 2004, K-Sketch 2008, Draco 2014, Kitty 2014, Space-Time Sketching 2015, Motion Amplifiers 2016), and consistently reports that novices produce lively animation in minutes. The 2023–2026 wave (EaseMarks 2023, Keyframer 2024, LogoMotion 2025, Squidgets 2025, Notational Animating 2026) replaces curve editing with sketched notations, strokes-as-widgets, or LLM-generated code paired with direct-edit widgets.

### Cited Findings

**Performance timing / drag-to-time (the most directly adoptable family)**
- Terra & Metoyer, "Performance Timing for Keyframe Animation" (SCA 2004): observed that for novices "setting keyframe values is straightforward while specifying the keyframe timing is difficult and often time consuming"; the method separates keyframe values from keyframe timing — the user performs the timing with a basic 2D device (mouse/pen tablet), the system analyzes the input, maps features of the user's input path to features of the keyframed motion, and distributes the keyframes in time to match the performed path; implemented as a Maya plugin — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/33142f18-9951-441a-a966-9f4b53c68bd8/full)
- A journal follow-up, "A performance-based technique for timing keyframe animations" (Graphical Models, c. 2007), exists; a search summary states its user study of 20+ subjects compared timing accuracy against a standard animation package, but I could not open the paper to confirm numbers — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1524070306000646)
- Walther-Franks, Herrlich, Karrer, Wittenhagen, Schröder-Kroll, Malaka & Borchers, "Dragimation: Direct Manipulation Keyframe Timing for Performance-Based Animation" (Graphics Interface 2012, pp. 101–108): "with traditional keyframe techniques, timing is a tedious and abstract process"; the technique lets an animator time keyframes by dragging the object rather than typing frame numbers or scrubbing; it "draws on direct manipulation techniques for video navigation that leverage the natural sense of timing all of us possess"; the figure caption states "the timing of the dragging motion is recorded and applied to the keyframes" — [RWTH PDF](https://almeida.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/karrer2012b.pdf); [GI proceedings page](https://graphicsinterface.org/?p=5099)
- Dragimation study: 27 participants including professional animators and novices; conditions were timeline scrubbing, sketch-based timing, and Dragimation; result: Dragimation "is comparable regarding objective error measurements to the sketch-based approach and significantly better than scrubbing and is the overall preferred technique" — [RWTH PDF](https://almeida.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/karrer2012b.pdf)
- A later citing paper summarizes that Dragimation "performs better with regard to learnability, ease of use, mental load, and overall preference compared to timeline scrubbing and a sketch-based approach" (secondary account) — [ResearchGate, Walther-Franks dissertation listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330213401_Direct_Animation_Interfaces_an_Interaction_Approach_to_Computer_Animation)
- Walther-Franks' 2014 Bremen dissertation "Direct Animation Interfaces" frames the problem: keyframe animation "specifies dynamics indirectly through abstract descriptions," performance animation is "inflexible because of its high technological overhead"; it proposes "direct animation interfaces" with design guidelines, a surface (multi-touch) performance-animation tool, and the Dragimation timing technique that "shows how concepts from video browsing can be applied to motion editing for more direct and efficient animation timing" (32 MB PDF at the repository) — [Univ. Bremen SUUB](https://media.suub.uni-bremen.de/handle/elib/1548)
- Dragimation's video-navigation ancestor: Dragicevic, Ramos, Bibliowicz, Nowrouzezahrai, Balakrishnan & Singh, "Video Browsing by Direct Manipulation" (CHI 2008) — "relative flow dragging" extracts pixel trajectories from the video and lets the user move an object along its visual trajectory to control playback, "decomposing arbitrary 2D motions into individual pixel trajectories, and allowing scrubbing over these trajectories in a way that accounts for induced motion, such as that produced by dynamic backgrounds"; the paper reports the technique "can out-perform the traditional seeker bar in video browsing tasks that focus on visual content rather than time" — [Toronto DGP PDF](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~bonzo/docs/p237-dragicevic.pdf); [Autodesk Research page](https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/video-browsing-by-direct-manipulation)
- TechCrunch (2008) reported the authors' claim that DimP users could reach a position "at least two times faster" than with a scroll bar (press account, not the paper's statistic) — [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2008/05/22/dimp-a-direct-manipulation-video-player)
- Karrer et al.'s DRAGON (CHI 2008) and its MobileHCI 2009 follow-up are the RWTH systems "where objects in the video can directly be dragged along their movement trajectories, thereby navigating the video timeline" — [Karrer 2008 PDF](https://course.khoury.northeastern.edu/csg170/ssl/Karrer08.pdf); [Karrer 2009 PDF](https://almeida.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/publications/karrer2009a.pdf)
- Kondo & Collins, "DimpVis" (IEEE VIS/TVCG 2014) applies the same idea to data: "visual hint paths" show how a selected item changes over time; dragging the item along its hint path sets the time for the whole view ("Was this bar ever at 500?" is answered by dragging the bar to that height); evaluation vs. a time slider and small multiples found DimpVis "subjectively preferred and quantitatively competitive with the traditional time slider, and significantly faster than small multiples for a variety of tasks" — [UC Davis VIS 2014 PDF](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2003_20tvcg12-kondo-2346250.pdf)

**Bret Victor**
- "Stop Drawing Dead Fish" (SF ACM SIGGRAPH, 16 May 2012; Vimeo recording lists Exploratorium, 27 Nov 2012; ~53 min): argues computer art tools should embrace two kinds of "life," the artist's real-time performance and the art's own simulated behaviour; in the second half "the artist directly manipulates art objects on the canvas" using "direct, geometric construction rather than indirect, algebraic 'code'"; the prototype is an iPad app where animation is built "more akin to puppet theatre than traditional keyframe-and-curves computer animation," with relationships between elements expressed as geometric equivalents of algebraic rules — [Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/64895205); [SF SIGGRAPH announcement](https://web3d.org/pipermail/x3d-public_web3d.org/2012-April/001989.html); [Dynamic Pictures essay with 2024 postscript](https://worrydream.com/DynamicPicturesMotivation/)
- "Inventing on Principle" (CUSEC, 20 Jan 2012): the animation shown (a leaf drops, the view pans across an autumn painting, a rabbit hops away) was made in an iPad app Victor wrote that "allows him to conduct the animation through gesture instead of requiring manual keyframes and tweening functions" (student write-up; the original Vimeo upload was deleted) — [worrydream.com/InventingOnPrinciple](https://worrydream.com/InventingOnPrinciple/); [CMU course write-up](https://cmuems.com/2013/a/julia/08/29/improvisational-animation/index.html)
- Victor's 2011 "Dynamic Pictures" essay argues for pictures that change in response to input, and its 2024 postscript lists "Stop Drawing Dead Fish" and "Drawing Dynamic Visualizations" as the follow-on work — [worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/DynamicPicturesMotivation/)

**Autodesk Research sketch-based animation line (Kazi, Chevalier, Grossman, Fitzmaurice)**
- Draco (CHI 2014; Honorable Mention, Best Video, Best Talk): a "kinetic texture" pairs a small sketched source patch with motion properties at two scales — "global motion controls the overall shape and direction of the collection, while granular motion directs the variations of individual elements"; two texture types: emitting textures (particle-system/flocking inspired; defined by source patch, emitter, sketched global motion paths and granular motion) and oscillating textures (repetitive harmonic motion; defined by source patch, a brush skeleton, an oscillating skeleton and granular motion); user study with professional animators and casual users; participants asked for "finer controls for granular motions, spatially with motion profile like controls" and more than two oscillating skeletons — [LRI PDF](https://www.lri.fr/~anab/teaching/evaluation/papers/draco.pdf); [HAL](https://inria.hal.science/hal-00926847/document); [Autodesk Research](https://www-int.autodesk.com/research/publications/draco)
- Draco shipped: Autodesk released SketchBook Motion for iPad in Sept 2016, which "began life as Project Draco"; features include path-following, rotation, a Particle tool (rain/dust) and a Grow tool for multiplying elements — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=79772); [CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/autodesk-release-sketchbook-motion.html)
- Kitty (UIST 2014): behaviour is stored in a relational graph where "each node is a scene entity and each edge is a function linking parameters between entities"; the graph is displayed in situ over the illustration, and "authors can specify edges and their associated functions through freeform sketching" — the artist draws a path between two objects to create a relation, picks the two parameters in a radial menu, then "directly sketches the mapping function" (e.g., dragging an egg into a pot triggers a splash only when the egg hits the surface); "without requiring any programming or scripting" — [Autodesk PDF](https://www.research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/kitty-sketching-dynamic-and.pdf_recaeoUxJfgubRMCL.pdf); [project page](https://www.autodeskresearch.com/projects/kitty)
- Motion Amplifiers / SKUID (CHI 2016; Kazi, Grossman, Umetani, Fitzmaurice): each amplifier implements one established principle of 2D animation and "deforms an underlying grid that drives the drawn strokes"; amplifiers can be stacked and the result refined; the paper reports people with no animation experience could quickly make expressive animated illustrations — [Autodesk Research page](https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/motion-amplifiers-sketching-dynamic-illustrations-using-the-principles-of-2d-animation); [PDF](https://research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/motion-amplifiers-sketching-dynamic.pdf_recLfzzXycCoes7Un.pdf)
- Motion Amplifiers detail (from search-engine extraction of the PDF): the amplifiers cover seven of the twelve principles — five geometric ones (squash and stretch, arcs, anticipation, follow-through, secondary motion), a sixth for timing (slow-in/slow-out), and a seventh the extraction garbled (verify in PDF); anticipation "pulls the object backwards before the primary action"; follow-through splits the object into rigid, stationary and passive regions, "the passive region catches up with the rigid region," a stiffness slider sets material feel, and parent–child hierarchical follow-through is supported; the bouncing ball "is created by configuring the motion profiles of the squash and stretch amplifier" — [Autodesk PDF](https://research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/motion-amplifiers-sketching-dynamic.pdf_recLfzzXycCoes7Un.pdf)
- The Autodesk patent family "Techniques for generating dynamic illustrations using principles of animation" (US 10,403,020) describes the same mechanism: a sketch is projected onto a grid, the grid is deformed according to control sketches, then the drawing is interpolated into frames — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10403020)
- Xing, Wei, Shiratori & Yatani, "Autocomplete Hand-drawn Animations" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2015): as the user draws frame-by-frame, the system predicts the next strokes spatially and across frames and offers beautification; a preliminary user study reported improved objective performance and subjective satisfaction — [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=210355)

**Sketch/stroke-encoded timing for characters (transferable ideas)**
- Thorne, Burke & van de Panne, "Motion Doodles" (SIGGRAPH 2004): after sketching a character, the user draws "a continuous sequence of lines, arcs, and loops"; the system parses the strokes and "maps them to a parameterized set of output motions that further reflect the location and timing of the input sketch"; 18 motion types in 2D; the 2003 tech report describes a character sketched and animated "within a minute" with limited training — [UBC project page](https://cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2004-TOG-doodles/index.html); [PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/doodle.pdf)
- Guay, Ronfard, Gleicher & Cani, "Space-time Sketching of Character Animation" (SIGGRAPH/TOG 2015): one stroke, the space-time curve (STC), drafts "a full coordinated motion"; "squash and stretch is controlled directly by drawing faster and slower" (pen speed carries timing); from the STC a dynamic line of action (DLOA) is computed that drives the character "through projective constraints"; the motion models are "purely geometric: they require no pre-existing data"; refinement is by "over-sketching strokes along the space-time curve, or by composing another DLOA on top"; a loop-shaped stroke "blends between the key frames at the side and a path-following DLOA in the middle section of the stroke" — [UW Graphics page](https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2015/GRGC15a/); [HAL PDF](https://hal.science/hal-01153763/file/SpaceTimeSketching.pdf); [Inria page](https://team.inria.fr/imagine/space-time-sketching-of-character-animation/)
- Choi et al., "SketchiMo" (SIGGRAPH/TOG 2016, KAIST/Weta): sketch input becomes "projective constraints that relate the sketch inputs to the unknown 3D poses"; "sketch targets" are editable motion properties (body lines, trajectories) and "sketch spaces" are view-dependent visualizations that "accentuate, right on the viewport, different aspects of the motion" (world-space joint paths, joint-to-parent relations, coordination, temporal character) — [KAIST PDF](https://vml.kaist.ac.kr/assets/Contents/Publications/International/2016ByungkukChoi_TOG/2016ByungkukChoi_TOG.pdf)
- Su et al., "Live Sketch" (CHI 2018): lets novices "extract motion from video examples and transfer it to a static drawing as dynamic deformation" — [HKUST PDF](https://hongbofu.people.ust.hk/doc/livesketch_CHI2018.pdf)
- Dontcheva, Yngve & Popović, "Layered Acting for Character Animation" (SIGGRAPH 2003): complex motion is built by several sequential performances layered on top of each other; under an hour of training; includes a motion-editing mode that infers a link between features of the animator's movement and the character's and re-applies a re-performed motion — [Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/layered-acting-for-character-animation)

**K-Sketch (demonstration-based 2D animation)**
- Davis, Colwell & Landay, "K-Sketch: A 'Kinetic' Sketch Pad for Novice Animators" (CHI 2008; Berkeley dissertation EECS-2008-171): after analyzing a library of 72 usage scenarios the authors defined 18 primitive animation operations (Translate, Rotate, Scale, ...); users "demonstrate motions in real time to record an animation," a motion path appears when recording, and a suggestive interface resolves ambiguous operations — [Berkeley tech report](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2008/EECS-2008-171.html); [CHI 2008 abstract](https://www.springermedicine.com/doi/10.1145/1357054.1357122)
- K-Sketch lab study vs. PowerPoint: participants "worked about three times faster, needed half the learning time, and reported lower cognitive load" with K-Sketch — [CHI 2008 abstract](https://www.springermedicine.com/doi/10.1145/1357054.1357122)

**2020–2026 work on sketched/notated timing and LLM-assisted keyframes**
- Nguyen (advisor Cesar Torres), "EaseMarks: Using Secondary Sketch Marks to Author and Communicate Motion Interpolation" (UT Arlington MSc thesis, 2023): "Easing curves are tedious to tune since they fundamentally lack the ability to encode spatial information"; inspired by cel-animation timing charts, secondary marks (hatches, loops) drawn beside a motion "denote motion interpolation decisions"; includes a sketching language, a crowdsourced study, and a tool where animators "sketch or select EaseMarks" to shape appearance/position over time — [UTA repository](https://uta-ir.tdl.org/uta-ir/handle/10106/31987?show=full); [MavMatrix](https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/cse_theses/521)
- Shi, Wei, Zhao, Zhao & Kazi, "Notational Animating: An Interactive Approach to Creating and Editing Animation Keyframes" (CHI 2026): analysis of 135 real-world animation sketches (Pinterest + animation texts) found notations are "contextual, ambiguous, and combinational" and produced a taxonomy of 23 notation types (e.g., ANTIC, DRAG, OVERSHOOT labels); the system translates notations into source/path/target motion units, provides "dynamically generated sliders for motion range adjustments, an auto-constructed timeline for timing edits and per-object feedback, and onion-skin overlays"; evaluation with 7 professional animators; "easy to express animation intent" averaged 5.8/7 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06880v1); [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790707)
- Notational Animating's code: JavaScript/Vite frontend + Python 3.12 backend; keyframe images are produced by OpenAI's image-edit API (default gpt-image-2) or Google Gemini, planning by an LLM; no LICENSE file listed — [GitHub](https://github.com/Xinyu-Shi/Notational-Animating)
- Fukusato, Maejima, Igarashi & Yotsukura, "Exploring inbetween charts with trajectory-guided sliders for cutout animation" (Multimedia Tools and Applications 83(15), 2024; research done 2023): given several keyframes the system "constructs trajectory-guided sliders that enable users to directly adjust inbetween values on a screen," with background guides for "on twos" and "slow-in/out"; a study with novice and amateur users "confirmed that the proposed slider is effective for manually constructing the inbetween charts" — [Waseda summary](https://www.wrs.waseda.jp/sp-r/admin/print/number/2023Q-009); [DBLP](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/122/6355.html)
- Kim, Chevalier & Singh, "Squidgets: Sketch-based Widget Design for Scene Manipulation" (UIST 2025, Honorable Mention): "curves resulting from visually abstracting scene elements provide natural handles for the direct manipulation of scene parameters"; a stroke is partially matched against scene curves to select a squidget and control its parameters; users can author custom curve handles bound to attributes; built in Maya; a controlled experiment on 2D translation and deformation tasks plus an informal study — [DGP project page](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/projects/squidgets/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06795v1)
- Tseng, Cheng & Nichols (Apple), "Keyframer" (arXiv Feb 2024; VL/HCC 2024): GPT-4 turns a static SVG plus a text prompt into CSS animation code; users refine via further prompts, direct code editing, property editors, and requested variants; informed by interviews with 9 professionals; study with 13 users; users favoured "decomposed" prompting (one element at a time) over holistic scene prompts, and the paper contributes a taxonomy of semantic prompt types for motion — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06071v2); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11303478/); [GitHub](https://github.com/apple/ml-keyframer)
- Adobe's LogoMotion (CHI 2025): LLM code generation for logo animation with "editing widgets, which are linked to the underlying code," for adjusting motion, grouping and timing; a comparison on 276 animations judged its output more content-aware than an industry-leading tool — [Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/logomotion-visually-grounded-code-synthesis-for-creating-and-editing-animation)
- Rai & Sharma, "Sketch Animation: State-of-the-art Report" (arXiv Oct 2025, rev. Feb 2026): surveys 2001–2025 sketch-animation work across UIST, CHI, TOG; groups methods into keyframe, physics-based, data-driven, motion-capture and deep-learning families; flags computational cost, scalability and interface usability as open problems — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10218v1)
- Cartoonimator (TEI 2025): a paper-based tangible keyframe kit; 9 adult users rated timing operations favourably; has a per-keyframe timestamp option — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3689050.3704955)

### Inferences
- Performance timing (Terra & Metoyer; Dragimation) is the cheapest high-value adoption for a 2D motion-graphics tool: the spatial keyframes already define a path; the engineering is (a) precompute arc-length parameterisation of the interpolated motion path for the selected layer, (b) during a drag, project the cursor onto the path (nearest point, with hysteresis to avoid jumping at self-intersections), (c) record (wall-clock time, path parameter) samples, (d) re-assign each keyframe's time to the wall-clock moment when the drag passed its path parameter, optionally smoothing/filtering the sampled time-warp. This is exactly the "relative flow dragging" idea from video browsing applied to authored paths. All of this runs on the CPU at UI rate and needs no new renderer features.
- Stroke-speed-as-timing (Motion Doodles, Space-Time Sketching, Draco's sketched motion paths) is a natural "draw a path to create keyframes" gesture: sample the stylus at input rate, fit a curve, place keyframes at speed extrema/corners and keep the stroke's time stamps as the keyframe times; Guay's result that pen speed can also drive squash/stretch suggests mapping speed to a secondary property (scale) as an optional "amplifier."
- Motion Amplifiers' grid-deformation approach transfers to 2D mesh/vector layers: implement anticipation, follow-through (rigid/stationary/passive regions with stiffness), arcs and slow-in/out as composable non-destructive operators on top of a base keyframe track rather than baking them into curves; this matches how After-Effects-class tools already layer effects.
- Kitty's in-situ relational graph is a credible alternative to expression fields for "link this property to that one" and would map well onto a node-graph backend exposed through sketched edges.
- The LLM-era systems (Keyframer, LogoMotion, Notational Animating) converge on one design lesson: generated motion must be exposed through direct-edit widgets (sliders, timeline bars, code/property editors) linked bidirectionally to the underlying representation; a WebGL tool could adopt the notation taxonomy and auto-built timeline without adopting image generation.

## Key Question 2: Curve and timing editing (editing many keyframes at once, spacetime constraints, tangent-space optimisation, editable motion paths with timing marks, direct-manipulation video/time navigation)

### Takeaway
Classic spacetime constraints (Witkin & Kass 1988) remains an offline, physically-based optimisation and was made interactive only through reformulations (Cohen 1992's spacetime windows, Liu–Gortler–Cohen 1994 hierarchical wavelets); the practically adoptable descendants for a keyframe tool are (1) Ciccone et al. 2019's tangent-space optimisation, which edits the artist's existing animation-curve tangents so a dragged point on the motion trail is hit at any frame without adding keyframes, (2) 1995's motion warping / displacement mapping, which edits many keyframes at once by adding a sparse smooth offset curve, and (3) editable motion trails with timing beads (Autodesk patent / Maya), which put time handles on the on-canvas path. 2026's bbsolver adds error-bounded joint reduction of key timing and vector-path handles aimed at After Effects.

### Cited Findings

**Spacetime constraints and whether it is practical**
- Witkin & Kass, "Spacetime Constraints" (SIGGRAPH 1988, pp. 159–168): the animator's goals, the character's physical structure and the available physical resources "together with Newton's laws, form a constrained optimization problem"; demonstrated on a Luxo lamp performing coordinated motions — [CMU RI](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/spacetime-constraints); [PDF mirror](https://alecjacobson.com/seminar/witkin-and-kass-1988.pdf)
- Cohen, "Interactive Spacetime Control for Animation" (SIGGRAPH 1992, pp. 293–302) extended the method; Liu, Gortler & Cohen, "Hierarchical Spacetime Control" (SIGGRAPH 1994) reformulated it with wavelets; Ngo & Marks, "Spacetime Constraints Revisited" (SIGGRAPH 1993) is another follow-up — [SIGGRAPH archive (Cohen)](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=120442); [Harvard PDF (Liu et al.)](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~sjg/papers/hsc.pdf); [SIGGRAPH archive (Ngo & Marks)](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118626)

**Tangent-space optimisation (the modern, curve-native version)**
- Ciccone, Öztireli & Sumner, "Tangent-Space Optimization for Interactive Animation Control" (SIGGRAPH/TOG 38(4), 2019, Disney Research/ETH): motivation — "forward kinematics leads to intuitive interpolations [but] does not naturally support positional constraints"; IK "can lead to inferior interpolations, is slow to compute, and does not allow for positional constraints at non-keyframe frames"; the method poses interpolation control under positional constraints as a space-time optimization "in the tangent space of the animation curves driving the controls"; it (1) manipulates positions and orientations over time, (2) "does not add new keyframes that might conflict with an artist's preferred keyframe style," and (3) "works in the space of artist editable animation curves"; CGPress notes it "works with existing animation curves, so it fits into current pipelines" — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3306346.3322938); [Disney Research PDF](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Tangent-Space-Optimization-of-Controls-for-Character-Animation.pdf); [CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/tangent-space-optimisation-of-controls-for-character-animation.html)
- Later VR work ("Smart Motion Trails for Animating in VR") integrated the tangent optimisation so "participants could directly grab the trajectory and adjust it to avoid wall collisions" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367563120_Smart_Motion_Trails_for_Animating_in_VR)
- Ciccone, Guay & Sumner, "Flow Curves: an Intuitive Interface for Coherent Scene Deformation" (CGF 35(7), Pacific Graphics 2016): a sketched flow curve deforms several disconnected objects into one smooth "subjective curve"; comprises a model of the principle of flow, algorithms to find subjective-curve elements spanning objects, and a view-dependent deformation representation; the authors report sketching flow curves "takes much less time than traditional 3D editing workflows" — [Disney Research page](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/flow-curves-an-intuitive-interface-for-coherent-scene-deformation/)

**Editing many keyframes at once (warping, displacement maps, multiresolution)**
- Witkin & Popović, "Motion Warping" (SIGGRAPH 1995): "a simple technique for editing captured or keyframed animation based on warping of the motion parameter curves"; "the animator interactively defines a set of keyframe-like constraints which are used to derive a smooth deformation that preserves the fine structure of the original motion"; clips are combined by overlapping and blending parameter curves — [CMU RI](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/motion-warping)
- Bruderlin & Williams, "Motion Signal Processing" (SIGGRAPH 1995): toolset of "multiresolution motion filtering, multitarget motion interpolation with dynamic time-warping, waveshaping and motion displacement mapping"; displacement mapping "permits editing of densely-sampled motion data with the ease of keyframing"; positioned as "complementary to keyframing, motion capture, and procedural animation" — [PDF mirror](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/alexandre.meyer/teaching/master_charanim/papers_pdf/bruderlin95motionSignalProcessing.pdf); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118724)
- A later motion-adaptation paper notes that "a variant of ... motion-displacement mapping, was simultaneously introduced as 'motion warping' by Witkin and Popovic" — [SIGGRAPH archive (Motion Warping)](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118725)

**Editable motion paths with timing marks**
- Autodesk patent US 9,305,386 "Editable motion trajectories": a trail on which keyframes can be dragged, synced to 2D Bézier curves, with "in" and "out" tangent handles tied to a keyframe on the trail — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9305386)
- Maya's motion trails (product embodiment): a visual representation of keys around animated models "letting you adjust timing, keys, tangents, and position directly," with "timing beads" that "control the ease-in and ease-out of animations at each keyframe" — [Maya 2016 help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2016/ENU/Maya/files/GUID-2F2ADC71-0DC5-4F2F-96C8-0B2F061F0703.htm); [Modify keys on a motion trail](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2016/ENU/Maya/files/GUID-FC9AE83B-11C9-45C2-A364-AFCDF23BD125.htm)
- Santosa, Chevalier, Balakrishnan & Singh, "Direct Space-Time Trajectory Control for Visual Media Editing" (CHI 2013, pp. 1149–1158): explores "the design space for using object motion paths to make and modify visual content over time as well as across the screen"; pen-based techniques use pressure and multi-touch for "stylizing, annotating, and editing video, slide presentations, and 2D animation"; motivation: in conventional tools "temporal control is manual and performed separately from the painting process," forcing "a frequent mental shift between the two dimensions"; embodied in DirectPaint (freehand painting over video), which was evaluated; a later paper describes it as using the trajectory "to propagate temporal changes" — [Toronto PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fchevali/resources/projects/directpaint/directpaint-CHI2013.pdf); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2470654.2466148)
- A JZUS-C 2011 paper edits rigid-object rotation by letting the user "edit its angular velocity trajectory by sketching curves," then solving "a variational curve fitting problem" for the altered rotation given keyframe orientations — [Unpaywall record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1631%2Fjzus.C1000373)

**Timeline-less navigation (dragging the object to scrub)**
- See Key Question 1 for Video Browsing by Direct Manipulation (2008), DRAGON (2008/2009) and DimpVis (2014); DimpVis was "quantitatively competitive with the traditional time slider, and significantly faster than small multiples" — [UC Davis PDF](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2003_20tvcg12-kondo-2346250.pdf)

**Key timing + path simplification (2026)**
- "bbsolver: A Unified Error-Bounded Spatiotemporal Optimization Solver for Key Timing and Topology-Consistent Vector Paths" (arXiv 2606.09741, 2026): instead of running temporal key reduction and spatial path simplification as separate passes, it "accepts or rejects key timing and path geometry together, under one sampled error budget"; for stable-topology paths it reduces keyframes and path handles jointly; for variable-topology paths it emits a host-compatible stream with uniform vertex counts (because AE — presumably After Effects — "can't replay vertices appearing or disappearing as ordinary path keys") plus a diagnostic stream; on a 12,684-sample walk-cycle test a post-solve verification pass added ~0.4–2.7 min (1.02×–1.15× wall-clock) across tolerances ε = 0.05, 1.0, 3.0 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09741)

### Inferences
- Full spacetime constraints (physics + nonlinear optimisation over the whole clip) is still not a realistic interactive feature for a 2D motion-graphics tool; the pragmatic descendants are the tangent-space formulation (optimise Bézier tangent handles of existing curves to satisfy on-canvas constraints at arbitrary frames) and motion-warping (add a sparse displacement curve). Both are small least-squares problems per edit and could run in a Web Worker at UI rate; the tangent-space method is especially attractive because it never adds keyframes, so it preserves the artist's dope sheet.
- A 2D implementation sketch of tangent-space optimisation: variables = the in/out tangent handles (and optionally key values) of each affected property curve; constraint = position of the layer at frame f equals the dragged point; objective = minimal change to tangents plus smoothness; solve with Gauss–Newton / least squares; update the on-canvas motion trail live.
- "Timing beads" on motion trails (Maya) plus DirectPaint's finding that separating spatial and temporal editing forces costly mental shifts argue for putting time handles directly on the canvas path (drag a bead along the path to retime; pull beads apart to ease), which combines naturally with Dragimation-style performance retiming.
- bbsolver's framing (one error budget for time and space) is a template for a "simplify animation" command that keeps WebGL path topology stable, which matters for GPU-side tessellation caching.

### Gaps
- Witkin & Kass computation times and Cohen's "spacetime windows" details could not be verified (search budget exhausted before that query ran).
- Tangent-space optimisation's solver details, interactive timing numbers and whether it was evaluated with users are unknown (Disney/ETH PDFs blocked).
- DirectPaint's exact pressure-to-time mapping and its study results were not retrievable.
- bbsolver's full method, authorship and venue were not visible beyond the search snippet.

## Key Question 3: Smoothing and filtering of keyframe curves (cartoon animation filter, motion smoothing with key-pose preservation, curve simplification/fitting)

### Takeaway
The Cartoon Animation Filter (Wang, Drucker, Agrawala & Cohen, SIGGRAPH 2006) is a one-parameter, signal-domain operator — subtract a Laplacian-of-Gaussian-smoothed, sign-flipped, optionally time-shifted second derivative from any motion signal — that adds anticipation, follow-through, exaggeration and squash-and-stretch; it has a direct companion (White, Loken & van de Panne 2006) that time-warps motion to concentrate it around keyframes (slow-in/slow-out) with duration-preserving or duration-extending modes. For reducing noise and keyframe count, the literature uses dynamic-programming Hermite/curve fitting or error-threshold simplification (2006–2008), with Roberts et al. 2019 reporting ~10% of original frames retained.

### Cited Findings
- Wang, Drucker, Agrawala & Cohen, "The Cartoon Animation Filter" (ACM TOG 25(3), SIGGRAPH 2006, pp. 1169–1173): "a simple filter that takes an arbitrary input motion signal and modulates it in such a way that the output motion is more 'alive' or 'animated'"; it "adds a smoothed, inverted, and (sometimes) time shifted version of the second derivative (the acceleration) of the signal back into the original signal"; "almost all parameters of the filter are automated. The user only needs to set the desired strength of the filter"; the output exhibits "anticipation, follow-through, exaggeration and squash-and-stretch effects"; tested on "hand drawn trajectories, ... simple animations within PowerPoint presentations, ... motion captured DOF curves, ... video segmentation results" — [UW GRAIL PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/wang-2006-tca.pdf); [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=157386)
- Follow-up papers write the filter as f̃(t) = f(t) − f(t) ⊗ LoG, i.e., input minus its convolution with a Laplacian-of-Gaussian kernel, which "can provide anticipation and follow-and-through effects"; the squash-and-stretch extension "works by differentially time-shifting the same LoG filter for the appropriate points on the boundary of the object," and a 2011 paper applies per-joint time shifts so each joint stretches independently — [Squash-and-stretch filter, SIGGRAPH Asia 2009](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1666778.1666789); [Stretchable Cartoon Editing, SIGGRAPH Asia 2011 PDF](http://www.animlife.com/publications/sigasia11a.pdf)
- A 2017 video-magnification paper notes that the LoG "allows us to take an exact derivative of a smoothed discrete signal" and that σ "allows for selecting the observation scale" (second-hand context for the σ parameter) — [arXiv 1704.04186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04186)
- A Chinese JCAD paper applied the LoG filter to motion-capture data for real-time 3D cartoon animation, reporting a more "alive" style — [JCAD](https://jcad.cn/en/article/id/7be177bd-6903-4575-b5ed-466f8bdcba21)
- White, Loken & van de Panne, "Slow In and Slow Out Cartoon Animation Filter" (SIGGRAPH 2006 research poster): takes motion-capture data that "is realistic but can look dull" and adds slow-in/slow-out to stylize it, based on Thomas & Johnston's principle; the tool "concentrates motion around key frames" and has two modes — "one keeps the original duration by speeding up the in-between frames, and the other keeps the in-between timing and produces a longer clip"; the authors note the approach applies beyond mocap "with the key frame selection being the main change"; inspired in part by Wang et al. — [UBC PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2006-siggraph-slowin.pdf); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=139393)
- Bruderlin & Williams (1995) multiresolution motion filtering lets a user re-weight frequency bands of a motion curve (details in Key Question 2) — [PDF mirror](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/alexandre.meyer/teaching/master_charanim/papers_pdf/bruderlin95motionSignalProcessing.pdf)
- Motion Amplifiers (CHI 2016) includes a dedicated timing amplifier for slow-in/slow-out alongside geometric amplifiers, i.e., easing is treated as a stackable operator rather than a per-key curve tweak — [Autodesk PDF](https://research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/motion-amplifiers-sketching-dynamic.pdf_recLfzzXycCoes7Un.pdf)
- "Keyframe reduction techniques for motion capture data" (3DTV-Conference 2008): compares (a) curve fitting with dynamic programming, "which also removes noise," against (b) curve simplification until a keyframe-count threshold; "curve fitting with dynamic programming performs about as well as curve simplification methods," with error depending on motion — [Bilkent repository](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/ca36eda0-4fc1-4b8e-b1f6-a12a9c00d3b4/full)
- "Combined filtering and key-frame reduction of motion capture data" (WSCG 2006 poster; Bilkent thesis): fits a Hermite curve to motion data by dynamic programming "to reduce keyframes and remove jitter at the same time" — [Bilkent thesis](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/1570e349-43fa-4f8a-838f-00c110e574a9); [WSCG record](https://otik.uk.zcu.cz/handle/11025/872?locale=en)
- Roberts, Lewis, Anjyo et al., "Optimal and interactive keyframe selection for motion capture" (Computational Visual Media 5(2), 2019, pp. 171–191): "can simplify motion capture to around 10% of the original number of frames while retaining most of its detail" and "outperforms both research algorithms and a leading commercial tool" (authors' claim) — [SciOpen](https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.1007/s41095-019-0138-z)
- A Bournemouth keyframe-extraction paper treats motion as curves, finds sharp slope changes, clusters them by density and refits segments, claiming lower reconstruction error than existing techniques — [Bournemouth eprints](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/35006/)
- Practitioner reimplementation of the 2008 simplification approach (Nuke, Python): start with a straight line between first and last frames and add keyframes where the curve deviates beyond a perpendicular error threshold (Douglas–Peucker-style) — [Nukepedia](https://www.nukepedia.com/written-tutorials/keyframe-reduction-script-for-nuke)

### Inferences
- The cartoon animation filter is trivially implementable on a sampled property curve in JavaScript/WASM: sample the curve at frame rate, convolve with a 1-D LoG kernel (σ tied to the dominant motion duration; the paper automates this), subtract, and re-fit keyframes — or better, apply it non-destructively as a "liveliness" effect on the evaluated channel so the artist keeps their keys. Per-vertex time shifts of the same kernel give squash-and-stretch on 2D shapes.
- The slow-in/slow-out filter is a time-warp around keyframes: given keyframe times k_i, build a monotone warp t' = w(t) that compresses time near each k_i; this is the same primitive as "ease all selected keys" and could be exposed as a single strength slider, with the duration-preserving mode as default.
- Combined "filter + reduce" (Hermite fit via DP) is the right primitive for turning recorded performances (Dragimation drags, drawn strokes, mocap/mouse recordings) into a sparse, hand-editable keyframe set; the Roberts 2019 ~10% retention figure is a reasonable target.

### Gaps
- Exact LoG kernel normalisation, σ selection rule, time-shift heuristics and the squash-and-stretch extension's formula from the 2006 paper could not be read (host blocked); the equation form above is from citing papers.
- No user study comparing filtered vs. hand-tuned curves for perceived quality was found.
- No research specifically on "smoothing while preserving key poses" for 2D vector keyframes beyond the slow-in/slow-out filter and keyframe-reduction literature was found.

## Key Question 4: Novel timeline UIs (layered/stacked timelines, dope-sheet+curve hybrids, nodes vs keyframes, live coding, programming-by-demonstration, animation by example, staggered/group motion)

### Takeaway
Research prototypes repeatedly replace or augment the dense per-property timeline with: event-only timelines whose ticks are dragged to retime (K-Sketch), relationships drawn in situ on the canvas instead of expressions (Kitty, Apparatus, Para), keyframe "groups" played sequentially/simultaneously with auto-completion over selections (Canis/CAST/CAST+), recommenders that generate staged keyframes from start/end states (Gemini/Gemini²), and auto-constructed timelines plus dynamically generated sliders over LLM/notation-generated motion (Notational Animating, Keyframer, LogoMotion). For group motion, the empirical evidence is that staggering does not help tracking and can hurt (Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri 2014).

### Cited Findings
- K-Sketch's timeline: "Conventional tools show every transformation on each object, whereas K-Sketch has a simplified timeline that shows only the time at which important events occur, highlighting events related to the currently selected object"; performing a motion "adds gray tick marks at its start and end, and users can modify the timing of a motion by selecting and dragging a tick mark along the time bar" — [Berkeley tech report](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2008/EECS-2008-171.html)
- Kitty (UIST 2014) replaces scripting with an in-situ relational graph edited by sketching edges and mapping functions (details in KQ1) — [Autodesk PDF](https://www.research.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2023/03/kitty-sketching-dynamic-and.pdf_recaeoUxJfgubRMCL.pdf)
- Ge, Lee & Wang, "Canis: A High-Level Language for Data-Driven Chart Animations" (EuroVis/CGF 2020): designers "select marks from a chart, partition those marks into mark units based on data attributes, and apply animation effects to the units, controlling when each effect starts"; the compiler "generates Lottie animation JSON files that can render natively across multiple platforms" — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/2f489f52-f081-4d03-a175-dc388880a952/full); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=657309)
- Ge, Lee & Wang, "CAST: Authoring Data-Driven Chart Animations" (CHI 2021): the visual specification "is built from keyframes that can be played sequentially or simultaneously, along with animation parameters such as duration and delay," with auto-completion that builds keyframe groups from a selection — [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/cast-authoring-data-driven-chart-animations/); [CHI 2021 PDF](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2021/01/CAST-CHI2021.pdf)
- Shen et al., "CAST+ (Canis Studio Plus)" (IEEE TVCG / VIS 2025): "enables interactive creation of chart animations through direct manipulation of keyframes," animating multiple mark types in one keyframe group "using new auto-completion algorithms based on generalized selection" — [IEEE VIS 2025](https://aa.ieeevis.org/year/2025/program/paper_d997f9c2-6bfc-421f-bfe6-26e0072cbe76.html)
- Kim & Heer, "Gemini" (UW IDL): "a declarative grammar and recommendation system for animated transitions between single-view statistical graphics"; specifications define transition "steps" over marks, axes, legends; the recommender "enumerates staged animation designs for given start and end states, and ranks those designs using a cost function informed by prior perceptual studies"; 9/11 expert designs exactly replicable, 8/11 reachable via edits to suggestions — [IDL page](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/gemini)
- Gemini² (IEEE VIS 2021 short paper): "complex animated transitions may be easier to understand when divided into separate, consecutive stages, but effective staging requires careful attention to both animation semantics and timing parameters"; given only start and end states it "can automatically recommend intermediate keyframes," using GraphScape to itemize change into semantic edit operations and regroup them into ordered stages; a user study found "recommendation ranking is well aligned with subjects' preferences" — [IDL page](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/gemini2); [IEEE VIS 2021](https://virtual.ieeevis.org/year/2021/paper_v-short-1033.html)
- Gemini's grammar/API (BSD-3-Clause): a `timeline` with `sync` blocks of components each carrying `timing.duration`; `recommend(start, end, {stageN: 2, totalDuration: 2000, ...})` returns designs sorted by effectiveness; `recommendKeyframes` enumerates keyframe paths with scores — [GitHub uwdata/gemini](https://github.com/uwdata/gemini)
- Schachman & Horowitz, Apparatus (2015): "a hybrid graphics editor and programming environment for creating interactive diagrams" that "combines the direct manipulation capabilities of a vector graphics editor with the dataflow capabilities of a spreadsheet"; a numerical solver keeps the visual and code sides linked so either can be edited; MIT-licensed CoffeeScript on GitHub (336 commits); the README/page says nothing about a timeline — [Strange Loop 2015](https://thestrangeloop.com/2015/apparatus-a-hybrid-graphics-editor-slash-programming-environment-for-creating-interactive-diagrams.html); [GitHub cdglabs/apparatus](https://github.com/cdglabs/apparatus)
- Jacobs et al., Para (CHI 2017) "supports the creation of declarative constraints in vector artwork" that "enable procedural relationships while facilitating live manual control and non-linear editing"; Dynamic Brushes lets artists build ad-hoc drawing tools whose behaviours can "respond to temporal events, and leverage external data"; both evaluated in open-ended studies with professional artists — [MIT Media Lab (Para)](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/publications/supporting-expressive-procedural-art-creation-through-direct-manipulation/); [Active Drawing project](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/active-drawing/overview)
- Notational Animating (CHI 2026) provides "an auto-constructed timeline for timing edits and per-object feedback" whose bars let users "directly manipulate temporal order and duration," plus "dynamically generated sliders" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06880v1)
- Keyframer (2024) pairs prompting with a CSS code editor and property editors; users preferred stacking decomposed prompts, and the authors argue "direct editing along with prompting enables iteration beyond one-shot prompting interfaces" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06071v2)
- LogoMotion (CHI 2025) exposes LLM-generated animation through editing widgets "linked to the underlying code" for motion, grouping and timing — [Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/logomotion-visually-grounded-code-synthesis-for-creating-and-editing-animation)
- Motion Doodles (2004) is itself a "cursive motion specification": one continuous stroke of lines, arcs and loops is parsed into a sequence of timed motions, i.e., a timeline drawn as a gesture — [UBC page](https://cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2004-TOG-doodles/index.html)
- Layered Acting (2003) builds motion as successive performance layers, each pass added on top of earlier ones — [Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/layered-acting-for-character-animation)
- Kinetic typography authoring: TextAlive (CHI 2015) is "an integrated environment for making kinetic typography videos" synchronized to music/speech, with a preliminary study across designers, programmers and casual users; Microsoft's Wakey-Wakey (UIST) transfers "the animation scheme of a rigid body on a given meme GIF to text in vector format" with an authoring tool for human control — [TextAlive](https://www.academia.edu/66303359/TextAlive); [Wakey-Wakey](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=978210)
- Group/staggered motion evidence: Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri, "The Not-so-Staggering Effect of Staggered Animated Transitions on Visual Tracking" (IEEE TVCG 20(12), 2014): "introducing staggering has a negligible, or even negative, impact on multiple object tracking performance"; costs are "a loss of common-motion grouping information about which objects travel in similar paths, and less predictability about when any specific object would begin to move"; simulations show staggering can reduce crowding in some dot-cloud transitions but tracking experiments did not confirm a benefit — [HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/CRISTAL-MINT/hal-01054408v1); [Northwestern PDF](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/ChevalierDragicevicFranconeri2014.pdf)
- Cartoonimator (TEI 2025) is a tangible, paper-based keyframe kit with per-keyframe timestamps — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3689050.3704955)

### Inferences
- An "event timeline" view (K-Sketch) that shows only motion start/end ticks for the selected layer, draggable to retime, is cheap to add on top of a conventional dope sheet and matches the way performance-recorded motions are stored (a motion = a span with recorded samples).
- Canis/CAST's "keyframe group + sequential/simultaneous + auto-complete from selection" is a concrete model for staggered/group motion authoring in motion graphics: select N layers, define one keyframe group, choose sequential (with delay) vs simultaneous, and let generalized selection extend the pattern — while heeding the 2014 finding that stagger is an aesthetic choice, not a comprehension aid.
- Gemini's recommender pattern (enumerate staged designs between two states, rank by a perceptual cost) is adoptable for "animate between two layouts" features (e.g., morphing between two arrangements of shapes) and its BSD code can be studied directly.
- Apparatus/Para/Kitty show that a node/constraint backend can be surfaced through on-canvas direct manipulation rather than a node editor; the open question for a timeline-centric tool is how such constraints coexist with keyframes (Para's "live manual control and non-linear editing" claim is the closest evidence that they can).
- Live-coding environments (Hydra, Motion Canvas) surfaced no peer-reviewed studies in this search; the closest evidence base is the LLM-code tools (Keyframer/LogoMotion), which found that code must be mirrored by direct-edit widgets.

### Gaps
- No research paper was found that evaluates a dope-sheet + curve-editor hybrid or a stacked/layered timeline for motion graphics specifically.
- No peer-reviewed study on live-coding animation tools (Hydra/Motion Canvas-class) was found.
- CAST's user-study numbers and whether its code is open source are unverified (the ChartAnimation GitHub org hosts "Canis" and "CAST" repos containing only README + index.html, no LICENSE).
- No research prototype dedicated to authoring staggered group motion in motion graphics was found; evidence comes from data-visualization tools.

## Key Question 5: Perception research on what makes motion look smooth (easing preference, slow-in/slow-out, springs vs Bézier, frame rate / motion blur)

### Takeaway
Controlled studies support slow-in/slow-out: Dragicevic et al. (CHI 2011) found it gave the best object-tracking performance among constant, slow-in/slow-out, fast-in/fast-out and adaptive pacing, because it makes motion endpoints predictable — not for the "naturalness" reasons animators cite; Heer & Robertson (2007) showed animated transitions beat static ones and that simple staging helps; Chevalier et al. (2014) showed staggering does not help tracking; Chalbi et al. (2020) showed that which motion cues produce grouping depends on the data/visualization. No controlled study comparing spring vs. cubic-Bézier easing for perceived naturalness was found; vendor claims exist but cite no data. Frame-rate evidence (Dolby judder study, reported second-hand) shows judder sensitivity dropping sharply from 30 to 60 fps and further at 120 fps, increasing with brightness and object speed.

### Cited Findings
- Dragicevic, Bezerianos, Javed, Elmqvist & Fekete, "Temporal Distortion for Animated Transitions" (CHI 2011, pp. 2009–2018): "One informal design guideline ... has long been the use of slow-in/slow-out pacing, but no empirical data exist to support this practice"; compared constant speed, slow-in/slow-out, fast-in/fast-out and adaptive speed (slows the visually complex parts); slow-in/slow-out "gave the best object-tracking performance, though the effect varied by transition type" — [PDF](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/timedistort.pdf); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1978942.1979233)
- Authors' slides: "Our work confirms animation folklore — Use Slow In/Slow Out for animations — But not for the reason quoted by animators"; the explanation is that frames at endpoints help predict where motion starts and stops while frames at complex segments help track; recommendation: use slow-in/slow-out, otherwise constant speed (secondary source: slides) — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/temporal-distortion-for-animated-transitions-7932420/7932420)
- Heer & Robertson, "Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics" (IEEE TVCG 13(6), InfoVis 2007): two controlled experiments showed "animated transitions can significantly improve graphical perception"; introduced a transition taxonomy and the DynaVis system; design principles grouped under congruence (keep graphics valid, marks represent the same data tuple) and apprehension (keep transitions simple, group similar ones, use staging for complex changes) — [IDL page](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions); [Berkeley PDF](https://vis.berkeley.edu/papers/animated_transitions/2007-AnimatedTransitions-InfoVis.pdf)
- Lecture summary of Heer & Robertson: "animated transitions are better than static transitions for estimating changing values"; "simple staging was preferred and showed benefits but timing important and in need of study"; "axis rescaling hampers perception" (secondary: UW CSE442 slides) — [CSE442 slides](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse442/18au/lectures/CSE442-Animation.pdf)
- FlowingData reported the DynaVis experiment had 26 users and that "the most convincing of the results was probably the measure of memory" (press account) — [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2008/02/20/is-an-animated-transition-from-a-scatter-plot-to-a-bar-graph-effective/)
- Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri (TVCG 2014): staggering has "negligible, or even negative" impact on multiple-object tracking (full quotes in KQ4) — [HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/CRISTAL-MINT/hal-01054408v1)
- Chalbi et al., "Common Fate for Animated Transitions in Visualization" (IEEE TVCG 2020; arXiv 1908.00661): tested how dynamic visual factors create perceived grouping; "the relative strength of these factors isn't fixed. It shifts with the visualization's characteristics and the underlying data"; validated with a Gapminder-style scatterplot and an election map; derives design guidance for animated transitions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.00661)
- Binucci, Förster, Katheder & Tappini, "Evaluating Animation Parameters for Morphing Edge Drawings" (Graph Drawing 2023; arXiv 2309.00456): varied animation speed and easing ("an easing technique commonly used in web design"); "fast animation and easing can help users in performing topology-based tasks," and "while participants appreciated the easing technique, they preferred a slower speed" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00456v2)
- Huhtala et al., "Animated UI transitions and perception of time" (CHI 2010, VTT): "bringing up the next image earlier dominates the perception of a fast transition over other variables examined" — [VTT](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/animated-ui-transitions-and-perception-of-time-a-user-study-on-an/)
- Merz, Tuch & Opwis (CHI 2016 LBW): pilot plus planned 300-participant study rating transition styles on "perceived pragmatic and hedonic quality"; results not visible in the record — [Basel edoc](https://edoc.unibas.ch/43792)
- Aalto master's thesis on web transitions: "most participants did not pay attention to animated transitions ... all of them preferred a user interface with animations over a user interface with no animations"; author states the findings "do not provide a solid ground for general conclusions regarding the duration and playback style" — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/6c78128d-ceb2-47d5-94d7-9bceae447a9d)
- Springs vs Bézier: Framer's glossary claims spring animations "feel more natural than linear or bezier-based easing" with no cited data; a design wiki frames it as a role heuristic (springs when motion must stay attached to the user, survive interruption or preserve velocity; easing when the system is announcing a change) — vendor/practitioner opinion, not evidence — [Framer dictionary](https://www.framer.com/dictionary/spring-animation); [userinterface.wiki](https://www.mintlify.com/raphaelsalaja/userinterface-wiki/animation/to-spring-or-not-to-spring)
- Frame rate/judder: a Dolby study (Alex Chapiro) on a 120 Hz LG OLED had viewers rate judder 0–9 across 30/60/120 fps, speed, luminance and contrast; "the clear trend of increases in perceived judder with brightness and object movement is evident for the 30 fps case. Difference diminish significantly at 60 fps and are lower still for 120 fps"; brighter HDR content makes judder more objectionable (trade-press summary of a company presentation) — [Display Daily](https://displaydaily.com/?p=60403)
- Sample-and-hold blur: on hold-type displays "the eye tracks the moving object continuously ... light intensity is integrated over one frame period," so moving edges blur; Blur Busters argues persistence, not GtG, dominates ("a 60Hz refresh ... is displayed for a whole 1/60th of a second (16.7ms persistence)"), and impulse/strobed displays reduce it (enthusiast/industry sources, not peer-reviewed) — [Cine-tal report (2006)](https://ftp.nluug.nl/av/gwvideotest/MotionPerceptiononLCD.pdf); [Blur Busters FAQ](https://www.blurbusters.com/faq/oled-motion-blur)
- A 2024 arXiv paper "Comparative Evaluation of Animated Scatter Plot Transitions" exists and cites the temporal-distortion work (not read) — [arXiv 2401.04692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04692)

### Inferences
- The perception literature justifies making slow-in/slow-out the default interpolation for positional motion in a motion-graphics tool (and for UI transitions inside the tool itself), while treating stagger as an aesthetic rather than a comprehension aid.
- Dragicevic's "adaptive speed" (slow down where the scene is visually complex) is an implementable auto-easing heuristic: compute a per-frame complexity measure (e.g., number of overlapping/crossing moving objects) and warp time so complex spans get more frames.
- For a WebGL2 tool, the display-side findings imply that rendering at the display's native refresh (60/120 Hz) matters more for perceived smoothness than sub-frame interpolation tricks, and that previews on bright HDR backgrounds will expose judder sooner; synthetic motion blur (which the tool can render) directly counters sample-and-hold blur judgments but no peer-reviewed study quantifying that for authored animation was found.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of spring vs Bézier perceived naturalness was found; this is a genuine gap in the literature as far as this search could tell.
- Dragicevic 2011's exact accuracy numbers, participant count and per-transition-type results were not retrievable (PDF host blocked).
- Heer & Robertson's experiment parameters (participants, tasks, significance) could not be confirmed from the primary paper; secondary sources give 26 users for the DynaVis experiment.
- Peer-reviewed frame-rate / motion-blur perception studies for animated graphics (as opposed to film/video judder) were not found; the Dolby result is a trade-press summary.

## Key Question 6: Open-source research code or demos that could be prototyped on the web

### Takeaway
Few of the classic systems released code; the usable open-source material is (1) Apparatus (MIT, CoffeeScript, browser-based direct-manipulation + dataflow diagrams), (2) Gemini/Gemini² (BSD-3, JavaScript, animated-transition grammar and staging recommender), (3) Apple's Keyframer (JavaScript/Node web app, LICENSE file present but type unverified), (4) Notational Animating (CHI 2026; JS/Vite + Python, no license listed), plus a student reimplementation of Space-Time Sketching and practitioner keyframe-reduction scripts. Most 2000s–2010s prototypes (Dragimation, Draco/Kitty/Motion Amplifiers, DimP, K-Sketch's current source) are not published as code; Draco survives only as the SketchBook Motion product.

### Cited Findings
- Apparatus: MIT license, CoffeeScript (`src/`) + Stylus, 336 commits, "hybrid graphics editor and programming environment for creating interactive diagrams"; no animation/timeline support mentioned — [GitHub cdglabs/apparatus](https://github.com/cdglabs/apparatus)
- Schachman's later browser-based Cuttle and earlier Shadershop are in the same vein (Future of Coding podcast) — [Future of Coding ep. 51](https://futureofcoding.org/episodes/051)
- Gemini / Gemini²: BSD-3-Clause, JavaScript (rollup/babel/jest), grammar + recommender for Vega/Vega-Lite transitions; API `recommend`, `recommendKeyframes`, `animateSequence`; limited to single-view charts with at most one x and one y axis — [GitHub uwdata/gemini](https://github.com/uwdata/gemini)
- Keyframer: `apple/ml-keyframer` — Node/JavaScript web app (components/pages), uses GPT-4 via OpenAI API, `npm install` / `npm run dev`; LICENSE file linked but type not shown on the page; custom SVGs should use named layers because names become element ids — [GitHub apple/ml-keyframer](https://github.com/apple/ml-keyframer)
- Notational Animating: `Xinyu-Shi/Notational-Animating` — Vite/JS frontend, Python 3.12 backend, Playwright tests, Vercel deploy config; image keyframes via OpenAI image-edit or Gemini; no LICENSE file listed — [GitHub](https://github.com/Xinyu-Shi/Notational-Animating)
- Space-Time Sketching reimplementation: `shrekshao/AnimationSketcher` (UPenn CIS 660 project) implements the DLOA approach from Guay et al. 2015 — Visual Studio solution with Maya scenes; no license stated — [GitHub](https://github.com/shrekshao/AnimationSketcher)
- Canis/CAST: the ChartAnimation GitHub org has "Canis" and "CAST" repos (HTML, last updated Feb 2023) each with only README.md and index.html and no LICENSE — effectively a demo page, not source — [GitHub ChartAnimation](https://github.com/chartanimation); [GitHub CAST](https://github.com/chartanimation/CAST)
- Keyframe reduction (practitioner): Nuke Python script implementing error-threshold curve simplification — [Nukepedia](https://www.nukepedia.com/written-tutorials/keyframe-reduction-script-for-nuke)
- K-Sketch: a free download circulated via third-party sites; official maintenance status unverified — [tamindir listing](https://www.tamindir.com/indir/k-sketch/)
- Draco is available commercially (not open) as Autodesk SketchBook Motion for iPad (2016) — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=79772)
- Cartoon Animation Filter, Slow-in/out filter, Dragimation, DimP, Kitty, Motion Amplifiers, Tangent-space optimisation, DirectPaint, Squidgets: no code releases were found in this search (Autodesk's Motion Amplifiers and Draco are covered by US patents 10,403,020 / "Animating sketches via kinetic textures" 9,734,618 et al.) — [Google Patents US10403020](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10403020); [USPTO 9734618](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9734618)
- A small helper for drawing easing curves in PixiJS exists (`pixi-easing-graph`) — [GitHub](https://github.com/mimshwright/pixi-easing-graph)

### Inferences
- Patent coverage on the Autodesk sketch-animation line (kinetic textures, motion amplifiers, editable motion trajectories) is a factor to check before cloning those interactions verbatim in a commercial product; the underlying ideas (LoG filtering, time-warps, path-drag retiming, displacement curves) come from unencumbered academic papers from 1995–2012.
- The simplest web prototypes to build from scratch (no code available but algorithms are fully described in the abstracts/citations above): Dragimation-style path-drag retiming; cartoon-animation-filter "liveliness" slider; slow-in/slow-out time-warp; Hermite-fit keyframe reduction of recorded performances; K-Sketch-style event timeline. Gemini's recommender code can be read for the staging/cost-function pattern.
