## Key Question 1 — Native easing: Easy Ease family, Keyframe Velocity dialog, interpolation types and shortcuts

### Takeaway
Easy Ease (F9) is a fixed recipe — speed 0, influence 33.33% on both sides of every selected keyframe — with no preference to change the default; experts route around it by typing numbers into the Keyframe Velocity dialog (Alt/Option + double-click, or Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+K) and by converting interpolation type with Ctrl/Cmd-click or Ctrl+Alt+H. The folklore numbers that surfaced are "70% as a starting point", "75% for a snappy move", "50 in / 60 out", and "influence 0 at the bottom of a bounce"; the 75/25, 85/15 and 60/40 ratios named in the brief did not turn up in any source.

### Cited Findings
- Easy Ease keyframes are defined as "Incoming and Outcoming Velocity of Speed=0 and Influence=33.33", a curve described as "very close of a sinusoid" — [Creative COW thread](https://creativecow.net/?p=2412183) (attribution approximate; surfaced with [Adobe Community: Ease vs eased keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease-vs-eased-keyframes/td-p/7493370)).
- No preference exists to change the 33.33% default: a forum reply "found nothing relating to ease, keyframe, velocity, or any other parameter identified in the Keyframe Velocity options in the prefs file"; users wanting e.g. 60% must edit each keyframe or use a script — [Adobe Community: Changing the ease default values](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/changing-the-ease-default-values/m-p/10906650).
- Shortcuts: F9 = Easy Ease (both sides); Shift+F9 = Easy Ease In only; Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+F9 = Easy Ease Out only — [School of Motion: 30 essential keyboard shortcuts](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/30-essential-keyboard-shortcuts-in-after-effects); [Tuts+ shortcuts cheat sheet](https://photography.tutsplus.com/articles/after-effects-keyboard-shortcuts-cheat-sheet-for-animators--cms-109189).
- Keyframe Velocity dialog: open with Option/Alt + double-click on a keyframe outside the Graph Editor to "numerically dial in the ease amount"; other users report Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+K opens the same dialog — [Adobe Community: Changing the ease default values](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/changing-the-ease-default-values/m-p/10906650); [Creative COW: Easy ease adjusting softness](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/easy-ease-adjusting-softness/) (attribution approximate).
- Dialog semantics: incoming velocity = ease in, outgoing velocity = ease out; speed is the rate of change at the keyframe and 0 makes motion stop there; influence "determines the X position of the control point, from 0 to 100% of the distance between keys"; a "Continuous" checkbox locks incoming and outgoing to the same values — [Adobe Community: Keyframe velocity speed and influence question](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/keyframe-velocity-speed-and-influence-question/m-p/9624203/highlight/true); [VDCI: custom ease in the speed graph](https://vdci.edu/learn/after-effects/mastering-motion-animations); [Creative COW: Easy ease adjusting softness](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/easy-ease-adjusting-softness/).
- In the scripting model (KeyframeEase object) "keyframe ease is determined by the speed and influence values, and influence accepts values in the range 0.1 to 100" — [After Effects Scripting Guide: KeyframeEase object](https://after-effects-scripting-guide.readthedocs.io/other/keyframeease.html).
- Folklore values: one user prefers "a velocity of 50:60 on their keyframes, meaning 50% incoming and 60% outgoing influence" — [Adobe Community: Changing the ease default values](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/changing-the-ease-default-values/m-p/10906650) (attribution approximate). Another: "speed 0 with influence 75%, which starts slow, accelerates to a snappy move, and decelerates again" — [Creative COW: Easy ease adjusting softness](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/easy-ease-adjusting-softness/) (attribution approximate).
- An Adobe Community expert suggests "an influence value of about 70% as a starting point for a nice acceleration curve", recommends "setting influence to zero on keyframes at the bottom of a bounce path", and suggests "setting Keyframe Velocity numerically instead of dragging for bouncing motion" — [Adobe Community: Upside down curve in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/upside-down-curve-in-graph-editor/m-p/13940904).
- Auto Bezier "will give half the ease of Easy Ease" — [Adobe Community: Ease vs eased keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease-vs-eased-keyframes/td-p/7493370) (attribution approximate).
- Hold keyframes: "CTRL+ALT+H toggles a keyframe to/from a Hold Keyframe"; several keyframes can be selected and converted at once — [Adobe Community: Can't get composition to stop and hold](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/can-t-get-composition-to-stop-and-hold-in-after-effects/m-p/9777140).
- Interpolation toggle by modifier-click: sources disagree on whether it is Ctrl-click in the Timeline and Alt-click in the Graph Editor or the reverse; an Adobe employee states "Control/Command + clicking on a keyframe changes its interpolation to Continuous Bezier"; the shortcut "still worked in version 23.2.3" — [Adobe Community: How to revert an Easy Ease keyframe to a normal one](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-to-revert-an-easy-ease-keyframe-to-a-normal-one/m-p/14830322).
- Ctrl+Alt+K opens the Keyframe Interpolation dialog; Shift+F3 opens the Graph Editor — [ProVideo Coalition: After Effects tips installment 3](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after_effects_tips_installment_3/) (attribution approximate); [Evercast: 35 must-know shortcuts](https://www.evercast.us/blog/after-effects-shortcuts) (attribution approximate).
- A tutorial line repeated across guides: "linear keyframes are almost never useful in After Effects, so F9 is a common first step" — [Tuts+ shortcuts cheat sheet](https://photography.tutsplus.com/articles/after-effects-keyboard-shortcuts-cheat-sheet-for-animators--cms-109189) (attribution approximate).
- The `ease()` expression does not exactly match keyframe Easy Ease: "the ease expression uses an exact influence of 1/3 while the keyframe one uses 33.333333%", a difference that is tiny but visible when compared — [Adobe Community: Ease vs eased keyframes](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/ease-vs-eased-keyframes/td-p/7493370) (attribution approximate).
- A third-party plugin, "Under the Influence", is cited as applying influence values without opening the dialog — [VDCI: custom ease in the speed graph](https://vdci.edu/learn/after-effects/mastering-motion-animations) (attribution approximate).

### Inferences
- The entire native "typed easing" UX is one modal dialog with four numbers plus a Continuous checkbox; experts want (a) a changeable default, (b) asymmetric in/out presets, (c) non-modal numeric entry. A native equivalent should expose in/out influence (0–100) and speed as always-visible fields with a user-settable default and remembered last values.
- Since the shortcut cluster (F9 / Shift+F9 / Ctrl+Shift+F9 / Ctrl+Alt+H / Ctrl-click / Ctrl+Shift+K / Ctrl+Alt+K) is muscle memory for AE users, a new app should map the same keys by default.
- The specific folklore ratios in the brief (75/25, 85/15, 60/40) are not documented in any source found; what is documented is "higher than 33% and often asymmetric" (50/60, 70, 75). Treat exact ratios as taste, not canon.

### Gaps
- No source found for the 75/25, 85/15 or 60/40 "ease out heavier than ease in" rules; no source found recommending "100% influence for a hard stop".
- Could not fetch Adobe's own keyframe-interpolation and speed pages (helpx.adobe.com blocked), so exact Adobe wording for the dialog fields and the Keyframe Interpolation dialog options (Temporal/Spatial/Roving) is unverified.
- Mac equivalent of Ctrl+Alt+H not confirmed (one source suggests Cmd+Opt+H).

## Key Question 2 — The Graph Editor: value vs speed graph, handles, Separate Dimensions trap, roving keyframes, Smoother, Exponential Scale, presets, Properties panel

### Takeaway
The Graph Editor is where the real easing happens, but it has two different models (value vs speed), handles that behave differently in each, and a well-known trap: a merged Position property has no handles in the Value Graph until you Separate Dimensions, which in turn deletes the spatial motion-path handles. Roving keyframes, the Smoother and Exponential Scale are legacy assistants that solve narrow problems; Adobe's 2025–2026 releases added nothing to the Graph Editor.

### Cited Findings
- Two modes: "Value Graph – Controls how the property value changes over time (e.g., scale percentage or position coordinates)"; the Speed Graph shows how fast the change happens; workflow "Press F9 or right-click and choose Keyframe Assistant > Easy Ease", then open the Graph Editor and "drag the bezier handles to tweak how fast or slow the animation eases in or out. For smoother motion, try creating an 'S' shape in the speed graph"; recommends the Speed Graph for Position/Scale — [AEJuice: How to use graph editor](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-use-graph-editor-in-after-effects/); [Ledet: Easily smooth keyframes with the Graph Editor](https://www.ledet.com/after-effects-smooth-keyframes).
- To edit speed, click "Choose Graph Type and Options" and select "Edit Speed Graph"; "drag the handles up to increase the speed after a keyframe or down to decrease it", and left/right to change how quickly motion accelerates or decelerates — [AEJuice: How to use the speed graph](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-use-the-speed-graph-in-after-effects).
- Adobe's help on the speed graph: "To increase the influence of the keyframe, drag the direction handle away from the center of the keyframe. To decrease the influence, drag the direction handle toward the center"; vertical drag of the incoming handle raises/lowers speed; "If you want a handle to have influence over more than one keyframe, use roving keyframes" — [Adobe Help: Speed (speed between keyframes)](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/speed-between-keyframes/speed.html).
- Contrasting expert view: speed-graph handles "are not bezier handles. They control the entry and exit speed only"; to shape curves "you may have to change the time between keyframes and watch out for spikes in the speed" — [Adobe Community: Speed graph handle curves](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/speed-graph-handle-curves/td-p/13036613).
- For Position "the speed graph is almost always the best choice"; the value graph's handles can change the motion path but "it is extremely difficult to manipulate a path using the graph editor" — [Adobe Community: What's the math behind speed graphs](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/what-s-the-math-behind-speed-graphs/m-p/9597181) (attribution approximate).
- Dissent: "try to use the value graph and you will have much precise control … the speed graph is an anomaly in the industry" — [Creative COW thread](https://creativecow.net/?p=249135).
- Joey Korenman (School of Motion) says he usually works in the value graph "because the speed graph tells him less about the motion"; the intro lesson shows pulling an influence handle so "the motion takes a while to pick up speed and then shoots quickly"; the speed graph is called useful for fine-tuning time remaps — [School of Motion: Intro to the Graph Editor](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/tutorials/intro-to-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects/); [School of Motion: The MoGraph secret weapon](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/tutorials/graph-editor-after-effects).
- To switch graphs via the timeline: "Right click this button and click 'Edit Value Graph'" (forum reply signed "Jake") — [Adobe Community: How do I make my graph editor go from the first to the second picture](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-do-i-make-my-graph-editor-to-go-from-the-first-to-the-second-picture/td-p/11876649).
- Newcomers confuse the graphs: "The speed graph is the light gray line, while colored lines are the value graph" — [Adobe Community: After Effects graph editor problem](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effects-graph-editor-problem/m-p/10552533).
- Separate Dimensions trap: "merged coordinates like position don't have bezier handles, but you can separate dimensions on transform position to get access to them"; "the only way to see the handles in the Value Graph is separating dimensions. Otherwise, you have to select Speed Graph"; "separating dimensions removes the Bezier Handles from the spatial motion path in the Composition panel"; editing X separately "throws off the Y path and its timing"; "editing the speed graph for Position is almost always a better idea than trying to edit the value graph" — [Adobe Community: Need help with bezier handles](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/need-help-with-bezier-handles/m-p/13804367); [Adobe Community: Missing bezier handles](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/missing-bezier-handles/td-p/5171509); [Adobe Community: Where are my bezier curve handles in the graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/where-are-my-bezier-curve-handles-in-the-graph-editor/m-p/11939629/highlight/true).
- Anchor Point has no per-dimension value-graph handles (2021 thread); workaround is to link anchor point X/Y to a null's separated Position so the null's handles can be edited — [Adobe Community: Where are my bezier curve handles](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/where-are-my-bezier-curve-handles-in-the-graph-editor/m-p/11939629/highlight/true).
- Some users restore missing handles with the Pen tool's Convert Vertex tool, though "the handles then appear on both sides instead of only the one side you want" — [Adobe Community: Bezier handles in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/bezier-handles-in-graph-editor/m-p/8351886/highlight/true) (attribution approximate).
- Roving keyframes: select several keyframes, right-click one, choose "Rove Across Time"; first and last keyframes stay locked in time; the in-between keyframes "move in time as needed to produce smoother speed changes"; the Position path itself is unchanged — [ProVideo Coalition: Hidden Gems Weekly – Roving Keyframes](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after-effects-hidden-gems-weekly-roving-keyframes/).
- The Smoother: Window > Smoother; select keyframes; choose Spatial or Temporal (Position defaults to Spatial, Opacity-type properties to Temporal); intended for motion-sketched paths and tracking/stabilization keys ("steadicam-like feel") — [ProVideo Coalition: Creating Motion Graphics Hidden Gems, Chapter 5 – Animation Assistants](https://www.provideocoalition.com/creating_motion_graphics_with_after_effects_hidden_gems_chapter_5_-_animati/).
- Exponential Scale: Animation > Keyframe Assistant > Exponential Scale; "produces a series of keyframes along a sloping exponential curve that imitates a zoom lens"; a user reported a sudden speed jump after re-applying it, and the suggested workaround was a 3D camera move instead — [ProVideo Coalition: Hidden Gems Chapter 5](https://www.provideocoalition.com/creating_motion_graphics_with_after_effects_hidden_gems_chapter_5_-_animati/); [Adobe Community: After Effect temporal interpolation](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/after-effect-temporal-interpolation/td-p/12997789).
- Animation presets: "you can save and reuse specific configurations of layer properties and animations, including keyframes, effects, and expressions"; saved via Animation > Save Animation Preset as an .ffx file in the Presets folder; applied by dragging from Effects & Presets, which "creates any necessary keyframes in that layer" — [Adobe Help: Effects and animation presets overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/apply-effects-and-animation-presets/effects-and-animation-presets/effects-animation-presets-overview.html). A user noted presets "only work across two key frames" for easing reuse, so each pair must be saved as its own preset — [School of Motion: Flow for After Effects review](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/training/flow-for-after-effects-review) (attribution approximate).
- Properties panel: a contextual panel showing the selected layer's controls; introduced to After Effects Beta in late 2021 (build 22.2.0x29) "to shorten the time navigating through the UI to reach a particular property"; properties can be located in the Timeline by double-click or "Reveal in Timeline" — [Adobe Help: Properties panel](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/properties-panel.html); [Adobe Community Beta: Introducing the Properties panel](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-beta-discussions/introducing-the-properties-panel-in-after-effects-beta/m-p/12590934/highlight/true).
- 2026 releases: version 26.5 (September 2026) added percentage-based guides, Object Matte disk caching, effect color labels, an updated Effect Controls panel and ACES 2.0; version 26.3 (June 2026) added Advanced 3D depth of field, SVG/Illustrator paste as shape layers, a faster mask tracker and Curl Noise — no Graph Editor or easing changes were listed — [Adobe Help: What's new in After Effects](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/after-effects/desktop/what-s-new/whats-new.html).
- A 2023 Beta regression: "speed graph editor speed curve no longer responds to easing changes" — [Adobe Community: AE Beta 2023.0.0 speed graph bug](https://community.adobe.com/questions-534/ae-beta-2023-0-0-speed-graph-editor-speed-curve-no-longer-responds-to-easing-changes-314154).
- An April 2026 forum post calls the AE graph editor "cramped and hard to work with" and says Flow is the kind of tool the poster wishes Adobe would match — [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/adding-frills-to-adobe-premiere-foundation-1625753) (attribution approximate; result surfaced under a Premiere-titled thread).
- A 2024 Reddit thread on favourite plugins: one commenter named Flow and Sapphire; others described AE's UX as rough and called extensions "workarounds for missing features"; the same thread reports ease-copy "glitches in some builds" — [r/AfterEffects, Oct 2024 (mirror)](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/AfterEffects/comments/1g73qs3/which_plugin_would_it_be).

### Inferences
- The value/speed split is the single largest source of confusion; experts pick one per property type (speed for Position, value for scalars) or pick one globally (Korenman: value). A new app can avoid the trap by showing a value graph with true Bezier handles for every channel (including merged X/Y Position) and offering a speed overlay as a read-only reference, which removes the need for Separate Dimensions as a prerequisite.
- Roving keyframes exist because AE ties spatial path smoothness to keyframe timing; a native "even out speed along path" toggle with first/last locked is the obvious equivalent.
- The Smoother and Exponential Scale are rarely cited in modern tutorials; a native "simplify/smooth keys" command and an exponential/zoom-lens scale option cover them.
- Because animation presets bundle values with eases and are awkward for multi-key eases, the market filled the gap with ease-only preset libraries (Flow, Easify, Kease, Dojo Ease).

### Gaps
- Could not verify Adobe documentation for "Auto-Select Graph Type", "Show Reference Graph", "Fit All Graphs to View"/"Fit Selection", snapping, box-selecting handles, the transform box for multiple keyframes, Shift-constrain or Alt-break-tangent behaviours (search budget exhausted before these queries ran; helpx blocked).
- No source found describing easing controls or keyframe navigation inside the 2024–2026 Properties panel; Adobe's 2025 release notes were not retrieved.
- No source found for the "Ctrl+Alt+V" paste trick or any native "paste ease only" command.

## Key Question 3 — Expressions people paste instead of keyframing: ease()/linear(), inertial bounce/overshoot (Ebberts), Penner, baking

### Takeaway
`linear()`/`ease()`/`easeIn()`/`easeOut()` are five-argument remap functions (driver, tMin, tMax, value1, value2) with a fixed sinusoid-like ease; the Dan Ebberts inertial-bounce family reads keyframe velocity and adds a decaying sine so two keyframes give an overshoot, which AE has no native tool for. Expression easing keeps keyframes editable and is parametric, but has no handles, no graph-editor view, and baking produces one keyframe per frame.

### Cited Findings
- `linear(t, tMin, tMax, value1, value2)`: below tMin holds value1, above tMax holds value2, straight-line in between; `ease(t, tMin, tMax, value1, value2)` same arguments but "the interpolation eases in and out so that the velocity is 0 at the start and end points"; `easeIn` is "similar to ease, except that the tangent is 0 only on the tMin side and interpolation is linear on the tMax side"; short forms `linear(t, value1, value2)` / `ease(t, value1, value2)` assume tMin=0, tMax=1 — [AE Expressions Guide: Interpolation](https://after-effects-expressions-guide.readthedocs.io/interpolation.html); [docsforadobe expressions: Interpolation](https://ae-expressions.docsforadobe.dev/general/interpolation/); [Adobe/JJ Gifford expressions interpolation PDF](https://www.adobe.com/go/learn_ae_jjgiffordexpressionsinterpolation).
- Worked examples: `linear(time, 0, 6, 20, 80)` ramps opacity 20→80 over six seconds; `x = ease(time, 0, 2, 500, 700)` eases X from 500 to 700 between 0 s and 2 s — [Creative COW: What does Linear() do?](https://creativecow.net/?p=1836190); [Creative COW thread](https://creativecow.net/?p=1869308) (attribution approximate).
- Penner easing functions can be loaded from a .jsx and called from expressions; they "work slightly differently to the default ease, since Penner's easing only asks for 3 (beginning value, change in value, duration)" — [dev.to: Penner easing functions in After Effects](https://dev.to/kocreative/penner-easing-functions-in-after-effects-1bok); [Adobe Community: Using Robert Penner's easing](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/using-robert-penner-s-easing/m-p/9583301/highlight/true).
- Inertial bounce (Ebberts): "applies a damped sine wave to a property's velocity after the last keyframe, so the layer overshoots its target and settles"; it plugs amplitude, frequency and decay into a sine function; "the bounce expression is great because it only takes two keyframes to create a bounce"; applied by Alt-clicking the stopwatch — [School of Motion: After Effects bounce expression](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/after-effects-bounce-expression).
- Long-comp failure and fix: Ebberts explained "After a couple of minutes or so, Math.exp(decay*t) will become such a huge number that JavaScript represents it as 'Infinity'"; with decay 5 it overflows at about t = 142 s; his fix limits the calculation to roughly the first 10 seconds after the keyframe — [Creative COW thread](https://creativecow.net/?p=1859347); [Adobe Community: Error in line 11 inertial bounce](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/error-in-line-11-ae-inertial-bounce-created-by-dan-ebberts-at-motionscript-com/m-p/9289049).
- Pairing tip: add Easy Ease Out to the last keyframe because "specifying Easy Ease Out will slow the animation down towards the end, which works well with the inertia expression" — [Adobe Community: Inertial bounce expression](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/inertial-bounce-expression-30984).
- Child-layer delay variant reads the parent's keyframes (community adaptation, not an Ebberts release) — [Adobe Community: How can I add delay to the inertial bounce expression](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-can-i-add-delay-to-the-inertial-bounce-expression-help/m-p/13036827).
- "After Effects does not have native Overshoot or Bounce tools"; manual overshoot is an extra keyframe past the target (e.g. scale to 150 then settle at 100), or adding keys and shaping the outgoing curve in the graph editor — [Mt. Mograph blog: The bounce and overshoot trick](https://mtmograph.com/blogs/tools/the-bounce-and-overshoot-animation-trick-every-motion-designer-should-know); [Motion Array: Realistic bouncing effect](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/how-to-create-a-realistic-bouncing-effect-in-after-effects/).
- EZ Inertia is a free script that adds bounce expressions with Amplitude, Frequency and Decay: "Amplitude affects the amount of overshoot and bounce back. Higher amplitude means greater amounts of bounce" — [Lester Banks](https://lesterbanks.com/?p=34843) (attribution approximate).
- Baking: the native method "bakes the values every single frame – resulting in a vast quantity of keyframes, even when the layer in question didn't move"; third-party bakers (Smart Baker, Easy Bake, AEJuice Bake Expressions with an "Unbake" button) prune redundant keys — [Lester Banks: Smart Baker](https://lesterbanks.com/2011/05/smart-baker-for-after-effects/); [aescripts forum: Easy Bake](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/1265/easy-bake); [Novedge: AEJuice Bake Expressions](https://novedge.com/products/buy-aejuice-bake-expressions).
- Where expression easing breaks: a Flow-applied ease "was lost once they added their own expression" and the user had to fall back to a custom Bezier approach — [Adobe Community: Custom ease / easing](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/custom-ease-easing/m-p/14601539). Ease and Wizz's developer advises that for finer control "nothing beats the graph editor for custom motion" — [aescripts forum: Ease and Wizz](https://aescripts.com/forums/discussion/14/ease-and-wizz).
- Expression easing's advantage: because easing is computed rather than baked, keyframes stay editable and "the easing will be updated immediately" when keys move — [aescripts: Ease and Wizz](https://aescripts.com/ease-and-wizz).
- Remap caution from an Adobe Community mentor: `linear()` is reliable for range remaps (e.g. 0–100 → 51–67) while `ease()` "can give results you did not intend, so try linear first" — [Adobe Community: Need help configuring expression with adjustable threshold](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/need-help-configuring-expression-with-adjustable-threshold/m-p/11035287/highlight/true).

### Inferences
- Overshoot/bounce is the clearest "expression instead of feature" case: users paste a 10-line Ebberts snippet (or buy After Ease/Excite) because AE has no overshoot keyframe type. A native "overshoot/elastic/bounce" ease family with amplitude, frequency and decay sliders, applied to a keyframe pair, is the direct equivalent.
- Expression easing fails on discoverability and editability (no handles, no graph, only numbers in code, fragile when combined with other expressions). A native app that treats Penner-style curves as first-class keyframe interpolation (with handles shown) removes the trade-off entirely.
- Baking is lossy and bloated in AE; a native equivalent should keep parametric eases parametric and only bake on export.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Dan Ebberts' original overshoot/bounce code text from motionscript.com (blocked); only descriptions and the Infinity caveat are sourced.
- `easeOut()` documentation text and the `smooth()` expression were not retrieved (budget exhausted).
- Adobe's own description of "Convert Expression to Keyframes" (one key per frame) was not retrieved; the every-frame behaviour is sourced only from a third-party baker's description.

## Key Question 4 — Scripts and plugins: Flow, EaseCopy, Keyframe Wingman, Ease and Wizz, Dojo Ease, Motion Tools Pro, Mt. Mograph Motion/Motion Studio, Easify, After Ease, Kease, KBar and others

### Takeaway
The market splits into four jobs: (1) cubic-bezier curve editors with preset libraries (Flow, Easify 2, Kease), (2) slider/numeric influence appliers (Mt. Mograph Motion Ease Sliders/Influence, Dojo Ease, Motion Tools Pro Ease Keyframes, MDS Easy Keyframes, Keyframe Wingman, Accurate Ease Keys), (3) ease-only copy/paste (EaseCopy, Keyframe Actions, Motion Tools Pro, Easify, SpotlightFX Copy Easing), and (4) overshoot/bounce generators (Ease and Wizz, After Ease, Excite, EZ Inertia). Prices range from free/name-your-own-price to $59.99 perpetual and $11–13/month subscription.

### Inferences

- Recurring plugin complaints (panel too small, handle lost outside panel, expression vs keyframe mode confusion, first-key influence not editable, lag per click, breakage on AE updates) are exactly the costs a built-in feature avoids.

## Key Question 5 — Workflow hacks recommended in tutorials and forums

### Takeaway
The documented expert workflow is: set keys → F9 → open the Graph Editor (speed graph for Position, value graph for scalars, or value graph everywhere per Korenman) → pull influence handles toward an S-curve or type numbers in Keyframe Velocity → use Rove Across Time on long paths → hold keyframes for pops → reach for a script when copying an ease or needing overshoot. Several tutorial sites and educators named in the brief could not be reached, so attribution to specific educators is thin.

### Cited Findings
- "Start with Easy Ease" then refine: select keys, right-click > Easy Ease, open the Graph Editor and drag handles; aim for an S shape; use the Speed Graph for motion properties like Position or Scale; "steep curves create sharp speed changes, while gradual hills produce smooth acceleration patterns"; preview as you adjust — [Ledet: Easily smooth keyframes](https://www.ledet.com/after-effects-smooth-keyframes); [AEJuice: How to use the speed graph](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-use-the-speed-graph-in-after-effects).
- Set the graph type deliberately: "click the 'Choose Graph Type and Options' button and select 'Edit Speed Graph.' This ensures you are editing the speed of motion, not the value of the property" — [AEJuice: How to use the speed graph](https://aejuice.com/blog/how-to-use-the-speed-graph-in-after-effects).
- Speed graph for position, value graph when editing values: "The graph editor is all about timing, and the speed graph is for adjusting acceleration between values … Edit the value graph to adjust the values"; "If you want to separate out X, Y and Z values, you need to separate the position values before you start"; verify with a RAM preview — [Adobe Community: Is the speed graph supposed to look like this](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/is-the-speed-graph-supposed-to-look-like-this/m-p/13084013) (attribution approximate).
- Korenman (School of Motion) works mainly in the value graph; the speed graph is reserved for time-remap fine-tuning — [School of Motion: Intro to the Graph Editor](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/intro-to-the-graph-editor-in-after-effects); [School of Motion: Graph editor article](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/graph-editor-after-effects).
- Ben Marriott says he "stopped using plugins that helped with easing so he could focus on really getting comfortable in the graph editor" and credits that period with the biggest jump in his animation skill — [VoiceTube transcript of a Ben Marriott video](https://jp.voicetube.com/videos/123011) (third-party transcript).
- Bounce via keyframes: set Keyframe Velocity numerically, influence 0 at the bottom of each bounce, ~70% elsewhere — [Adobe Community: Upside down curve in graph editor](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/upside-down-curve-in-graph-editor/m-p/13940904).
- "Check 'Continuous' at first so the incoming and outgoing are the same" when using the Keyframe Velocity dialog — [Creative COW: Easy ease adjusting softness](https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/easy-ease-adjusting-softness/).
- Roving keyframes to even out speed along long paths: "keyframe timings are freed and can shift earlier or later to even out speed without altering the Position path itself" — [ProVideo Coalition: Roving Keyframes](https://www.provideocoalition.com/after-effects-hidden-gems-weekly-roving-keyframes/).
- Hold keyframes for stops/pops: a hold keyframe "says 'don't interpolate,' so the keyframe holds its value until the next keyframe is encountered"; Ctrl+Alt+H — [Adobe Community: Can't get composition to stop and hold](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/can-t-get-composition-to-stop-and-hold-in-after-effects/m-p/9777140).
- Pair Easy Ease Out on the last keyframe with the inertial bounce expression — [Adobe Community: Inertial bounce expression](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/inertial-bounce-expression-30984).
- Paste cubic-bezier.com curves into Flow for web-matched easing — [Toolfarm: Flow v1.5.x](https://www.toolfarm.com/?p=22477).
- Put easing scripts on KBar buttons (EaseCopy: click copy / Alt-click paste ease / Shift-click paste value) to save screen space — [aescripts learn: EaseCopy plus KBar](https://aescripts.com/learn/easecopy-plus-kbar).
- Motion Array "Create better eases" and "How to easily create better animations" tutorials exist on the same workflow (contents not retrieved) — [Motion Array: Create better eases](https://motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/create-better-eases-in-after-effects/); [Motion Array: Better animations](https://motionarray.com/tutorials/after-effects-tutorials/how-to/how-to-easily-create-better-animations-in-adobe-after-effects).

### Inferences
- Every hack is a step the user repeats on every pair of keyframes; the native feature should collapse "F9 → open graph → choose graph type → drag handles → check numbers" into one in-place control on the keyframe with the curve visible.
- The "speed for Position, value for everything else" rule is a workaround for AE's lack of handles on merged Position; it disappears if the value graph supports multi-dimensional properties natively.

## Key Question 6 — Known pain points that motivate plugins

### Takeaway
The pain points are consistent across forums and product pages: no numeric cubic-bezier input, no ease-only copy/paste, no preset library, a cramped and modal graph editor whose two modes confuse users, missing handles on merged/anchor properties, no changeable default, and no overshoot without expressions. Plugins fix these but add their own costs (lag, breakage on AE updates, mode confusion, tiny panels).

### Cited Findings
- No changeable ease default; nothing in the prefs file — [Adobe Community: Changing the ease default values](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/changing-the-ease-default-values/m-p/10906650).

### Inferences
- Each pain point maps to one native feature: (1) numeric cubic-bezier / influence fields on the keyframe; (2) Paste Ease Only with EaseCopy's matching/scaling/flip rules; (3) an ease preset library with import/export and team sharing; (4) a value graph with handles on every channel including merged Position and Anchor Point, no Separate Dimensions prerequisite; (5) a user-settable default ease; (6) overshoot/elastic/bounce as first-class interpolation types; (7) in-context (non-modal, non-panel) controls so nothing is lost "outside the panel" and nothing lags per click; (8) stable across app updates because it is built in.

## Key Question 7 — Native-feature implications for a new motion-graphics app (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Everything AE users do with shortcuts, dialogs, expressions and plugins reduces to about nine built-in behaviours: a two-slider in/out ease control with numeric cubic-bezier and influence/speed fields; a user-settable default ease; preset libraries with sharing; ease-only copy/paste; an always-on value graph with handles on all channels; a read-only speed reference; roving (even-speed) toggle; hold keys and interpolation cycling on the same keys AE uses; and parametric overshoot/elastic/bounce interpolation with amplitude, frequency and decay.

### Cited Findings
- (This section synthesises findings already cited in Questions 1–6; see those sections for sources.)

### Inferences
- Controls: per-keyframe in/out influence sliders 0–100 with mirrored centre drag and Shift/Ctrl/Alt snapping (pattern shared by MDS Easy Keyframes, Mt. Mograph Ease Sliders, Dojo Ease); numeric fields accepting `cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2)` text (Flow); speed fields and a "Continuous/linked" toggle (Keyframe Velocity dialog).
- Defaults: a settable default ease (requested on Adobe forums for years, unavailable natively) and asymmetric defaults (50/60, 70, 75 are the folklore anchors found).
- Library: ~25 Penner presets out of the box (Flow's baseline), unlimited user presets (Easify), multi-key presets for anticipation/overshoot/bounce (Kease), import/export/share (Flow), applied to selected keys or key pairs in one click (Keyframe Wingman's "one-click easing").
- Copy/paste: "Paste Ease" that requires matching key counts or multiples, scales to the target's value delta, flips for reversed direction, with a pass-through option for 3+ key strips (EaseCopy).
- Graph: value graph with Bezier handles on every channel including merged X/Y Position and Anchor Point (removing the Separate Dimensions trap), plus a speed/velocity reference overlay; box-select and group-edit of handles.
- Timing tools: rove-across-time toggle locking first/last keys; hold interpolation on Ctrl+Alt+H; interpolation cycling on Ctrl-click; F9 / Shift+F9 / Ctrl+Shift+F9 as default bindings.
- Overshoot: elastic/bounce/overshoot as keyframe interpolation types with amplitude, frequency and decay (friction) sliders and an enable toggle (Excite/After Ease/EZ Inertia parameter set), kept parametric rather than baked.
- Expression parity: expose `linear()/ease()/easeIn()/easeOut()` equivalents for scripted remaps but never require them for ordinary easing.
